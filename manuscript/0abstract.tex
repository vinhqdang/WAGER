Vision benchmarks with strong annotation priors can reward a new model whose gain does
not come from better instance-specific visual prediction. Existing audits compare a model
against a frequency baseline, confounding how well each system fits the prior with
whether the \emph{difference} between them uses information beyond it. We introduce
WAGER, a pairwise attribution algorithm. For two frozen probabilistic models it forms
their proper-score contrast, matches examples sharing a declared prior feature and
crosses their labels, giving an exact finite-sample identity: observed gain equals a
prior-transported gain plus an antisymmetric instance-alignment gain. The residual is an
order-two U-statistic which, under the quadratic score, equals twice the within-cell
prediction--label covariance gained by the new model, linking it to the classical
resolution term of proper-score decomposition. It costs $O(NK)$ time, needs no fitted
prior model, and admits cluster-robust inference. Two further exact results follow:
leave-one-out transport removes an attenuation bias of $(n_c-1)/n_c$ that a naive
in-sample plug-in incurs, and coarsening the audit cell shifts the estimate by a
between-cell covariance term. On 229,605 Visual Genome relations a class-only network
improves the frequency baseline entirely through the prior channel, while a frozen-CLIP
model scoring $0.04655$ \emph{worse} overall has significantly \emph{better} instance
alignment ($+0.00641$, $p=.002$) than the box-geometry model it loses to -- an advantage
that survives both post-hoc prior matching and calibration matching. Applied unchanged
to long-tailed classification, it exposes the composition of two re-weighting schedules'
gains that aggregate scores leave hidden: a near-zero total that decomposes into two
large cancelling channels, which a calibration-matched reanalysis traces to a genuine
loss of within-class discrimination, and a real improvement that the same reanalysis
shows to be almost entirely instance alignment rather than prior refitting.
