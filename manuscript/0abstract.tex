Two probabilistic classifiers are compared on a test set and the newer one scores higher.
When the labels depend partly on a grouping variable both models observe --- the
object-class pair in a relation-prediction benchmark, the frequency tier in a long-tailed
one --- that difference mixes two improvements: the new model may fit the label
frequencies \emph{within} groups more closely, or it may match its predictions to the
individual cases they were made for. An aggregate difference cannot say which, and
testing it for significance does not separate them. We give an exact decomposition into
those two parts. The construction is elementary: score each model's prediction for one
test case against the label of a \emph{different} case in the same group. Averaging over
such within-group relabellings gives the \emph{transported gain}, the part that survives
relabelling; what remains is a \emph{within-group covariance gain}. The identity is exact
in finite samples, needs no fitted baseline model, no sample splitting and no tuning
parameter, and costs $O(NK)$ for $N$ cases and $K$ labels. The remainder is a U-statistic
of order two equal to the within-group covariance between the two models'
predicted-probability difference and the label indicator. That covariance is the quantity
a covariance rearrangement of a proper score isolates \citep{yates1982external}; it
equals twice a difference of classical resolution terms when both models are calibrated
within groups, but not in general, because a covariance between forecast values --- unlike
a rank-based measure --- is not invariant to recalibration. We show the covariance
identity holds for every Bregman score, prove asymptotic normality with a consistent
cluster-robust variance, give a sensitivity bound for an unrecorded grouping variable
together with the correlation at which it would overturn our own estimates, and show that
using a group's own label frequency as the counterfactual attenuates the estimate by
exactly $(n_c-1)/n_c$. Simulations establish interval coverage and delimit what the two
parts separate, including a recalibration sensitivity large enough that we audit
confidence-matched pairs. On two released scene-graph checkpoints, matched for confidence
and grouped by object-class pair, essentially the entire proper-score difference is
transported gain under either of two proper scores; the estimate depends on that
configuration, and we report the alternatives rather than one of them.
