Vision benchmarks with strong annotation priors can reward a new model whose gain does
not come from better instance-specific visual prediction. Existing audits
compare a model against a frequency baseline, which confounds how well each system fits
the prior with whether the \emph{difference} between them uses information beyond it. We
introduce WAGER, a pairwise attribution algorithm. For two frozen probabilistic models it
forms their proper-score contrast, matches examples sharing a declared prior feature, and
crosses their labels, yielding an exact finite-sample identity: observed gain equals a
prior-transported gain plus an antisymmetric instance-alignment gain. The residual is an
order-two U-statistic which, under the quadratic score, equals twice the within-cell
prediction--label covariance gained by the new model, linking it to the classical
resolution term of proper-score decomposition. It costs $O(NK)$ time, needs no fitted
prior model, and admits cluster-robust inference and a randomization test. Two further
exact results follow: leave-one-out transport removes an attenuation bias of
$(n_c-1)/n_c$ that a naive in-sample plug-in incurs, and coarsening the audit cell shifts
the estimate by an explicit between-cell covariance term. On 229,605 Visual Genome
relations, a class-only network improves the frequency baseline entirely through the
prior channel, whereas box geometry adds an alignment gain of $0.01439$ (95\% CI
$[0.01373,0.01504]$). Applied unchanged to long-tailed classification, WAGER separates
two re-weighting schedules that aggregate scores cannot: one whose near-zero total gain
conceals a $+0.19713$ prior gain cancelling a $-0.19569$ alignment loss, and one whose
genuine improvement is two-thirds prior-recoverable and one-third instance alignment,
concentrated on rare classes.
