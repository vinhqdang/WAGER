\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate three questions: (i) does WAGER remain centered under the null and respond
monotonically to known instance signal; (ii) does it distinguish class-prior fitting
from spatial evidence on a real long-tailed benchmark; and (iii) are conclusions stable
to score, cell granularity, and sparse-cell filtering? All confidence intervals are 95\%.

\subsection{Controlled validation}\label{sec:simulation}

We generate 24 prior cells with five labels and draw each cell's label distribution from
a Dirichlet law. The old model is exactly the cell prior. The new model is
$q_1=(1-\beta)q_0+\beta q_{\rm oracle}$, where $q_{\rm oracle}$ assigns $0.98$ to the
realized label. Forty repetitions are run at each $\beta\in\{0,.1,\ldots,.8\}$. The
estimated alignment gain grows linearly and monotonically with the injected signal
(Figure~\ref{fig:simulation}, left).

For type-I evaluation, we add mean-zero perturbations to prior predictions and randomly
reassign the perturbed prediction vectors within each cell, ensuring no systematic
prediction--label alignment. Across 200 datasets, the 99-shift randomization test rejects
in $0.030$ of runs at level $0.05$; the mean estimated alignment gain is
$-8.97\times10^{-5}$. The null $p$-values are approximately uniform and null estimates
remain centered at zero (Figure~\ref{fig:simulation}, center and right).

\subsection{Visual Genome setup}\label{sec:data}

We use Visual Genome \cite{krishna2017visual} in the standard VG150 PredCls regime:
ground-truth boxes and object labels are supplied, and the model predicts one of 50
predicate labels for each ordered object pair. The training/test split contains
532,987/229,605 relations from 65,228/27,955 images. The prior cell is
$\phi=(\text{subject class},\text{object class})$. Of 8,614 test cells, 6,346 contain
at least two relations. WAGER identifies 227,337 test relations (99.0\%); the 2,268
singleton cells are reported and excluded.

Five deliberately controlled predictors expose different information. FREQ is the
training-count distribution conditioned on the class pair. FREQ+OVERLAP additionally
uses a binary box-overlap indicator. MLP-CLASS consumes fixed subject/object class
embeddings and is therefore a function of $\phi$ alone. MLP-SPATIAL adds 12 normalized
box-geometry features (relative center, scale, aspect, intersection, containment, and
IoU); MLP-SPATIAL+ increases its capacity. All models are trained once on the training
split. WAGER consumes only their cached test distributions. We use the quadratic score,
image-clustered intervals, and 499 within-cell cyclic randomizations.

\subsection{Direct gain attribution}\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:wager-results} and Figure~\ref{fig:results} give the central result.
MLP-CLASS improves the quadratic score over FREQ by $0.03386$, but its gain vector is
constant inside every class-pair cell. WAGER therefore assigns the entire improvement
to prior transport and exactly zero to instance alignment. This is an algebraic
consequence of the estimator, not a learned baseline passing a sanity check.

MLP-SPATIAL versus FREQ gains $0.04270$ in total: $0.02832$ is retained after label
transport and $0.01439$ is instance alignment (CI $[0.01373,0.01504]$). The direct
comparison MLP-SPATIAL versus MLP-CLASS is more revealing. Its total gain is only
$0.00885$, because prior fitting deteriorates by $0.00554$, while spatial alignment
improves by $0.01439$. Thus the alignment share is 1.63: the useful instance-specific
change is partially hidden by a worse prior component. A ratio constrained to $[0,1]$
or thresholded at $0.5$ would misdescribe this tradeoff.

MLP-SPATIAL+ adds $0.00374$ over MLP-SPATIAL, of which $0.00321$ is alignment (CI
$[0.00289,0.00353]$). FREQ+OVERLAP illustrates the opposite caution: overlap creates a
small positive alignment term, but its negative prior term is larger, so the model is
worse overall. WAGER does not label any use of image-derived information as an
improvement; it reports both channels and their sign.

\subsection{Sensitivity analyses}\label{sec:ablations}

For MLP-SPATIAL versus MLP-CLASS, the conclusion survives every planned sensitivity
check (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}). Changing from the quadratic to a probability-floored
log score changes scale ($0.01439$ to $0.06536$) but not sign or significance.
Coarsening $\phi$ from the class pair to subject class increases the credited alignment
to $0.02181$. Proposition~\ref{prop:coarsen} identifies why: the coarser cell's estimate
equals the class-pair estimate's conditional average plus a between-class-pair covariance
term, and that term is positive here, meaning some of the credited gain reflects
class-pair-level structure that the finer audit had already correctly assigned to
$\Delta P$. This is precisely the sense in which the class-pair audit is the conservative
choice: it is not that coarsening is wrong, but that it cannot be assumed neutral, and
only the finer partition's estimate isolates alignment gain net of every prior regularity
expressible in $\phi$. Raising the minimum cell size from 2 to 5, 10, and 20 yields
$0.01395$, $0.01330$, and $0.01225$; all cluster-robust lower limits remain positive.
Unlike the rejected projection-based formulation, there is no smoothing strength, split
fraction, clipping constant, betting order, or gain-ratio decision threshold to tune.

\subsection{Real-pixel signal on Visual Genome}\label{sec:vgvisual}

Every model in Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:ablations} is a function of the
declared class pair and, for MLP-SPATIAL(+), of box geometry derived from the
annotation, not of the image itself. This leaves open whether WAGER's alignment
channel behaves sensibly when a model consumes genuine pixel content rather than a
geometric proxy for it. We add MLP-VISUAL: subject and object boxes are cropped from
the original Visual Genome image, encoded with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 image encoder
\citep{radford2021learning}, and the two 512-dimensional crop embeddings are
concatenated with the same class embeddings used by MLP-CLASS before a small MLP head
(256, 128 hidden units) is trained to predict the predicate. CLIP is not fine-tuned; the
only trained parameters are the MLP head, so any alignment gain reflects information
CLIP's pretraining already extracted from pixels, matched to the SGG label by the MLP,
not new representation learning on VG itself. Feature extraction uses the full test
split ($229{,}605$ relations) and a fixed, seeded subsample of $150{,}000$ of the
$532{,}987$ training relations (capped for tractability; Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}
reports the exact sampling procedure and the count of images with a missing or
unreadable file).

\subsection{Cross-domain validation: long-tailed image classification}\label{sec:cifarlt}

Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:vgvisual} are all instances of one task (predicate
classification on an ordered object pair). Section~\ref{sec:related-longtail} argued
that long-tailed image classification poses a structurally identical question: a
class-frequency prior can be fit better without any per-instance improvement. We test
this directly on CIFAR-100-LT \citep{cui2019classbalanced}, an exponential-imbalance
(ratio $100$) subsample of CIFAR-100's training split with the original, balanced test
split retained for evaluation. We train two real classifiers with no other differences:
a ResNet-32 \citep{he2016deep} baseline with vanilla cross-entropy, and the same
architecture trained with the class-balanced (effective-number) re-weighting of
\citet{cui2019classbalanced}. Both are trained from random initialization for the same
number of epochs with the same optimizer, schedule, and data augmentation (Appendix
\ref{app:implementation} lists all hyperparameters); the only difference is the per-class loss
weight. The declared prior feature $\phi$ is the class label itself, so every test cell
has exactly $100$ examples by construction (CIFAR-100's balanced test split); we also
report a coarsened $\phi$ that groups classes into head/mid/tail training-frequency
tiers, mirroring the coarsening analysis of Section~\ref{sec:coarsening} in a second
domain. Because CIFAR-100 test images are independent (unlike VG's multiple relations
per image), no image-cluster grouping is needed and the plain H\'ajek interval of
Section~\ref{sec:inference} applies directly.
