\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

We first describe the corpus (Section~\ref{sec:data}), validate WAGER on a simulation
suite where ground truth is known (Section~\ref{sec:phase1}), apply it to a real
Visual~Genome scene-graph study (Sections~\ref{sec:sgg}--\ref{sec:strat}), and finally
probe sensitivity to design choices (Section~\ref{sec:abl}). All reported intervals are
at $\alpha=0.05$ unless stated.

\subsection{Dataset and statistics}\label{sec:data}
Our study uses the Visual~Genome scene-graph corpus \cite{krishna2017visual} in the
PredCls regime (ground-truth boxes and object labels given, predicate to be
predicted), with the standard VG150 vocabulary of $150$ object classes and $50$
predicates. Table~\ref{tab:data} summarizes the corpus. Two properties make it an
ideal stress test for WAGER. First, it is \emph{heavy-tailed}: the five most frequent
predicates account for $71.6\%$ of all relation instances and the single predicate
``on'' for $32.4\%$ (Figure~\ref{fig:longtail}), so a frequency lookup already secures
most of any recall-style metric. Second, the prior-feature space is \emph{sparse}:
only $49.2\%$ of the $150{\times}150$ possible ordered class pairs are observed in
training, and $46.4\%$ of the observed cells contain fewer than five instances---which
is exactly why a naive cell mean fails and the hierarchical backoff of
Section~\ref{sec:method} is required.

\subsection{Prior features and benchmark generality}\label{sec:phi}
WAGER is instantiated through the prior feature $\phi$, the statistic through which
annotation frequency acts (Table~\ref{tab:phi}). Our empirical study targets
Visual~Genome PredCls, where $\phi$ is the ordered class pair; the same machinery
applies to VQA/GQA (question type) and LVIS (frequency tier) without modification,
which is the sense in which WAGER is benchmark-agnostic.

\subsection{Simulation validity and power}\label{sec:phase1}
Before touching real models we verify the framework's guarantees on synthetic data
with known ground truth, by mixing a prior forecaster with an oracle,
$q_f=(1-\beta)\,\text{prior}+\beta\,\text{oracle}$, so the true reasoning fraction is
controlled by $\beta$. Figure~\ref{fig:phase1} summarizes three gates, all of which
pass:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Type-I error.} Feeding $q_f=\bar q_f$ (model $=$ prior) over $600$
    runs, the empirical exceedance rate is $0.017\le\alpha=0.05$, and
    $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}\approx 0.004$, confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:evalue}.
    \item \textbf{Coverage.} The anytime-valid intervals attain $0.93$ empirical
    coverage against a $0.90$ target.
    \item \textbf{Recovery.} The estimated RGR tracks the injected ground-truth RGR
    with Pearson $r=0.998$ and is monotone in $\beta$; $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}$
    tracks the true reasoning information with $r=0.999$.
\end{itemize}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig7_phase1.png}
\caption{\textbf{Phase-1 validation.} (a) Estimated vs.\ ground-truth RGR
($r=0.998$). (b) Monotone recovery of injected reasoning information. (c) Type-I error
and one-minus-coverage stay within their nominal levels.}

\subsection{Real study: models and the anchor}\label{sec:sgg}
We build five predicate predictors of increasing access to image information beyond the
class pair: \textbf{FREQ} (training-count lookup
$P(\text{predicate}\mid\text{subject},\text{object})$, constant within a $\phi$-cell);
\textbf{FREQ+OVERLAP} (FREQ plus a binary box-overlap indicator);
\textbf{MLP-CLASS} (an MLP on class embeddings only---still essentially
$\phi$-measurable); \textbf{MLP-SPATIAL} (adds translation/scale-invariant box
geometry: relative position, scale, aspect, IoU); and \textbf{MLP-SPATIAL+} (a larger
spatial network). The spatial features are the ``pixels beyond $\phi$'' that
distinguish reasoning from prior-fitting. All five are trained once; WAGER then
consumes their cached predicate distributions.

\paragraph{The anchor holds.}
Table~\ref{tab:models} reports the readouts. As the credibility gate demands, FREQ
returns $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}=-0.005$ (CI straddling/below $0$) and e-value
$0.49\approx 1$: WAGER correctly certifies that a lookup table performs no visual
reasoning. FREQ+OVERLAP is likewise certified non-reasoning. The MLPs that genuinely
consult box geometry accumulate decisive evidence (e-values up to the numerical
ceiling $\sim\!10^{304}$) and positive $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}$, growing with spatial
capacity. Crucially, test accuracy moves by under $1.3$ points across all five models,
so accuracy alone cannot tell whether a gain is reasoning or prior-fitting---the
distinction WAGER is built to make.

\paragraph{Almost all ``information'' is prior-fitting.}
Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} decomposes total information into the two channels. For every
model the prior channel ($I^{\mathrm{prior}}\approx 2.5$ nats) dwarfs the reasoning
channel ($I^{\mathrm{reason}}\le 0.07$ nats): even the strongest model spends over
$97\%$ of its information budget on frequency-fitting. Figure~\ref{fig:wealth} shows
the testing-by-betting view directly: the capital of FREQ and FREQ+OVERLAP never
leaves the neighborhood of its starting value, while the spatial MLPs compound capital
linearly in log-scale---visual confirmation that only those models out-predict their
own self-prior projection. Figure~\ref{fig:proj} illustrates the mechanism inside a
single $\phi$-cell: the projection $\bar q_f$ is the frozen cell forecaster, and the
spread of per-instance scores $d_i$ around it is exactly what the betting process
harvests.

\subsection{Qualitative examples}\label{sec:qual}
Figure~\ref{fig:qual} grounds the per-instance score on the actual images we process.
For each annotated relation we show the predicted predicate, the FREQ top-1 prediction,
and WAGER's score $d_i$ for MLP-SPATIAL. Where the geometry is informative---a relation
whose predicate depends on the relative position or overlap of the two boxes---$d_i$ is
clearly positive (``image helps''); where the class pair alone already determines the
likely predicate, $d_i\approx 0$ (``prior suffices''). This is the instance-level view
of the aggregate decomposition: WAGER does not reward a confident prediction per se, only
one that beats the model's own frequency-collapsed self.

\subsection{Where reasoning lives: head, body, and tail}\label{sec:strat}
An aggregate $I^{\mathrm{reason}}$ can hide \emph{where} a model reasons. We stratify
the per-instance scores by predicate frequency tier (head: top $5$ predicates; body:
next $11$; tail: remaining $34$) and report the mean reasoning score $\bar d$ per tier
in Figure~\ref{fig:strat} and Table~\ref{tab:strat}. The pattern is striking and
consistent: \emph{head predicates are essentially won by the prior} (for the spatial
MLPs $\bar d\le 0.026$, and for FREQ it is even slightly negative), whereas the
\emph{body and tail carry almost all of the reasoning signal} ($\bar d=0.16$--$0.19$
for MLP-SPATIAL/+). In other words, the small aggregate reasoning channel is not spread
uniformly; it is concentrated on exactly the rare predicates that headline recall
metrics underweight. This is the WAGER-native restatement of a familiar concern---that
aggregate scores mask tail behavior \cite{li2022rethinking, zhu2024rectify}---now with a
distribution-free per-tier measurement rather than a re-weighted metric.

\subsection{Headline finding: the gains are non-substantive}\label{sec:headline}
Figure~\ref{fig:rgr} and Table~\ref{tab:rgr} give the gain attribution. Every accuracy
gain over the frequency baseline is flagged \emph{non-substantive}: the upper CI of its
RGR is well below $0.5$, so most of each reported improvement is prior-recoverable
rather than reasoning. The decision rule is not vacuous---it fires positively when
warranted: the gain from MLP-CLASS to MLP-SPATIAL, which adds genuine box geometry, is
\emph{reasoning-supported} ($\mathrm{RGR}=0.77$, CI $[0.74,0.79]$). The further step to
MLP-SPATIAL+ adds capacity but little new visual signal and is again non-substantive
($\mathrm{RGR}=0.44$). WAGER thus distinguishes a gain that comes from new image
information from one that comes from better prior-fitting or extra capacity, which raw
accuracy (spread of under $1.3$ points across all five models) cannot.

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:abl}
Figure~\ref{fig:abl} varies the main design choices for the MLP-SPATIAL-vs-FREQ gain.
The verdict is stable across the prior-feature granularity (cell vs.\ subject-only),
the shrinkage pseudo-count $m\in\{0,0.25,1\}$, and the number of permutations
$R\in\{10,30,60\}$: all remain non-substantive. The one sensitive knob is the clip
$c$: at the most aggressive clipping ($c=\log K/2$) the ratio crosses $0.5$, because
clipping compresses the large prior channel more than the small reasoning channel; we
therefore report the conservative default $c=\log K$ throughout and recommend it. The
stability of the verdict under $\phi$ coarsening is the most important check: if a
relevant frequency channel were missing from $\phi$, coarsening it would move the
verdict, and it does not.
