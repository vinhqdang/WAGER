\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

Our evaluation proceeds from controlled validation to a real-data application. We first
describe the corpus, the prior-feature instantiation, and the models
(Section~\ref{sec:data}). We then test WAGER in simulations for which the true
reasoning contribution is known (Section~\ref{sec:phase1}), apply it to scene graph
generation on Visual~Genome (Sections~\ref{sec:sgg}--\ref{sec:strat}), and finally ask
whether the conclusions depend on the principal design choices
(Section~\ref{sec:abl}). Unless otherwise noted, all intervals use $\alpha=0.05$.

\subsection{Datasets and experimental setup}\label{sec:data}
Our study uses the Visual~Genome scene-graph corpus \cite{krishna2017visual} in the
PredCls regime---ground-truth boxes and object labels are given and the predicate is to
be predicted---with the standard VG150 vocabulary of $150$ object classes and $50$
predicates. Table~\ref{tab:data} summarizes the corpus. Two properties make it an ideal
stress test for a tool that separates reasoning from prior-fitting. First, the predicate
distribution is heavily \emph{long-tailed}: the five most frequent predicates account
for $71.6\%$ of all relation instances and the single predicate ``on'' for $32.4\%$
(Figure~\ref{fig:longtail}), so a frequency lookup already secures most of any
recall-style metric. Second, the prior-feature space is \emph{sparse}: only $49.2\%$ of
the $150{\times}150$ possible ordered class pairs are observed in training, and $46.4\%$
of the observed cells contain fewer than five instances. This sparsity is exactly why a
naive cell mean fails and the hierarchical backoff of Section~\ref{sec:method} is
required to keep the self-prior projection honest. We hold out half of the test images,
image-blocked, as the frozen projection fold ($113{,}255$ relations) and bet on the
remaining $116{,}350$.

WAGER is instantiated through the prior feature $\phi$, the statistic through which
annotation frequency acts. For Visual~Genome PredCls $\phi$ is the ordered class pair,
but the same machinery applies to VQA/GQA with $\phi$ the question type and to LVIS with
$\phi$ the frequency tier (Table~\ref{tab:phi}); this is the sense in which the method
is benchmark-agnostic, and we return to those settings in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

To exercise the method we construct five predicate predictors that expose progressively
more information beyond the ordered class pair. FREQ is the training-count lookup
$P(\text{predicate}\mid\text{subject},\text{object})$ and is therefore constant within a
$\phi$-cell. FREQ+OVERLAP supplements it with a binary indicator of box overlap, while
MLP-CLASS learns from class embeddings alone and so remains essentially
$\phi$-measurable. MLP-SPATIAL additionally observes translation- and scale-invariant
box geometry---relative position, scale, aspect ratio, and intersection over union---and
MLP-SPATIAL+ increases the capacity of that spatial model. The geometric measurements
are the information beyond $\phi$ that the study uses to separate reasoning from
prior-fitting. Each model is trained once, after which WAGER operates only on its cached
predicate distributions.

\subsection{Simulation validity and power}\label{sec:phase1}
We begin with synthetic data because it permits direct tests of validity and power
against known population quantities. A prior forecaster is mixed with an oracle as
$q_f=(1-\beta)\,\text{prior}+\beta\,\text{oracle}$, making $\beta$ a controlled source
of within-cell information. At the null, where $q_f=\bar q_f$, the wealth process
exceeds the rejection threshold in $0.017$ of $600$ runs---below the nominal
$\alpha=0.05$---while $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}\approx0.004$, as predicted by
Theorem~\ref{thm:evalue}. The anytime-valid intervals attain empirical coverage of
$0.93$ against a $0.90$ target. As $\beta$ increases, the estimated RGR changes
monotonically and follows its ground-truth value with Pearson $r=0.998$, and the
correlation between estimated and true reasoning information is $r=0.999$.
Figure~\ref{fig:phase1} presents the three results together. Thus, before any real-data
claim is made, the simulations establish type-I control, interval coverage, and faithful
recovery of the quantity WAGER is intended to measure.

\subsection{Results on Visual Genome}\label{sec:sgg}
Table~\ref{tab:models} first confirms the anchor behaviour the framework requires. FREQ
returns $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}=-0.005$, with a confidence interval straddling or
below zero, and an e-value of $0.49\approx 1$: WAGER correctly certifies that a lookup
table performs no visual reasoning, and FREQ+OVERLAP is likewise certified
non-reasoning. The MLPs that genuinely consult box geometry instead accumulate decisive
evidence, with e-values reaching the numerical ceiling $\sim\!10^{304}$ and positive
$\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}$ that grows with spatial capacity. Test accuracy, by contrast,
varies by less than $1.3$ percentage points across all five systems, so it gives almost
no indication of whether an improvement arises from reasoning or from prior-fitting---the
distinction WAGER is built to make.

The information decomposition in Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} explains the discrepancy
between accuracy and certified reasoning. For every model the prior channel
($I^{\mathrm{prior}}\approx 2.5$ nats) dwarfs the reasoning channel
($I^{\mathrm{reason}}\le 0.07$ nats): even the strongest model spends more than $97\%$
of its information budget on frequency-fitting. The testing-by-betting view in
Figure~\ref{fig:wealth} makes the same point dynamically---the capital of FREQ and
FREQ+OVERLAP never leaves the neighbourhood of its starting value, while the spatial
MLPs compound capital linearly on a log scale, the signature of a model that genuinely
out-predicts its own self-prior projection. Figure~\ref{fig:proj} resolves the
mechanism inside a single $\phi$-cell: the projection $\bar q_f$ is the frozen cell
forecaster, and it is the spread of per-instance scores $d_i$ around it that the betting
process converts into capital.

These aggregate readouts have a concrete instance-level counterpart on the images
themselves. Figure~\ref{fig:qual} overlays our subject/object boxes and relation arrows
on the actual Visual~Genome images we process, annotating each relation with its
predicted predicate, FREQ's top guess, and the score $d_i$ for MLP-SPATIAL. Where the
predicate depends on the relative position or overlap of the two boxes the score is
clearly positive (``image helps''); where the class pair alone already determines the
likely predicate it is near zero (``prior suffices''). The figure makes visible what
the decomposition asserts: WAGER does not reward a confident prediction as such, only
one that beats the model's own frequency-collapsed self.

\subsection{Where reasoning concentrates, and whether gains are substantive}\label{sec:strat}
An aggregate $I^{\mathrm{reason}}$ can hide \emph{where} a model reasons, so we stratify
the per-instance scores by predicate-frequency tier (head: top $5$ predicates; body:
next $11$; tail: remaining $34$). The mean score $\bar d$ per tier
(Figure~\ref{fig:strat}, Table~\ref{tab:strat}) reveals a striking and consistent
pattern: head predicates are essentially won by the prior---for the spatial MLPs
$\bar d\le 0.026$, and for FREQ it is even slightly negative---whereas the body and tail
carry almost all of the reasoning signal, with $\bar d=0.16$--$0.19$ for MLP-SPATIAL and
MLP-SPATIAL+. The small aggregate reasoning channel is therefore not spread uniformly;
it is concentrated on precisely the rare predicates that headline recall metrics
underweight. This is the WAGER-native restatement of a familiar concern, that aggregate
scores mask tail behaviour \cite{li2022rethinking, zhu2024rectify}, now expressed as a
distribution-free per-tier measurement rather than a re-weighted metric.

The decisive question---whether a reported gain is substantive---is answered by the
Reasoning Gain Ratio (Figure~\ref{fig:rgr}, Table~\ref{tab:rgr}). Every accuracy gain
over the frequency baseline is flagged \emph{non-substantive}, its RGR upper limit well
below $0.5$, so most of each reported improvement is prior-recoverable rather than
reasoning. The decision rule is not vacuous: it fires positively when warranted. The
step from MLP-CLASS to MLP-SPATIAL, which adds genuine box geometry, is
\emph{reasoning-supported} ($\mathrm{RGR}=0.77$, CI $[0.74,0.79]$), whereas the further
step to MLP-SPATIAL+ adds capacity but little new visual signal and is again
non-substantive ($\mathrm{RGR}=0.44$). WAGER thus separates a gain that comes from new
image information from one that comes from better prior-fitting or extra capacity---a
distinction that raw accuracy, varying by under $1.3$ points across all five models,
cannot draw.

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:abl}
Finally we ask whether the headline verdict survives the principal design choices, using
the MLP-SPATIAL-versus-FREQ gain as the test case (Figure~\ref{fig:abl}). The verdict is
stable across the prior-feature granularity (cell versus subject-only), the shrinkage
pseudo-count $m\in\{0,0.25,1\}$, and the number of permutations $R\in\{10,30,60\}$: all
remain non-substantive. The one sensitive knob is the clip $c$: under the most
aggressive clipping ($c=\log K/2$) the ratio crosses $0.5$, because clipping compresses
the large prior channel more than the small reasoning channel, so we report the
conservative default $c=\log K$ throughout. The stability under $\phi$ coarsening is the
most important of these checks, since a missing frequency channel in $\phi$ would move
the verdict when $\phi$ is coarsened, and it does not.
