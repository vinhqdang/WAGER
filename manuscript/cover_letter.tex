Dear Editors,

Thank you for the opportunity to revise the manuscript above. This letter accompanies a
substantially revised submission responding to the decision of major revision. Rather
than a numbered point-by-point response, the sections below summarize the revision
comprehensively; a table of the principal changes is given near the end of this letter.

The paper concerns the comparison of two probabilistic predictors when the labels are
partly determined by a nuisance feature both models observe. In that situation the
score difference confounds two quantities: a closer fit to the conditional label
distribution of the nuisance feature, and genuinely better prediction of individual
cases. Significance testing on the difference does not separate them, and the customary
remedy --- fitting a nuisance-only baseline and comparing against it --- introduces
estimation error, smoothing choices and model-family mismatch into what should be an
exact statement about a fixed pair. Machine-learning benchmarks in computer vision
supply the applications here, but the estimation problem is general.

WAGER answers that question directly, and the contribution is primarily methodological.
Given two frozen probabilistic models, it forms their proper-score contrast, matches
test examples sharing a declared prior feature, and crosses their labels. This yields an
exact finite-sample identity --- the observed gain equals a prior-transported gain plus
an antisymmetric instance-alignment gain --- whose residual is an order-two U-statistic
that, under the quadratic score, equals the within-cell prediction--label covariance the
new model has gained, linking it to the classical resolution term of proper-score
decomposition. The estimator runs in $O(NK)$ time on cached predictions, fits no
baseline model, has no tunable hyperparameter, and comes with cluster-robust intervals
and a cell-stratified randomization test. This revision adds four further results aimed
at this journal's readership specifically: the covariance identity is shown to hold for
the entire Bregman-score family, not the quadratic score alone; an asymptotic normality
theorem justifies the reported intervals in the large-sample limit; a Cauchy--Schwarz
sensitivity bound quantifies how far an unrecorded confounder could move the residual
from what a fully adjusted audit would find; and, at a trivial audit feature, the
statistic and its interval reduce exactly to a clustered Diebold--Mariano/Giacomini--White
test, situating the estimator precisely relative to comparative forecast evaluation.
Two further exact results were already established: leave-one-out transport removes an
attenuation bias of $(n_c-1)/n_c$ that a naive in-sample plug-in incurs, and coarsening
the audit cell shifts the estimate by an explicit between-cell covariance term.

Because the method is a measuring instrument, the paper takes its validation seriously.
Beyond recovery and null designs, we verify the coverage of the primary interval in a
regime that stresses it, and we report discriminant-validity experiments establishing
what the two channels do and do not separate --- including an honest limitation, that a
monotone recalibration moves score mass between the channels, together with the
calibration-matched protocol that removes it and a demonstration that the protocol
works.

The empirical results show the instrument earning its keep, and the clearest
demonstration concerns a method the field already compares rather than any predictor of
ours. We evaluate the released causal MOTIFS checkpoint of Tang et al.\ (CVPR 2020) in
both of its modes with the authors' own code on the standard Visual Genome PredCls
split, reproducing their published behaviour, and decompose the debiased model against
the biased baseline. Read naively, the Total Direct Effect variant appears to lose a
little instance alignment. That reading does not survive the control this paper argues
for: the two models differ sharply in confidence, and once temperatures are matched on
held-out images the proper-score difference is overwhelmingly prior-transported under
both the quadratic and the log score, though the two scores disagree on whether any
instance-alignment remainder survives ($-0.00006$, 95\% CI $[-0.00120,+0.00109]$ under
the quadratic score; $+0.04396$, CI $[+0.04079,+0.04714]$ under the log score) --- in
either reading at least an order of magnitude smaller than the prior-channel shift. A
widely used debiasing method therefore buys ten points of mean recall while changing
within-cell instance alignment by at most a small fraction of that amount, possibly
none --- which is what its construction subtracts, so the finding characterises the
method rather than indicting it, while showing that a metric read as evidence about
rare predicates can move sharply on evidence that is mostly, and perhaps entirely, of
a different kind.

Two further results point in complementary directions. A model reading real pixels
through a frozen CLIP encoder scores $0.04655$ \emph{lower} overall --- an evaluator
reading that number would discard it --- yet aligns with individual instances
significantly \emph{better} than the model that beats it, its entire deficit lying in
the annotation prior and its advantage surviving both post-hoc prior correction and
calibration matching. Applied unchanged to long-tailed image classification across
seeds and imbalance ratios, a calibration-matched reanalysis overturns what the raw
decomposition appears to say about two re-weighting schedules. A third domain with
neither a spatial nor a class-frequency prior --- long-tailed text classification ---
shows the same estimator, unmodified, produces a different and equally interpretable
composition: the schedule that buys real instance alignment on one domain buys almost
none on the other, which is itself evidence that the decomposition tracks a genuine
property of the training-data pair rather than a fixed property of a method's name.

I believe the work fits this journal's remit in the theory and practice of statistical
inference and model comparison: the central contribution is an estimand and its exact
finite-sample and asymptotic inference theory --- U-statistic identities, a Hájek
influence function, an asymptotic normality result, and a sensitivity analysis for an
unmeasured confounder in the tradition of Rosenbaum and Cinelli \& Hazlett --- together
with an exact relationship to the classical Diebold--Mariano/Giacomini--White tests for
comparative forecast evaluation. The computer-vision and text-classification benchmarks
are the applications on which the instrument is exercised and validated, not the claim
itself; no state-of-the-art result is asserted, and none is needed for the argument.

\textbf{Summary of principal changes in this revision.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Four new theoretical results aimed at this journal's readership: the
Bregman-score generalization of the covariance identity (Theorem~2), the asymptotic
normality theorem for the dataset-level estimator (Theorem~3), the sensitivity bound
for an unrecorded confounder and its data-free worst-case corollary (Proposition~3 and
Corollary), and the exact reduction to a clustered Diebold--Mariano/Giacomini--White
test at a trivial audit feature (Corollary).
\item A third empirical domain, long-tailed text classification on 20 Newsgroups, with
neither a spatial nor a class-frequency prior, added to the existing scene-graph and
long-tailed image classification studies.
\item Two results corrected after further internal review, both verified by hand before
editing: a related-work argument claiming a self-prediction bias "does not cancel under
subtraction'' was found to be false when both compared models share the same audit
partition, and has been replaced with the paper's actually-valid distinguishing point
(three newly verified citations added); and the new asymptotic normality theorem's proof
required an explicit condition, now stated, that the paper's own heavy-tailed real-data
application does not attain in-sample, with the reported coverage simulation supplying
the finite-sample evidence in its place.
\item The headline empirical finding (Section~4.6) has been restated to reflect a
robustness check the manuscript itself reports: the quadratic and log scores agree that
the audited debiasing method's improvement is overwhelmingly prior-transported, but
disagree on whether any instance-alignment remainder is exactly zero, and the revision
now states this precisely rather than reporting only the score that read as null.
\end{itemize}
