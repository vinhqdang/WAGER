\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced WAGER, Within-cell Antisymmetric Gain Evaluation of Reasoning, a direct
algorithm for attributing the score improvement between two vision models. By crossing
labels among examples with the same prior feature, WAGER constructs the prior-only
counterfactual from the evaluated data themselves and obtains an exact identity between
total, prior-transported, and instance-alignment gains. The residual is an interpretable
U-statistic, equals a conditional covariance gain under the quadratic score, and is
computable in $O(NK)$ time. Controlled experiments support null calibration and signal
recovery, and on Visual Genome the method assigns a class-only improvement entirely to
the prior channel while identifying the additional alignment that spatial geometry
supplies. Carried over without modification to long-tailed classification, it goes on to
distinguish a re-weighting schedule whose near-zero aggregate score conceals two large
cancelling channels from one whose genuine improvement proves to be part prior-refitting
and part rare-class alignment, a separation that neither an aggregate score nor an
accuracy difference can make. It is this gain-specific mechanism, rather than the
familiar combination of a frequency baseline with a generic significance test, that
constitutes the paper's technical contribution.
