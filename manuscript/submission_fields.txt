TITLE
-----
WAGER: Within-cell Antisymmetric Gain Evaluation of Reasoning

ABSTRACT (249 words; CVIU limit 250)
----------------------------------------
Vision benchmarks with strong annotation priors can reward a new model whose gain does not come from better instance-specific visual prediction. Existing audits compare a model against a frequency baseline, confounding how well each system fits the prior with whether the difference between them uses information beyond it. We introduce WAGER, a pairwise attribution algorithm. For two frozen probabilistic models it forms their proper-score contrast, matches examples sharing a declared prior feature and crosses their labels, giving an exact finite-sample identity: observed gain equals a prior-transported gain plus an antisymmetric instance-alignment gain. The residual is an order-two U-statistic which, under the quadratic score, equals twice the within-cell prediction-label covariance gained by the new model, linking it to the classical resolution term of proper-score decomposition. It costs O(NK) time, needs no fitted prior model, and admits cluster-robust inference. Two further exact results follow: leave-one-out transport removes an attenuation bias of (n_c-1)/n_c that a naive in-sample plug-in incurs, and coarsening the audit cell shifts the estimate by a between-cell covariance term. On 229,605 Visual Genome relations a class-only network improves the frequency baseline entirely through the prior channel, while a frozen-CLIP model scoring 0.04655 worse overall has significantly better instance alignment (+0.00641, p=.002) than the box-geometry model it loses to. Applied unchanged to long-tailed classification, it separates two re-weighting schedules that aggregate scores cannot: one whose near-zero total gain conceals a +0.19713 prior gain cancelling a -0.19569 alignment loss, and one whose genuine improvement is two-thirds prior-recoverable and one-third instance alignment.
