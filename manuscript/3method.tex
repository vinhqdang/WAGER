\subsection{Setup and the self-prior projection}\label{sec:method-proj}
Let $x$ be an instance (for scene graph generation, an ordered object pair and its
image region), $y\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ the ground-truth label, and $\phi(x)$ the
\emph{prior features}: the cheap, frequency-carrying sufficient statistic through which
annotation frequency acts (for SGG, $\phi(x)=(\text{subject class},\text{object
class})$). A model $f$ supplies a predictive distribution $q_f(y\mid x)$, the softmax
of cached logits, and $u(y)=1/K$ is the uniform reference. We never assume that $q_f$
is calibrated, nor that the true conditionals $p(y\mid x)$ or $p(y\mid\phi)$ are known.
The only structural assumption is that instances on an evaluation stream $B$ are
\emph{exchangeable}, which we enforce by randomizing their order with image-blocked
permutations (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

The object at the centre of the method collapses a model onto its own prior.

\begin{definition}[Self-prior projection]\label{def:proj}
For a fixed model $f$, its self-prior projection is the model's own predictions
averaged within each prior-feature cell,
\begin{equation}
\bar q_f(y \mid \phi) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\, q_f(y \mid X)\;\middle|\;\phi(X)=\phi \,\right].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

By construction $\bar q_f$ is the best summary of $f$ that is measurable with respect to
the prior features alone: a forecaster that has discarded every pixel-level cue and
kept only what frequency context implies. The decisive choice is to compare $f$ with
this frequency-collapsed version of \emph{itself} rather than with an external
frequency table. An external table $\hat p(y\mid\phi)$ built from training counts would
make the null ``$f$ reasons no better than the prior'' hold only if the table equalled
the true $p(y\mid\phi)$, which it never exactly does; that mismatch is a free parameter
an adversary or an unlucky dataset could exploit to manufacture or hide reasoning.
Freezing $\bar q_f$ from $f$'s own predictions removes the parameter, because under the
null the two forecasters share every nuisance---calibration, vocabulary bias,
temperature---so any out-of-fold predictive advantage of $q_f$ over $\bar q_f$ can come
only from information beyond $\phi$.

We estimate $\bar q_f$ on a fold $A$ disjoint from the evaluation stream. A naive cell
mean is unusable when most cells are sparse (Figure~\ref{fig:longtail}), so we use a
hierarchical empirical-Bayes backoff with Krichevsky--Trofimov-style smoothing: writing
$n_\phi$ for the cell count and $\pi(\phi)$ for a parent distribution,
\begin{equation}
\hat{\bar q}_f(\cdot\mid\phi)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{j\in A}q_f(\cdot\mid x_j)\,\mathbb{1}[\phi(x_j)=\phi]\;+\;m\,\pi(\phi)}{n_\phi + m},
\end{equation}
with the hierarchy cell~$(\text{subj},\text{obj})\to$ coarse~$(\text{subj})\to$ global
mean. A second-order Jensen correction computed from the within-cell variance debiases
the log-projection; it cancels in the total information $I^{\mathrm{tot}}$ and only
re-allocates mis-credited reasoning back into the prior channel. A model whose
predictions are a function of $\phi$ alone---FREQ, or a class-only network---uses the
identical backoff, so its out-of-fold reasoning score collapses to $\approx 0$, which
is exactly what makes the FREQ anchor hold on real data (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

\subsection{Certifying and quantifying reasoning by betting}\label{sec:method-bet}
On the evaluation stream we score each instance by how much the model beats its own
projection, and then bet on those scores.

\begin{definition}[Prior-excess log-score]\label{def:score}
On the evaluation stream, define
\begin{equation}
d_i \;=\; \log q_f(y_i \mid x_i) - \log \hat{\bar q}_f(y_i \mid \phi(x_i)),
\qquad \tilde d_i = \mathrm{clip}(d_i,-c,c),
\end{equation}
with default clip $c=\log K$. A positive $d_i$ means that at instance $i$, using the
image beyond $\phi$ helped predict the true label. Clipping keeps the score bounded,
which is what licenses the betting argument below; the level $c$ trades robustness
against sensitivity, and we fix it at $\log K$, the natural scale of a log-likelihood
ratio over $K$ classes.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wealth process]\label{def:wealth}
We test $H_0:\ \mu:=\mathbb{E}[\tilde d_i]\le 0$ (``$f$ predicts no better than its
prior-projection'') against $H_1:\mu>0$ via
\begin{equation}
W_0=1,\qquad W_n=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\!\bigl(1+\lambda_i\,\tilde d_i\bigr),\qquad
\lambda_i\in[0,\tfrac1c)\ \text{predictable},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_i$ chosen online by the Online-Newton-Step (aGRAPA) rule of Waudby-Smith
and Ramdas \cite{waudbysmith2024estimating}, a closed-form predictable update requiring
no tuning.
\end{definition}

Framing the question as a bet on a predictive mean, rather than as a test of the
conditional independence $Y\perp X\mid\phi(X)$, is deliberate: consistent
conditional-independence testing is impossible without further assumptions
\cite{shah2020hardness}, whereas testing $\mathbb{E}[\tilde d]\le 0$ with a betting
martingale is precisely the regime in which game-theoretic statistics delivers
finite-sample validity \cite{shafer2021testing, ramdas2023game,
waudbysmith2024estimating}.

\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid certificate]\label{thm:evalue}
Under $H_0$, $(W_n)$ is a non-negative supermartingale with $\mathbb{E}[W_n]\le 1$;
it is therefore an e-value, and by Ville's inequality \cite{ville1939etude}, for any
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, $\Pr[\exists n: W_n\ge 1/\alpha]\le\alpha$. Rejecting $H_0$ the
first time $W_n\ge 1/\alpha$ controls type-I error at level $\alpha$ with no fixed
sample size and no distributional assumption beyond exchangeability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\lambda_i$ is $\mathcal F_{i-1}$-measurable and $1+\lambda_i\tilde d_i\ge 0$ because
$\lambda_i<1/c$ and $\tilde d_i\ge -c$. Under $H_0$,
$\mathbb{E}[1+\lambda_i\tilde d_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]=1+\lambda_i\,\mathbb{E}[\tilde
d_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le 1$, so $(W_n)$ is a non-negative supermartingale;
telescoping gives $\mathbb{E}[W_n]\le 1$ and Ville's inequality yields the bound. The
validity holds \emph{without knowing the true prior} precisely because $\bar q_f$ is
frozen on fold $A$ and treated as a fixed competing forecaster. A full proof is given
in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Growth rate $=$ usable reasoning information]\label{thm:growth}
With the log-optimal predictable bet
$\lambda_i^\star=\arg\max_\lambda \mathbb{E}[\log(1+\lambda\tilde d)\mid\mathcal
F_{i-1}]$, the growth rate converges almost surely, and as $c\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
G(f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac1n\log W_n
=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log q_f(Y\mid X)-\log\bar q_f(Y\mid\phi(X))\right]=:\mathcal I_f^{\mathrm{reason}}\ \ge 0 .
\end{equation}
If $q_f$ equals the true posterior and $\bar q_f$ the true $p(\cdot\mid\phi)$, then
$\mathcal I_f^{\mathrm{reason}}=I(Y;X\mid\phi(X))$, the conditional mutual information.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Kelly \cite{kelly1956new} and Cover and Thomas \cite{cover2006elements} the
log-optimal growth rate equals the expected log-score differential, which the strong
law drives to $\mathbb{E}[\tilde d]$; as $c\to\infty$, $\tilde d_i\to d_i$. The chain
rule $I(Y;X)=I(Y;\phi(X))+I(Y;X\mid\phi(X))$, valid because $\phi$ is a function of
$X$, identifies the residual with conditional mutual information in the calibrated case
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
\end{proof}

The same wealth process therefore \emph{certifies} reasoning (through $W_n$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:evalue}) and \emph{quantifies} it (through $G(f)$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:growth})---the central unification of the method. The measurement is
moreover robust to miscalibration.

\begin{corollary}[Calibration invariance]\label{cor:calib}
$\mathcal I_f^{\mathrm{reason}}$ is invariant to any recalibration map applied
uniformly within a $\phi$-cell (e.g.\ per-cell temperature scaling), because the map
cancels between $q_f$ and $\bar q_f$. Hence miscalibration shared within a prior cell
cannot inflate measured reasoning; by Gibbs' inequality an arbitrarily miscalibrated
$q_f$ can only \emph{under}-estimate the true reasoning information, never manufacture
it.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The Reasoning Gain Ratio}\label{sec:method-rgr}
We decompose each model's total information over the uniform reference into a
prior-fitting channel and a reasoning channel,
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\log\bar q_f(Y\mid\phi)-\log u(Y)]}_{I^{\mathrm{prior}}_f}
+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\log q_f(Y\mid X)-\log\bar q_f(Y\mid\phi)]}_{I^{\mathrm{reason}}_f}
= I^{\mathrm{tot}}_f ,
\end{equation}
and attribute a reported improvement to one channel or the other.

\begin{definition}[Reasoning Gain Ratio]\label{def:rgr}
For a claimed improvement from $f$ to $f'$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RGR}(f',f)=\frac{I^{\mathrm{reason}}_{f'}-I^{\mathrm{reason}}_{f}}
{I^{\mathrm{tot}}_{f'}-I^{\mathrm{tot}}_{f}} ,
\end{equation}
the fraction of the information gain attributable to better reasoning rather than
better prior-fitting. If the upper confidence limit of $\mathrm{RGR}$ is below $0.5$,
the majority of the gain is prior-recoverable and the claim is flagged
\emph{non-substantive}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid CI for RGR]\label{thm:rgr}
Each of $I^{\mathrm{reason}}$ and $I^{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the mean of a bounded score, so
the betting confidence sequence of \cite{waudbysmith2024estimating} gives each an
anytime-valid $(1-\alpha)$ interval. Combining the numerator and denominator intervals
by Fieller's method \cite{fieller1954some} under a Bonferroni split yields a
$(1-2\alpha)$ anytime-valid interval for $\mathrm{RGR}(f',f)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Algorithm and implementation}\label{sec:method-algo}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:wager} summarizes the driver. Two properties make it both cheap and
exact. First, the GPU is used only once, to dump the cached probability vectors; all
WAGER computation is deterministic CPU NumPy with an $O(NK)$ hot loop, so the full
five-model study including $R$ permutations and every interval completes in about three
minutes on a single core. Second, because the per-instance scores are order-independent
under a frozen train-fit projection, the $R$ image-blocked permutations only reshuffle
the betting order, which builds the e-value distribution and averages the anytime-valid
intervals across orders. Blocking at the image level---all relations of an image move
together in randomized image order---yields an exchangeable stream at the block level,
which is all Theorem~\ref{thm:evalue} requires.

A concrete instance fixes intuition. Consider a cell $\phi=(\text{man},\text{horse})$
whose frozen projection assigns $\bar q_f(\text{riding}\mid\phi)=0.30$. If, for an
instance whose true predicate is ``riding'', the model sees the boxes overlap with the
man above the horse and outputs $q_f(\text{riding}\mid x)=0.62$, then
$d=\log 0.62-\log 0.30=+0.73>0$ and the bettor profits, because the image moved the
prediction toward the truth beyond what the class pair alone implies. If instead the
model returns the cell-typical $0.30$, then $d=0$ and the bettor neither gains nor
loses. A model that is merely \emph{confident} but constant within the cell earns no
capital at all---the mechanism behind both the FREQ anchor and calibration invariance
(Corollary~\ref{cor:calib}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{WAGER core routine}\label{alg:wager}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require cached $q_f$ for each model; prior map $\phi$; coarse key; level $\alpha$;
clip $c=\log K$; permutations $R$.
\Ensure per-model e-value and $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}},\hat I^{\mathrm{prior}}$ with
CIs; per-pair RGR with verdict.
\State fit frozen $\bar q_f$ on fold $A$ via hierarchical backoff (Def.~\ref{def:proj})
\For{each model $f$ and each $i$ in stream $B$}
    \State $d_i\gets\mathrm{clip}(\log q_f(y_i\mid x_i)-\log\bar q_f(y_i\mid\phi_i),-c,c)$;\quad
    $e_i\gets\mathrm{clip}(\log\bar q_f(y_i\mid\phi_i)-\log u(y_i),-c,c)$
\EndFor
\For{$r=1\dots R$ (image-blocked permutations of $B$)}
    \State $\lambda_i\gets\textsc{ONS}(\tilde d_{1:i-1})$;\quad
    $W_n\gets\prod(1+\lambda_i d_i)$ \Comment{e-value, growth (Thm.~\ref{thm:evalue},\ref{thm:growth})}
\EndFor
\State $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}\!\gets\!\mathrm{mean}(d)$,
$\hat I^{\mathrm{prior}}\!\gets\!\mathrm{mean}(e)$, with betting CIs
\State for each pair: $\mathrm{RGR}\gets\Delta\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}/\Delta\hat
I^{\mathrm{tot}}$ with Fieller CI (Thm.~\ref{thm:rgr}); verdict by the $0.5$ rule
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
