\subsection{Problem setup and notation}
Let $x$ be an instance (for scene graph generation, an ordered object pair and its
image region), $y\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ the ground-truth label, and $\phi(x)$ the
\emph{prior features}: the cheap, frequency-carrying sufficient statistic through
which annotation frequency acts (for SGG, $\phi(x)=(\text{subject class},
\text{object class})$). A model $f$ supplies a predictive distribution
$q_f(y\mid x)$ (a softmax of cached logits); $u(y)=1/K$ is the uniform reference. We
never assume $q_f$ is calibrated, nor that the true conditionals $p(y\mid x)$ or
$p(y\mid\phi)$ are known. We assume only that instances on an evaluation stream $B$
are \emph{exchangeable}, which we enforce by randomizing order with image-blocked
permutations (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

\subsection{The self-prior projection}
\begin{definition}[Self-prior projection]\label{def:proj}
For a fixed model $f$, its self-prior projection is the model's own predictions
averaged within each prior-feature cell,
\begin{equation}
\bar q_f(y \mid \phi) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\, q_f(y \mid X)\;\middle|\;\phi(X)=\phi \,\right].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
By construction $\bar q_f$ is the best summary of $f$ measurable with respect to the
prior features alone: a forecaster that has discarded every pixel-level cue and kept
only what frequency context implies. The essential construction compares $f$ with its
\emph{own} frequency-collapsed self rather than to an external FREQ table whose
mismatch with the true prior would break validity; the two share calibration,
vocabulary, and idiosyncrasies, so any out-of-fold predictive advantage of $q_f$ over
$\bar q_f$ can come only from information beyond $\phi$.

\paragraph{Estimation on real, heavy-tailed data.}
We estimate $\bar q_f$ on a fold $A$ disjoint from the evaluation stream. The naive
cell mean is unusable when most cells are sparse (Figure~\ref{fig:longtail}), so we
use a hierarchical empirical-Bayes backoff with Krichevsky--Trofimov-style smoothing.
Writing $n_\phi$ for the cell count and $\pi(\phi)$ for a parent distribution,
\begin{equation}
\hat{\bar q}_f(\cdot\mid\phi)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{j\in A}q_f(\cdot\mid x_j)\mathbb{1}[\phi(x_j)=\phi]\;+\;m\,\pi(\phi)}{n_\phi + m},
\end{equation}
with the backoff hierarchy cell~$(\text{subj},\text{obj})\to$
coarse~$(\text{subj})\to$ global mean. A second-order Jensen correction, computed
from the within-cell variance, debiases the log-projection; it cancels in the total
information $I^{\text{tot}}$ and only re-allocates mis-credited reasoning back into
the prior channel. A model whose predictions are a function of $\phi$ alone (FREQ, a
class-only network) uses the identical backoff, so its out-of-fold reasoning score
collapses to $\approx 0$---this is exactly what makes the FREQ anchor hold on real
data (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

\subsection{Per-instance score and the wealth process}
\begin{definition}[Prior-excess log-score]\label{def:score}
On the evaluation stream, define
\begin{equation}
d_i \;=\; \log q_f(y_i \mid x_i) - \log \hat{\bar q}_f(y_i \mid \phi(x_i)),
\qquad \tilde d_i = \mathrm{clip}(d_i,-c,c),
\end{equation}
with default clip $c=\log K$. Here $d_i>0$ means that at instance $i$, using the image
beyond $\phi$ helped predict the true label.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wealth process]\label{def:wealth}
We test $H_0:\ \mu:=\mathbb{E}[\tilde d_i]\le 0$ (``$f$ predicts no better than its
prior-projection'') against $H_1:\mu>0$ via
\begin{equation}
W_0=1,\qquad W_n=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\!\bigl(1+\lambda_i\,\tilde d_i\bigr),\qquad
\lambda_i\in[0,\tfrac1c)\ \text{predictable},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_i$ chosen online by the Online-Newton-Step (aGRAPA) rule of
Waudby-Smith and Ramdas \cite{waudbysmith2024estimating}, a closed-form predictable
update requiring no tuning.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid certificate]\label{thm:evalue}
Under $H_0$, $(W_n)$ is a non-negative supermartingale with $\mathbb{E}[W_n]\le 1$;
it is therefore an e-value, and by Ville's inequality \cite{ville1939etude}, for any
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, $\Pr[\exists n: W_n\ge 1/\alpha]\le\alpha$. Rejecting $H_0$ the
first time $W_n\ge 1/\alpha$ controls type-I error at level $\alpha$ with no fixed
sample size and no distributional assumption beyond exchangeability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\lambda_i$ is $\mathcal F_{i-1}$-measurable and $1+\lambda_i\tilde d_i\ge 0$ because
$\lambda_i<1/c$ and $\tilde d_i\ge -c$. Under $H_0$,
$\mathbb{E}[1+\lambda_i\tilde d_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]=1+\lambda_i\,\mathbb{E}[\tilde
d_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le 1$, so $(W_n)$ is a non-negative supermartingale;
telescoping gives $\mathbb{E}[W_n]\le 1$, and Ville's inequality yields the bound. The
design choice that buys validity \emph{without knowing the true prior} is that
$\bar q_f$ is frozen on fold $A$ and treated purely as a competing forecaster: we run
an honest predictive comparison of two fixed forecasters, not a conditional-independence
test (which is hard in general \cite{shah2020hardness}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Growth rate $=$ usable reasoning information]\label{thm:growth}
With the log-optimal predictable bet
$\lambda_i^\star=\arg\max_\lambda \mathbb{E}[\log(1+\lambda\tilde d)\mid\mathcal
F_{i-1}]$, the growth rate converges almost surely, and as $c\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
G(f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac1n\log W_n
=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log q_f(Y\mid X)-\log\bar q_f(Y\mid\phi(X))\right]=:\mathcal I_f^{\mathrm{reason}}\ \ge 0 .
\end{equation}
If $q_f$ equals the true posterior and $\bar q_f$ the true $p(\cdot\mid\phi)$, then
$\mathcal I_f^{\mathrm{reason}}=I(Y;X\mid\phi(X))$, the conditional mutual information.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Kelly \cite{kelly1956new} and Cover and Thomas \cite{cover2006elements}, the
log-optimal growth rate of a forecasting game equals the expected log-score
differential, which the strong law drives to $\mathbb{E}[\tilde d]$; as $c\to\infty$,
$\tilde d_i\to d_i$. The chain rule
$I(Y;X)=I(Y;\phi(X))+I(Y;X\mid\phi(X))$, valid because $\phi$ is a function of $X$,
identifies the residual with conditional mutual information in the calibrated case.
\end{proof}
The same wealth process thus \emph{certifies} (via $W_n$) and \emph{quantifies} (via
$G(f)$) reasoning beyond the prior---the central unification.

\begin{corollary}[Calibration invariance]\label{cor:calib}
$\mathcal I_f^{\mathrm{reason}}$ is invariant to any recalibration map applied
uniformly within a $\phi$-cell (e.g.\ per-cell temperature scaling), because the map
cancels between $q_f$ and $\bar q_f$. Hence miscalibration shared within a prior cell
cannot inflate measured reasoning; by Gibbs' inequality an arbitrarily miscalibrated
$q_f$ can only \emph{under}-estimate the true reasoning information, never manufacture
it.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The Reasoning Gain Ratio}
Decompose each model's total information over uniform into a prior-fitting and a
reasoning channel,
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\log\bar q_f(Y\mid\phi)-\log u(Y)]}_{I^{\mathrm{prior}}_f}
+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\log q_f(Y\mid X)-\log\bar q_f(Y\mid\phi)]}_{I^{\mathrm{reason}}_f}
= I^{\mathrm{tot}}_f .
\end{equation}
\begin{definition}[Reasoning Gain Ratio]\label{def:rgr}
For a claimed improvement from $f$ to $f'$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RGR}(f',f)=\frac{I^{\mathrm{reason}}_{f'}-I^{\mathrm{reason}}_{f}}
{I^{\mathrm{tot}}_{f'}-I^{\mathrm{tot}}_{f}} ,
\end{equation}
the fraction of the information gain attributable to better reasoning rather than
better prior-fitting. \textbf{Decision rule:} if the upper confidence limit of
$\mathrm{RGR}$ is below $0.5$, the majority of the gain is prior-recoverable and the
claim is flagged \emph{non-substantive}.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid CI for RGR]\label{thm:rgr}
Each of $I^{\mathrm{reason}}$ and $I^{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the mean of a bounded score, so
the betting confidence sequence of \cite{waudbysmith2024estimating} gives each an
anytime-valid $(1-\alpha)$ interval. Combining numerator and denominator intervals by
Fieller's method \cite{fieller1954some} under a Bonferroni split yields a $(1-2\alpha)$
anytime-valid interval for $\mathrm{RGR}(f',f)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Algorithm and implementation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:wager} summarizes the driver. Two implementation choices make the
procedure efficient while preserving the stated comparison. First, the GPU is used
\emph{once}, only to dump cached probability
vectors; all WAGER computation is CPU NumPy with an $O(NK)$ hot loop. Second, because
the per-instance scores are order-independent under a frozen train-fit projection, the
$R$ image-blocked permutations only reshuffle the betting order to build the e-value
distribution and average the anytime-valid intervals across orders.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{WAGER core routine}\label{alg:wager}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require cached $q_f$ for each model; prior map $\phi$; coarse key; level $\alpha$;
clip $c=\log K$; permutations $R$.
\Ensure per-model e-value and $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}},\hat I^{\mathrm{prior}}$ with
CIs; per-pair RGR with verdict.
\State fit frozen $\bar q_f$ on fold $A$ via hierarchical backoff (Def.~\ref{def:proj})
\For{each model $f$ and each $i$ in stream $B$}
    \State $d_i\gets\mathrm{clip}(\log q_f(y_i\mid x_i)-\log\bar q_f(y_i\mid\phi_i),-c,c)$;\quad
    $e_i\gets\mathrm{clip}(\log\bar q_f(y_i\mid\phi_i)-\log u(y_i),-c,c)$
\EndFor
\For{$r=1\dots R$ (image-blocked permutations of $B$)}
    \State $\lambda_i\gets\textsc{ONS}(\tilde d_{1:i-1})$;\quad
    $W_n\gets\prod(1+\lambda_i d_i)$ \Comment{e-value, growth (Thm.~\ref{thm:evalue},\ref{thm:growth})}
\EndFor
\State $\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}\!\gets\!\mathrm{mean}(d)$,
$\hat I^{\mathrm{prior}}\!\gets\!\mathrm{mean}(e)$, with betting CIs
\State for each pair: $\mathrm{RGR}\gets\Delta\hat I^{\mathrm{reason}}/\Delta\hat
I^{\mathrm{tot}}$ with Fieller CI (Thm.~\ref{thm:rgr}); verdict by the $0.5$ rule
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Design rationale}
Four choices distinguish WAGER from a naive ``compare to FREQ'' diagnostic, and each is
what makes the certificate valid rather than merely suggestive.

\paragraph{Self-prior, not an external baseline.}
If we compared $q_f$ to an external frequency table $\hat p(y\mid\phi)$ built from
training counts, the null ``$f$ reasons no better than the prior'' would only hold if
that table equaled the true $p(y\mid\phi)$---which it never exactly does. The mismatch
is a free parameter that an adversary (or an unlucky dataset) can exploit to
manufacture or hide reasoning. Freezing $\bar q_f$ from $f$'s own predictions removes
that parameter: under $H_0$ the comparison is between two forecasters that share every
nuisance (calibration, vocabulary bias, temperature), so any out-of-fold edge is
attributable to $\phi$-excess information alone.

\paragraph{Betting, not a conditional-independence test.}
Asking ``is $Y\perp X \mid \phi(X)$ under $f$?'' is a conditional-independence question,
and consistent CI testing without further assumptions is impossible
\cite{shah2020hardness}. WAGER sidesteps this by testing a \emph{predictive} mean
($\mathbb{E}[\tilde d]\le 0$) with a betting martingale, which is exactly the regime
where game-theoretic statistics gives finite-sample validity
\cite{shafer2021testing, ramdas2023game, waudbysmith2024estimating}.

\paragraph{Clipping for boundedness.}
The wealth factor $1+\lambda\tilde d$ must stay non-negative for the supermartingale
argument; clipping $d$ to $[-c,c]$ and capping $\lambda<1/c$ guarantees it. The
clipping level controls the trade-off between robustness and sensitivity
(Section~\ref{sec:abl}); we
fix $c=\log K$, the natural scale of a log-likelihood ratio over $K$ classes.

\paragraph{Image-blocked exchangeability.}
Multiple relations share an image and are not independent. Permuting at the image level
(all pairs of an image move together, in randomized image order) yields an exchangeable
stream at the block level, which is all Theorem~\ref{thm:evalue} needs, and averaging
over $R$ such permutations stabilizes the e-value and the interval.

\subsection{Computational cost}
WAGER consumes cached probability vectors, never models. The GPU is used once, to dump
$\{(y_i, q_f(\cdot\mid x_i), \phi_i)\}$ to disk; all subsequent computation is CPU
NumPy. Fitting the projection is a single pass of cell-wise accumulation
($O(N_AK)$); the betting stream and the prior channel are $O(N_BK)$; the betting
confidence sequence evaluates a grid of $G$ candidate means with a vectorized
fixed-$\lambda$ hedged capital process ($O(N_BG)$ but fully vectorized and chunked to
bound memory). The full five-model VG study, including $R$ permutations and all
intervals, completes in roughly three minutes on a single CPU core after the one-time
logit dump, which is why the framework scales to large test sets and many model pairs.

\subsection{A worked micro-example}
To make the score concrete, consider a cell $\phi=(\text{man},\text{horse})$ with frozen
projection $\bar q_f(\text{riding}\mid\phi)=0.30$. For an instance whose true predicate
is ``riding'' and where the model, seeing the boxes overlap with the man above the horse,
outputs $q_f(\text{riding}\mid x)=0.62$, the reasoning score is
$d=\log 0.62-\log 0.30=+0.73>0$: the image moved the prediction toward the truth beyond
what the class pair alone implies, and the bettor profits. For a second instance where
the model returns the cell-typical $0.30$, $d=0$ and the bettor neither gains nor loses.
A model that is merely \emph{confident} but constant within the cell (e.g.\ always
outputting $0.30$) produces $d\equiv 0$ and earns no capital---this is the mechanism
behind the FREQ anchor and behind calibration-invariance (Corollary~\ref{cor:calib}).
