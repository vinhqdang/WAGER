\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Benchmark priors and shortcut learning}
Dataset bias has long complicated comparisons between visual recognition systems
\cite{torralba2011unbiased,geirhos2020shortcut}. In SGG, the FREQ predictor estimates
$p(\text{predicate}\mid\text{subject},\text{object})$ and is surprisingly competitive
\cite{zellers2018neural}; mean recall and causal interventions were proposed to reduce
this dependence \cite{chen2019knowledge,tang2019learning,tang2020unbiased}, although
metric pathologies remain \cite{li2022rethinking}. More recent SGG work continues this
line under open-vocabulary and knowledge-enhanced settings
\cite{li2024pixels,zhang2024hiker,sun2024unbiased}. The community's reporting standards
have themselves responded to the shortcut: mean recall is reported alongside recall,
often broken down by predicate-frequency group \cite{tang2020unbiased,li2022rethinking},
and zero-shot recall -- introduced with the earliest language-prior models
\cite{lu2016visual} -- checks whether a gain survives on triplets absent from training,
exactly the frequency-exploitation worry \cite{tang2020unbiased}. In VQA, GQA-OOD
stratifies evaluation by answer rarity within question groups \cite{kervadec2021roses},
and VQA-CP changes language priors between train and test \cite{agrawal2018dont},
though changing-prior splits can themselves be exploited \cite{teney2020value}; recent
VQA work continues to target the same language-prior shortcut
\cite{xu2025qirl,kuang2024debiasing}. Benchmark-level audits of exploitable non-visual
shortcuts have also expanded to broader multimodal evaluation
\cite{brown2025benchmark,zhou2025shortcut}. All of these are prior-aware slicings,
splits, or stress tests of a \emph{single} model's performance, and they are effective
as far as they go. What none of them provides is what WAGER targets: an exact
decomposition, with uncertainty, of the score difference between two submitted systems
into a prior-transported channel and an instance-alignment channel. A stratified or
zero-shot metric can show \emph{that} a gain is uneven across the prior; it does not
attribute the pairwise gain itself, on the full evaluation set, into the two channels
with a finite-sample identity and inference.

\subsection{Long-tailed recognition}\label{sec:related-longtail}
The same shortcut, in a structurally identical form, appears well outside scene graph
generation. A long-tailed classifier can raise its aggregate accuracy simply by fitting
the training-time class-frequency prior more precisely, without becoming any better at
discriminating instances within a class \cite{ma2024geometric,duan2024longtailed}. The
canonical remedies rebalance the training objective or the classifier itself:
effective-number class-balanced re-weighting \cite{cui2019classbalanced}, deferred
re-weighting \cite{cao2019learning}, logit adjustment \cite{menon2021long}, and
post-hoc re-training or re-scaling of a decoupled classifier \cite{kang2020decoupling},
with a continuing stream of recent variants
\cite{ma2024geometric,li2026pih2t,luo2025rebalancing}, much as mean-recall and
causal-intervention methods rebalance SGG training. What such remedies share with their
SGG counterparts is that they intervene on how a model is trained or adjusted rather
than on how a trained pair is compared, so none of them attributes an observed score
gap between two finished models to frequency-fitting on the one hand and
instance-specific improvement on the other. That is the question
Section~\ref{sec:cifarlt} puts to WAGER, using the benchmark's own coarse superclasses
as the declared prior feature $\phi$; the estimator exposes the composition of two
re-weighting schedules' gains that their aggregate scores leave hidden -- one whose
near-zero total conceals a large prior improvement cancelling an equally large
alignment loss, the other whose calibration-matched improvement is almost entirely
rare-class instance alignment.

\subsection{Information and predictive probing}
Usable-information analyses measure information accessible to a chosen predictor family
\cite{xu2020theory,ethayarajh2022understanding,hewitt2021conditional}. Their central
objects are model- or family-level quantities. WAGER is deliberately narrower and more
operational: it attributes the proper-score difference between two concrete frozen
models. We do not identify conditional mutual information and do not claim that a
positive residual establishes causal or compositional understanding.

\subsection{Proper scores, randomization, and U-statistics}
Strictly proper scoring rules reward honest predictive distributions
\cite{gneiting2007proper}; WAGER uses their model-pair contrast. A recent survey
revisits estimation and forecast evaluation under proper scoring rules broadly
\cite{waghmare2026proper}, and the reliability--resolution--uncertainty partition
itself continues to be re-derived and extended: to general strictly proper scores and
multiclass outcomes \cite{charpentier2026decomposing}, to entropy/divergence-based
uncertainty quantification \cite{hofman2025uncertainty}, and via an alternative
covariance rearrangement for the Brier score specifically \cite{vieira2026yates}. WAGER
uses the same partition but applies it to a paired gain contrast rather than to one
forecaster, as detailed in Section~\ref{sec:related-decomposition}. Permutation methods
have been used to assess classifier performance and conditional independence
\cite{ojala2010permutation,berrett2020conditional}, with recent work characterizing when
generalized permutation tests attain optimal power \cite{kim2026dispersion} and
extending conditional-independence testing via ball-divergence measures
\cite{banerjee2026ball} and transport-map reductions to the unconditional case
\cite{he2025conditional}. Conditional permutation tests typically estimate or assume a
conditional distribution when confounders are continuous or high-dimensional. WAGER
exploits a different setting: the benchmark supplies a discrete audit cell $\phi$, and
the target is not generic conditional independence but a specific model-to-model score
gain. This permits exact within-cell transport without estimating a nuisance law. The
antisymmetric residual is an order-two U-statistic in the sense of Hoeffding
\cite{hoeffding1948class}; its Hájek-projection inference is part of the same broader
program of finite- and large-sample U-statistic theory that recent work extends to
anytime-valid confidence sequences \cite{cai2026anytime}, enabling an efficient
all-pairs estimator and influence-function inference here. Pairwise betting has also
been used to test exchangeability sequentially \cite{saha2024testing}; WAGER is a
fixed-sample gain decomposition and makes no sequential-validity claim -- the
sequential variant a live leaderboard would need, with anytime validity as submissions
arrive, is a distinct open problem. Its new element
is the gain-contrast transport identity and the resulting attribution algorithm, rather
than permutation or U-statistic theory by itself.

Three adjacent literatures locate the same object from different directions. Inference
on a paired score differential is the classical territory of comparative forecast
evaluation: the Diebold--Mariano test \cite{diebold1995comparing} and conditional
predictive ability tests \cite{giacomini2006tests} ask whether one forecaster beats
another, optionally conditional on covariates -- the closest existing relative of a
gain evaluated conditional on $\phi$. WAGER adds to that tradition an exact
decomposition of the differential itself. Second, the prior channel is precisely the
component that label-shift procedures manipulate post hoc: re-weighting a classifier's
outputs to a new label distribution \cite{saerens2002adjusting,lipton2018detecting} can
add or remove prior-transported gain without touching instance-level evidence, which is
what the prior-matching experiment of Section~\ref{sec:vgvisual} exploits as a
falsification test. Third, evaluating calibration within cells of a declared grouping,
uniformly over many candidate groupings, is the subject of multicalibration
\cite{hebert2018multicalibration}; that literature is the formal treatment of the
multiplicity question raised by the choice of $\phi$
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Relation to classical proper-score decompositions}\label{sec:related-decomposition}
The covariance identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov-id} is a relative of the reliability
resolution--uncertainty partition of a single proper score
\cite{murphy1973vector,degroot1983comparison}, later generalized and made
conditioning-set explicit by \citet{brocker2009reliability}: conditioned on a chosen
grouping variable, a proper score splits into a calibration term and a resolution term,
and the resolution term is a covariance between the forecast and the outcome indicator
within groups. We deliberately build on this fact rather than obscure it. WAGER is not a
new proper score, and $\Delta R_c$ is best understood as a \emph{resolution
difference}, not a new quantity to be evaluated on its own. Two things distinguish WAGER
from applying the classical decomposition to $q_0$ and $q_1$ separately and subtracting
the two resolutions. First, that indirect route requires two resolution estimates, each
of which is generally biased in finite samples by the same self-prediction mechanism
formalized in Section~\ref{sec:attenuation}; the bias does not cancel under subtraction
because it scales with each model's own cell counts. WAGER instead forms the paired
gain vector $H_x(y)$ first and decomposes the \emph{contrast} in one pass, which is what
makes the exact finite-sample identity and the antisymmetric U-statistic kernel possible.
Second, the classical decomposition is a diagnostic for a single forecaster's calibration
and sharpness; it neither targets nor equips a specific pairwise comparison with
inference. WAGER supplies image-cluster-robust confidence intervals and a
cell-stratified randomization test for exactly the gain a benchmark submission claims.
The technical contribution here is narrower than a new form of score decomposition: it is
the transport construction and finite-sample identity that convert a known resolution
concept into a model-to-model attribution algorithm with an exactness and inference
guarantee that the single-model decomposition does not provide.
