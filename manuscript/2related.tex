\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Dataset bias and shortcut learning.}
The tendency of models to exploit superficial statistics rather than the intended
signal is long documented \cite{torralba2011unbiased, misra2016seeing} and was framed
as a general failure mode by Geirhos et al.~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}. The standard
responses---reweighting, resampling, or building out-of-distribution splits---change
\emph{how models are trained or which test set is used}; they do not deliver a
certified measurement of how much of a particular reported gain is shortcut.

\paragraph{Debiasing and evaluation in scene graph generation.}
Frequency priors dominate scene graph generation, motivating a large body of
debiasing work: re-weighted and re-sampled losses, dynamic tree contexts
\cite{tang2019learning}, knowledge routing \cite{chen2019knowledge}, causal
total-direct-effect inference \cite{tang2020unbiased}, and recent informative-SGG
methods \cite{gao2025informative}. On the evaluation side, mean Recall@K
\cite{chen2019knowledge, tang2019learning} and its independent-mean refinement
\cite{li2022rethinking} rebalance metric weight but remain point estimates and can be
gamed by suppressing head predictions. WAGER is complementary: it is an
\emph{evaluation primitive} that attributes a gain and reports an interval, rather
than a method that improves tail recall.

\paragraph{Language priors in VQA and multimodal LLMs.}
The ``blind'' solvability of VQA \cite{goyal2017making} prompted changing-prior
splits \cite{agrawal2018dont}, which were then shown to be exploitable by label
inversion \cite{teney2020value, shrestha2020negative}. The problem persists for
modern vision-language models: benchmarks such as VLind-Bench \cite{lee2025vlind} and
analyses like LanP \cite{wu2025lanp} document substantial reliance on language priors,
and recent work continues to develop debiasing methods
\cite{peng2025overcoming}. These efforts diagnose a benchmark or fix a model; WAGER
instead certifies and quantifies a \emph{gain between two systems} with a validity
guarantee, and instantiates uniformly across modalities through the choice of prior
feature $\phi$ (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

\paragraph{Long-tail recognition and detection.}
Closely related is long-tailed object detection, where rare-category average precision
is dominated by frequency priors \cite{gupta2019lvis} and recent work targets the
resulting regression and classification bias \cite{zhu2024rectify}. WAGER's
head/body/tail stratification (Section~\ref{sec:strat}) speaks directly to this
literature, but measures \emph{usable reasoning per tier} rather than re-weighting a
metric.

\paragraph{Benchmark reliability and contamination.}
A broader reliability crisis now surrounds static benchmarks: surveys and analyses
document that leaderboard gains can reflect data contamination and memorization rather
than capability, especially for multimodal models where both images and text may leak
\cite{chen2025contamination, song2025leaked, liu2025reasoning}. The principal response
has been to develop \emph{dynamic} or refreshed benchmarks. WAGER addresses a
complementary question: it leaves the benchmark fixed and asks, with a certificate, whether
a specific reported gain reflects information beyond a stated confound.

\paragraph{Usable information.}
Our reasoning channel measures \emph{usable} (not information-theoretic) information,
in the predictive, family-relative sense of $\mathcal{V}$-information
\cite{xu2020theory, ethayarajh2022understanding}, conditional probing
\cite{hewitt2021conditional, voita2020information}, and usable-information dataset
diagnostics \cite{zhang2024checklist}. WAGER's novelty is to measure this quantity
\emph{beyond a self-induced prior baseline} and to attach an anytime-valid certificate
to it.

\paragraph{Game-theoretic statistics and anytime-valid inference.}
WAGER is built on testing-by-betting and e-values
\cite{shafer2021testing, vovk2021evalues, ramdas2023game, grunwald2024safe}, the
bounded-mean capital process of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas
\cite{waudbysmith2024estimating}, and the forecaster-comparison e-process of Henzi
and Ziegel \cite{henzi2022valid}; recent work has further developed the foundations
and methodology
\cite{koning2026anytime, chugg2026evalues, polson2026bayes} and extending e-values to
applied sequential monitoring \cite{sokolova2026adaptive}. The growth-rate--information identity goes
back to Kelly \cite{kelly1956new} and the universal-portfolio view
\cite{cover1991universal, cover2006elements}. To our knowledge WAGER is the first to
cast benchmark-confound attribution as a game-theoretic e-process, yielding a single
object that both certifies (Theorem~\ref{thm:evalue}) and quantifies
(Theorem~\ref{thm:growth}) reasoning beyond annotation-frequency priors. Unlike
conditional-independence testing, which is provably hard in general
\cite{shah2020hardness}, we run an honest predictive comparison of two fixed
forecasters, which is exactly what betting certifies.
