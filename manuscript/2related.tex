\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
Prior approaches to dataset bias and shortcut learning \cite{torralba2011unbiased, geirhos2020shortcut, misra2016seeing} typically focus on mitigating biases during training or reweighting test sets. However, WAGER is the first to cast benchmark-confound attribution as a game-theoretic e-process \cite{shafer2021testing, vovk2021evalues, ramdas2023game, waudbysmith2024estimating, henzi2022valid, grunwald2024safe}. This yields an anytime-valid, distribution-free certificate, whereas all prior critiques in SGG, VQA, and LVIS provide only point estimates.

Our self-prior projection $\bar{q}_f$ serves as a new estimator. By comparing a model to its own frequency-collapsed self, rather than an external empirical baseline, the null hypothesis becomes exact without knowing the true prior, and the evaluation is provably calibration-invariant \cite{guo2017calibration}. Furthermore, the process unifies certification and quantification: the betting growth rate equals usable reasoning information beyond the prior \cite{kelly1956new, cover2006elements, cover1991universal, xu2020theory, ethayarajh2022understanding, hewitt2021conditional, voita2020information}.
