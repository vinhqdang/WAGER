\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Benchmark priors and shortcut learning}
Dataset bias has long complicated comparisons between visual recognition systems
\cite{torralba2011unbiased,geirhos2020shortcut}. In SGG, the FREQ predictor estimates
$p(\text{predicate}\mid\text{subject},\text{object})$ and is surprisingly competitive
\cite{zellers2018neural}; mean recall and causal interventions were proposed to reduce
this dependence \cite{chen2019knowledge,tang2019learning,tang2020unbiased}, although
metric pathologies remain \cite{li2022rethinking}. VQA-CP changes language priors
between train and test \cite{agrawal2018dont}, but changing-prior splits can themselves
be exploited \cite{teney2020value}. These methods alter training, data, or metrics.
WAGER instead audits a fixed pair of probabilistic predictors on a fixed test set.

\subsection{Information and predictive probing}
Usable-information analyses measure information accessible to a chosen predictor family
\cite{xu2020theory,ethayarajh2022understanding,hewitt2021conditional}. Their central
objects are model- or family-level quantities. WAGER is deliberately narrower and more
operational: it attributes the proper-score difference between two concrete frozen
models. We do not identify conditional mutual information and do not claim that a
positive residual establishes causal or compositional reasoning.

\subsection{Proper scores, randomization, and U-statistics}
Strictly proper scoring rules reward honest predictive distributions
\cite{gneiting2007proper}; WAGER uses their model-pair contrast. Permutation methods have
been used to assess classifier performance and conditional independence
\cite{ojala2010permutation,berrett2020conditional}. Conditional permutation tests
typically estimate or assume a conditional distribution when confounders are continuous
or high-dimensional. WAGER exploits a different setting: the benchmark supplies a
discrete audit cell $\phi$, and the target is not generic conditional independence but a
specific model-to-model score gain. This permits exact within-cell transport without
estimating a nuisance law. The antisymmetric residual is an order-two U-statistic in the
sense of Hoeffding \cite{hoeffding1948class}, enabling an efficient all-pairs estimator
and influence-function inference. Pairwise betting has also been used to test
exchangeability sequentially \cite{saha2024testing}; WAGER is a fixed-sample gain
decomposition and makes no sequential-validity claim. Its new element is the
gain-contrast transport identity and the resulting attribution algorithm, rather than
permutation or U-statistic theory by itself.

\subsection{Relation to classical proper-score decompositions}\label{sec:related-decomposition}
The covariance identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov-id} is a relative of the reliability
resolution--uncertainty partition of a single proper score
\cite{murphy1973vector,degroot1983comparison}, later generalized and made
conditioning-set explicit by \citet{brocker2009reliability}: conditioned on a chosen
grouping variable, a proper score splits into a calibration term and a resolution term,
and the resolution term is a covariance between the forecast and the outcome indicator
within groups. We deliberately build on this fact rather than obscure it. WAGER is not a
new proper score, and $\Delta R_c$ is best understood as a \emph{resolution
difference}, not a new quantity to be evaluated on its own. Two things distinguish WAGER
from applying the classical decomposition to $q_0$ and $q_1$ separately and subtracting
the two resolutions. First, that indirect route requires two resolution estimates, each
of which is generally biased in finite samples by the same self-prediction mechanism
formalized in Section~\ref{sec:attenuation}; the bias does not cancel under subtraction
because it scales with each model's own cell counts. WAGER instead forms the paired
gain vector $H_x(y)$ first and decomposes the \emph{contrast} in one pass, which is what
makes the exact finite-sample identity and the antisymmetric U-statistic kernel possible.
Second, the classical decomposition is a diagnostic for a single forecaster's calibration
and sharpness; it neither targets nor equips a specific pairwise comparison with
inference. WAGER supplies image-cluster-robust confidence intervals and a
cell-stratified randomization test for exactly the gain a benchmark submission claims.
The technical contribution here is narrower than a new form of score decomposition: it is
the transport construction and finite-sample identity that convert a known resolution
concept into a model-to-model attribution algorithm with an exactness and inference
guarantee that the single-model decomposition does not provide.
