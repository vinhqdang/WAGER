\section{The real-pixel study across seeds}\label{app:vgseeds}

Every head of \cref{sec:vgvisual} was retrained with five seeds, which set the
initialisation and the batch order; the training subsample of $100{,}000$ relations, the
CLIP features and the test set are the same throughout, and seed $0$ reproduces the reported
predictions exactly (largest absolute difference $0$). A fourth head,
MLP-VISGEO-S, receives the class embeddings, the twelve geometry features and the CLIP crop
features together, so that what pixels add \emph{to} the geometric proxy is measured
directly. Each row of \cref{tab:vg-seeds} uses the paper's protocol: temperatures fitted on
the same held-out half of the images, the split on the other half, and the within-cell AUC
with an image bootstrap of $200$ replicates.

The case-level advantage of pixels over geometry is stable across seeds, in contrast with
the roughly fourfold seed variation of the CIFAR-100-LT effect (\cref{sec:cifarlt}); so is
the accuracy deficit. The AUC difference has the same sign in every seed and is small against
its bootstrap interval, which is why the paper reports the covariance and not better
recognition. The combined head loses at group level almost as much as the CLIP head does
against geometry, and gains about twice its case-level part.
