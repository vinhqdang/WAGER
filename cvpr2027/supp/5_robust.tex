\section{How robust the scene-graph audit is}\label{app:robust}

Three choices in \cref{sec:sggaudit} could carry its conclusions: the random half of the test
images on which each model's temperature is fitted, the 50 predicates themselves, several of
which are near-synonyms, and the label mix of the test set. This section varies each.
Scripts: \texttt{run\_sgg\_split\_robustness.py}, \texttt{run\_predicate\_merge.py},
\texttt{run\_label\_shift.py}; tables are generated from their results files.

\subsection{Calibration halves and a shared temperature}\label{app:splits}

The confidence-matched rows of \cref{sec:sggaudit} fit each model's temperature on a random
half of the test images and split the other half. The released checkpoints come with no
validation-set outputs, and we did not rerun them on the validation split; the calibration
half shares no image with the audited half, so no relation is used both to fit a temperature
and to be scored. To see how much the result depends on which half, \cref{tab:splits} repeats
the split for 20 independent halves. The fitted temperatures move by at most $0.06$ across
halves. TDE's quadratic case-level part ranges from $-0.00131$ to $+0.00059$; its interval
excludes zero in three halves, each time on the negative side, and never on the positive side.
Under the log score it is positive in all twenty ($+0.04142$ to $+0.04761$). The control's and
IETrans's parts keep their sign, and exclude zero, in every half under both scores.

TDE and the baseline share weights, so one may prefer a single temperature for both, fitted on
the pooled calibration half. That temperature ($1.47$) lies between the two models' own optima
($0.92$ and $2.50$), so neither model is confidence-matched, and TDE's case-level part turns
negative under both scores in every half (quadratic $-0.02035$ to $-0.01738$, log $-0.09440$ to
$-0.08727$). We keep the per-model temperature as the primary protocol, because only it
compares each model at its own best confidence. Under neither protocol, in any of the 20
halves, does TDE show a positive quadratic case-level part whose interval excludes zero.

\subsection{Merging near-synonymous predicates}\label{app:merge}

A case-level part near zero could reflect predicates that no model can tell apart from the
image: \textsf{on}, \textsf{sitting on} and \textsf{standing on}; \textsf{wearing} and
\textsf{wears}; \textsf{has}, \textsf{with} and \textsf{of}. We apply two merges declared
before looking at their results. \emph{Synonym} merges eleven placement verbs into
\textsf{on}, three \textsf{in} forms, the two \textsf{wear} forms, and six possessive forms
into \textsf{has}, leaving every other predicate itself (32 classes). \emph{Coarse} collapses
the 50 predicates into three families: geometric, possessive and semantic. A model's
probability for a merged class is the sum over its members; for recall, a relation counts as a
hit for a merged class when any member would (the smallest of the members' first-hit ranks).
Temperatures are fitted on the unmerged distributions on the audit's calibration half. We also
restrict to the $166{,}940$ of $183{,}639$ relations whose subject--object box pair carries a
single annotated predicate, so that no second annotated predicate competes with the label.

\Cref{tab:merge} reports the result. No conclusion of \cref{sec:sggaudit} depends on the
predicate vocabulary. TDE's matched case-level part stays null under both merges ($+0.00059$,
interval $[-0.00050,+0.00168]$; $-0.00015$, $[-0.00095,+0.00065]$) and on single-annotation
pairs, and the interval of its AUC change covers zero under both. IETrans's stays negative and
excludes zero under every merge ($-0.01540$ synonym, $-0.00761$ coarse); under the coarse merge
its mean-recall case-level part also turns significantly negative ($-0.0063$). The
logit-adjusted control keeps a positive matched case-level part throughout. Summing
probabilities over a merged class can reorder the merged classes within a cell, so under a
merge the control's AUC is no longer fixed by construction; it moves by at most $0.0012$.

\subsection{A label-shifted test}\label{app:labelshift}

\Cref{sec:method} writes a cell's case-level part as $\dR_c=\sum_{y<z}p(y)p(z)\,D(y,z)$, where
$D(y,z)$ depends only on the class-conditional behaviour of the gain. A label shift that leaves
$P(X\mid Y,C)$ fixed changes the weights $p(y)p(z)$ and no $D$, while the group-level part
depends on the cell's label frequencies directly. The prediction is that group-level parts
move under such a shift, and a case-level part keeps its sign unless its $D(y,z)$ have mixed
signs. We test it without retraining anything by reweighting the test relations: relation
$i$ in cell $c$ gets weight $w_i=1/(L_c\,\hat p_c(y_i))$, with $L_c$ the number of predicates
present in the cell and $\hat p_c$ their frequencies, so that every predicate present in a
cell carries equal total weight. This is the test set a deployment with uniform within-pair
predicate frequencies would see. The split is recomputed with pairs $(i,j)$ of a cell weighted
by $w_iw_j$, so that a gain constant within a cell still has a case-level part of exactly zero
and unit weights give the estimator of \cref{sec:method}; the within-cell AUC is recomputed
with the same comparison weights. Temperatures and calibration halves are those of
\cref{sec:sggaudit,sec:vgvisual}; intervals are image bootstraps (100 replicates).

\Cref{tab:labelshift} gives the result for the eight comparisons of the two audits. The
group-level part changes sign in seven of them; in the eighth, the class-pair model against
FREQ, it shrinks from $-0.01412$ to $-0.00180$. With the group-level parts, the totals reverse
too: the CLIP model, last on the benchmark by every aggregate, beats geometry by $+0.02478$
under uniform labels. Of the six case-level parts whose interval excludes zero on the
benchmark, five keep their sign and grow in size: geometry over the class-pair model
($+0.00882\to+0.01152$), CLIP over geometry ($+0.00472\to+0.00619$), IETrans over the baseline
($-0.01623\to-0.01830$). The exception is TDE against the logit-adjusted control, whose
case-level part turns from $-0.00398$ to $+0.00723$; and TDE's null against the baseline
becomes $+0.01430$, interval $[+0.01144,+0.01632]$. Both exceptions involve TDE, and both
mean that TDE's $D(y,z)$ have mixed signs: it gains within-cell discrimination between some
pairs of predicates and loses it between others, and the benchmark's label mix weights the
two to a null. The threshold-free AUC does not separate TDE from the baseline at either mix:
$+0.0026$ on the benchmark, $-0.0183$, interval $[-0.0684,+0.0082]$, under uniform labels.
What \cref{sec:sggaudit} says about TDE's case-level part therefore holds at the benchmark's
label mix; under a different mix the same predictions carry a case-level part, of a sign the
split computes but cannot know in advance.

\subsection{Zero-shot CLIP on the canonical split}\label{app:zeroshot}

The CLIP model of \cref{sec:vgvisual} trains a head on Visual Genome labels. To audit the
model as the audience uses it, we score the canonical test relations of \cref{sec:sggaudit}
zero-shot: frozen CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021learning} encodes the union box of subject
and object and is compared with one prompt per predicate, ``a photo of a \{subject\}
\{predicate\} a \{object\}'' (\texttt{colab\_clip\_zeroshot.py}). The map from dataset index
to image file follows the codebase's loader, and every image's size matches the one stored
with the audit. Nothing is trained, so the model has no predicate prior of its own. FREQ is
the MOTIFS frequency branch, constant within a cell up to 31 relations whose scored pair is a
duplicate box with another label (hence its AUC of $0.5002$). Against FREQ a model's
case-level part is its own within-cell information. ``Zero-shot CLIP + FREQ'' multiplies the
two distributions and renormalises, which leaves the within-cell AUC unchanged. Protocol as in
\cref{sec:sggaudit}: per-model temperature on the calibration half, split on the other.

Zero-shot CLIP ranks the correct predicate first for $2.5\%$ of relations, and its group-level
loss against FREQ is $-0.51900$. It does carry within-cell information: its AUC is $0.5164$,
above the $0.5$ of any model constant within cells, and its case-level part over FREQ is
$+0.00046$, interval $[+0.00041,+0.00051]$. But this is a small fraction of what MOTIFS learns
from Visual Genome: MOTIFS's AUC is $0.5705$, the difference $-0.0542$, interval
$[-0.0655,-0.0423]$, and its case-level part over FREQ is $+0.02943$. Given the training prior,
zero-shot CLIP reaches $0.6289$ top-1, but its case-level part over FREQ ($+0.00989$) is a third
of MOTIFS's. The vision--language model the audience uses does not, zero-shot, rank predicates
within an object pair the way a trained scene-graph model does, in line with evidence that such
models often treat captions as bags of words~\cite{yuksekgonul2023when}. TDE's case-level part
over FREQ, $+0.02937$, equals MOTIFS's to the third decimal, as the null of \cref{sec:sggaudit}
implies.
