\section{How robust the scene-graph audit is}\label{app:robust}

Three choices in \cref{sec:sggaudit} could carry its conclusions: the random half of the test
images on which each model's temperature is fitted, the 50 predicates themselves, several of
which are near-synonyms, and the label mix of the test set. This section varies each.
Scripts: \texttt{run\_sgg\_split\_robustness.py}, \texttt{run\_predicate\_merge.py},
\texttt{run\_label\_shift.py}; tables are generated from their results files.

\subsection{Calibration halves and a shared temperature}\label{app:splits}

The confidence-matched rows of \cref{sec:sggaudit} fit each model's temperature on a random
half of the test images and split the other half. The released checkpoints come with no
validation-set outputs, and we did not rerun them on the validation split; the calibration
half shares no image with the audited half, so no relation is used both to fit a temperature
and to be scored. To see how much the result depends on which half, \cref{tab:splits} repeats
the split for 20 independent halves. The fitted temperatures move by at most $0.06$ across
halves. TDE's quadratic case-level part ranges from $-0.00131$ to $+0.00059$; its interval
excludes zero in three halves, each time on the negative side, and never on the positive side.
Under the log score it is positive in all twenty ($+0.04142$ to $+0.04761$). The control's and
IETrans's parts keep their sign, and exclude zero, in every half under both scores.

TDE and the baseline share weights, so one may prefer a single temperature for both, fitted on
the pooled calibration half. That temperature ($1.47$) lies between the two models' own optima
($0.92$ and $2.50$), so neither model is confidence-matched, and TDE's case-level part turns
negative under both scores in every half (quadratic $-0.02035$ to $-0.01738$, log $-0.09440$ to
$-0.08727$). We keep the per-model temperature as the primary protocol, because only it
compares each model at its own best confidence. Under neither protocol, in any of the 20
halves, does TDE show a positive quadratic case-level part whose interval excludes zero.

\subsection{Merging near-synonymous predicates}\label{app:merge}

A case-level part near zero could reflect predicates that no model can tell apart from the
image: \textsf{on}, \textsf{sitting on} and \textsf{standing on}; \textsf{wearing} and
\textsf{wears}; \textsf{has}, \textsf{with} and \textsf{of}. We apply two merges declared
before looking at their results. \emph{Synonym} merges eleven placement verbs into
\textsf{on}, three \textsf{in} forms, the two \textsf{wear} forms, and six possessive forms
into \textsf{has}, leaving every other predicate itself (32 classes). \emph{Coarse} collapses
the 50 predicates into three families: geometric, possessive and semantic. A model's
probability for a merged class is the sum over its members; for recall, a relation counts as a
hit for a merged class when any member would (the smallest of the members' first-hit ranks).
Temperatures are fitted on the unmerged distributions on the audit's calibration half. We also
restrict to the $166{,}940$ of $183{,}639$ relations whose subject--object box pair carries a
single annotated predicate, so that no second annotated predicate competes with the label.

\Cref{tab:merge} reports the result. No conclusion of \cref{sec:sggaudit} depends on the
predicate vocabulary. TDE's matched case-level part stays null under both merges ($+0.00059$,
interval $[-0.00050,+0.00168]$; $-0.00015$, $[-0.00095,+0.00065]$) and on single-annotation
pairs, and the interval of its AUC change covers zero under both. IETrans's stays negative and
excludes zero under every merge ($-0.01540$ synonym, $-0.00761$ coarse); under the coarse merge
its mean-recall case-level part also turns significantly negative ($-0.0063$). The
logit-adjusted control keeps a positive matched case-level part throughout. Summing
probabilities over a merged class can reorder the merged classes within a cell, so under a
merge the control's AUC is no longer fixed by construction; it moves by at most $0.0012$.

Could the benchmark leave little room for any case-level gain? One kind of ceiling is
measurable: two relations with the same image and the same subject and object boxes but
different predicates receive the same prediction from every model that sees the image and the
boxes, so every comparison between them is a tie. They carry $0.07\%$ of the AUC's comparison
weight ($0.64\%$ weighted as the split weights cells), which caps the within-cell AUC at
$0.9996$ (\texttt{sgg\_auc\_ceiling.py}). MOTIFS's $0.5705$ is far below that; how much of
the gap is annotator style rather than missing recognition, the data cannot say.

\subsection{A label-shifted test}\label{app:labelshift}

\Cref{sec:method} writes a cell's case-level part as $\dR_c=\sum_{y<z}p(y)p(z)\,D(y,z)$, where
$D(y,z)$ depends only on the class-conditional behaviour of the gain. A label shift that leaves
$P(X\mid Y,C)$ fixed changes the weights $p(y)p(z)$ and no $D$, while the group-level part
depends on the cell's label frequencies directly. So a case-level part keeps its sign under
every such shift if its $D(y,z)$ share one sign, and can change sign otherwise; that much
follows from the identity, and the reweighted estimator below inherits it. What the data
decide is which comparisons have $D(y,z)$ of mixed sign, and how far the group-level parts
move. This is a sensitivity analysis, not an independent test.

Relation $i$ in cell $c$ gets weight $w_i=1/(L_c\,\hat p_c(y_i))$, with $L_c$ the number of
predicates present in the cell and $\hat p_c$ their frequencies, so that every predicate present
in a cell carries equal total weight; the path $w_i^\alpha$, $\alpha\in\{0,\tfrac14,\tfrac12,
\tfrac34,1\}$, moves from the benchmark's mix to that uniform one. The split is recomputed
with pairs $(i,j)$ of a cell weighted by $w_iw_j$, so a gain constant within a cell still has a
case-level part of exactly zero and unit weights give the estimator of \cref{sec:method}; the
within-cell AUC is recomputed with the same comparison weights. Temperatures are those fitted
at the benchmark's mix, on the calibration halves of \cref{sec:sggaudit,sec:vgvisual}, each
study on its own temperature grid; they are not refitted under the shift. Intervals are a
delete-a-group jackknife over images (100 groups). An image bootstrap biases the case-level
part towards zero here, because a resampled image's same-label pairs have kernel zero and
receive weight $\mathbb E[w^2]=2$. At the uniform end the weights are very unequal: the
Kish effective sample size of the audit half falls from $92{,}027$ to $7{,}296$.

\Cref{tab:labelshift} gives the result for the eight comparisons of the two audits. The
group-level part changes sign in seven of them; in the eighth, the class-pair model against
FREQ, it shrinks from $-0.01432$ to $-0.00241$. With the group-level parts, the totals reverse
too: the CLIP model, last on the benchmark by every aggregate, beats geometry by $+0.02981$
under uniform labels. Of the six case-level parts whose interval excludes zero on the
benchmark, five keep their sign and grow in size: geometry over the class-pair model
($+0.00891\to+0.01157$), CLIP over geometry ($+0.00464\to+0.00615$), IETrans+Rwt over the
baseline ($-0.01623\to-0.01830$). The exception is TDE against the logit-adjusted control,
whose case-level part turns from $-0.00398$ to $+0.00723$; and TDE's null against the
baseline becomes $+0.01430$, interval $[+0.01116,+0.01744]$. Along the path
(\cref{tab:labelshiftpath}) TDE's part rises monotonically and already excludes zero at
$\alpha=\tfrac14$ ($+0.00238$, $[+0.00092,+0.00385]$): its null holds at the benchmark's exact
mix and nowhere near it. Both exceptions mean that TDE's $D(y,z)$ have mixed signs: it gains
within-cell discrimination between some pairs of predicates and loses it between others,
consistent with the tier pattern of \cref{app:rankrob}. The threshold-free AUC does not
separate TDE from the baseline at either end: $+0.0026$ on the benchmark, $-0.0183$, interval
$[-0.0684,+0.0082]$, under uniform labels.

\subsection{How the case-level verdicts depend on the measure}\label{app:rankrob}

\paragraph{AUC weighting.} The within-cell AUC of \cref{sec:method} averages the AUCs of each
cell's label pairs with weight $n_c(y)n_c(z)$, the number of comparisons. Across cells this
weights a cell by about $n_c^2$: the largest cell carries $27\%$ of the weight, against $2.5\%$
of the relations. Weighting by $n_c(y)n_c(z)/n_c$ instead combines cells as the case-level
part does. \Cref{tab:rankweighting} gives both, on all identified relations and on the audit
half on which the matched split is computed. TDE's change covers zero under all four. The
logit-adjusted control's moves by float32 rounding only. IETrans+Rwt's loss excludes zero
under the split's weighting ($-0.0235$, $[-0.0275,-0.0182]$) though not by comparison. CLIP
against geometry is the case the weighting decides: by comparison it favours CLIP on the audit
half ($+0.0214$, $[+0.0001,+0.0435]$), by relation it favours geometry on all relations
($-0.0067$, $[-0.0120,-0.0019]$), and the other two estimates cover zero.

\paragraph{Where TDE's discrimination moved.} \Cref{tab:tierpair} restricts the
comparison-weighted AUC change to label pairs by predicate tier. $65\%$ of the weight sits on
head--head pairs, where TDE's change is $+0.0068$, $[-0.0003,+0.0134]$; on all other pairs it is
$-0.0051$, $[-0.0130,+0.0026]$. Neither excludes zero, so we read it as a pattern consistent with
the label-shift result rather than as a finding. IETrans+Rwt's loss is concentrated on
head--body ($-0.0250$) and body--body ($-0.0260$) pairs, both excluding zero.

\paragraph{Recalibration family.} \Cref{tab:recalibration} repeats the matched split under
three families, each fitted on the calibration half and each preserving every model's
within-cell ranking: one temperature per model (the protocol of \cref{sec:sggaudit}), one
temperature shared by the two models, and a temperature with a per-class bias per model. The
third absorbs any global shift of the log-prior, so the logit-adjusted control becomes the
baseline exactly (case-level part $0.00000$), which the first cannot achieve ($+0.00392$): the
simulation of \cref{sec:simulation} shows the same for a synthetic class-wise offset, which a
temperature leaves at $+0.0106$ and the third family removes. Under it TDE's case-level part
is $+0.00445$, $[+0.00354,+0.00536]$ (quadratic) and $+0.02543$ (log), while IETrans+Rwt's loss
is unchanged ($-0.01679$, $-0.06609$). IETrans+Rwt's case-level loss is negative under all six
protocol-score combinations; TDE's sign is not.

\subsection{Zero-shot CLIP on the canonical split}\label{app:zeroshot}

The CLIP model of \cref{sec:vgvisual} trains a head on Visual Genome labels. As a first look
at a vision--language model used with no training at all, we score the canonical test relations of \cref{sec:sggaudit}
zero-shot: frozen CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021learning} encodes the union box of subject
and object and is compared with one prompt per predicate, ``a photo of a \{subject\}
\{predicate\} a \{object\}'' (\texttt{colab\_clip\_zeroshot.py}). The map from dataset index
to image file follows the codebase's loader, and every image's size matches the one stored
with the audit. Nothing is trained, so the model has no predicate prior of its own. FREQ is
the MOTIFS frequency branch, constant within a cell up to 31 relations whose scored pair is a
duplicate box with another label (hence its AUC of $0.5002$). Against FREQ a model's
case-level part is its own within-cell information. ``Zero-shot CLIP + FREQ'' multiplies the
two distributions and renormalises, which leaves the within-cell AUC unchanged. Protocol as in
\cref{sec:sggaudit}: per-model temperature on the calibration half, split on the other.

Zero-shot CLIP ranks the correct predicate first for $2.5\%$ of relations, and its group-level
loss against FREQ is $-0.51900$. It does carry within-cell information: its AUC is $0.5164$,
above the $0.5$ of any model constant within cells, and its case-level part over FREQ is
$+0.00046$, interval $[+0.00041,+0.00051]$. But this is a small fraction of what MOTIFS learns
from Visual Genome: MOTIFS's AUC is $0.5705$, the difference $-0.0542$, interval
$[-0.0655,-0.0423]$, and its case-level part over FREQ is $+0.02943$. Given the training prior,
zero-shot CLIP reaches $0.6289$ top-1, but its case-level part over FREQ ($+0.00989$) is a third
of MOTIFS's. Frozen ViT-B/32 with one prompt per predicate is a weak baseline, at $2.5\%$ top-1 close to
chance, so this bounds what such a model carries only from below: a larger model, prompt
ensembles or region-level features may carry more, and we draw no general conclusion about
vision--language models from it. What it shows is that the split separates a model with no
predicate prior and little within-pair information from one with both, in line with evidence
that such models often treat captions as bags of words~\cite{yuksekgonul2023when}. TDE's case-level part
over FREQ, $+0.02937$, equals MOTIFS's to the third decimal, as the null of \cref{sec:sggaudit}
implies.
