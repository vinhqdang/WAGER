\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Scene graph generation is evaluated on a benchmark whose labels are unusually predictable
from something every model sees. Given the ordered object pair (\textsf{man},
\textsf{surfboard}), the predicate on Visual Genome~\cite{krishna2017visual} is very often
\textsf{riding}, and a lookup table of training frequencies is famously
competitive~\cite{zellers2018neural}. Recall@$K$ rewards models that echo this prior, so the
field now leads with mean recall, which averages recall over predicates and so rewards getting
the rare ones~\cite{chen2019kern,tang2019vctree}. Debiasing methods are now judged by how far they raise it.
Total Direct Effect (TDE)~\cite{tang2020unbiased}, among the most cited of them, raises
mean recall@50 of a MOTIFS model from $14.6$ to $24.8$ on the canonical VG150 split, in our
evaluation of the released checkpoint.

What did those ten points buy? A model can move probability towards rare predicates
\emph{within each subject--object pair} --- say \textsf{carrying} more often for
(\textsf{man}, \textsf{surfboard}) --- without becoming any better at deciding \emph{which}
relations are a man carrying rather than riding. Or it can learn to make that decision. The
metric credits both alike. Existing remedies --- head/body/tail-stratified
recall~\cite{li2021bgnn}, independent mean recall~\cite{li2022rethinking}, zero-shot
recall~\cite{lu2016visual}, rarity-stratified accuracy~\cite{kervadec2021roses} --- slice one
model's performance differently; none attributes the gain \emph{between} two models to one
route or the other.

\paragraph{An exact split.} Take two frozen models and a test set grouped by a discrete
feature both observe, here the subject--object pair (\cref{fig:concept}). Score each model's
prediction for one relation against the label of a \emph{different} relation in the same
group. This keeps each group's predicate frequencies, so gain earned at the level of the group
survives it, and breaks the link between a relation and its own label, so gain earned on
individual cases does not. The observed gain splits exactly into a \emph{group-level} part
$\hdP$ and a \emph{case-level} remainder $\hdR$, a within-group covariance between the change
in the predictions and the label (\cref{thm:population}), for any proper score and for mean
recall itself, with no fitted reference model, in $O(NK)$ on cached predictions, with
image-clustered intervals.

\paragraph{What it finds.} We re-ran TDE's released checkpoint, replayed the official
evaluator exactly so that recall can be computed under any labelling, and split mean recall
itself (\cref{sec:sggaudit}). Of the ten points, $87\%$ is group-level; the fifteen rarest
predicates contribute almost nothing. A thresholded metric also earns case-level credit
whenever its operating point moves, so we read the remainder against a control: subtracting
the training predicate prior from the baseline's logits~\cite{menon2021long}, which changes
no ranking within any group, recovers nine of the ten points with the same split at under a
tenth of TDE's cost in $R@50$, and against it TDE's case-level part is $-0.0008$, interval
straddling zero. A within-group AUC detects no change in TDE's ranking, and its
proper-score case-level part is small and changes sign with the calibration protocol and the
label mix. IETrans~\cite{zhang2022finegrained}, which relabels training data within object
pairs, gains 21 points, $97\%$ group-level, and loses within-group discrimination under every
measure. The converse appears in a controlled study on real pixels (\cref{sec:vgvisual}): a
model reading frozen CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} crops, which every aggregate ranks
last, carries $+0.00464$ more case-level covariance than the geometry model ranked above it,
without ranking better.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) Audits of two released debiasing checkpoints that split their
mean-recall gains exactly, with the mechanism behind TDE's. (ii) An exact, model-free
decomposition of the difference between two frozen models, for every Bregman score and for
label-weighted hit rates such as mean recall, with cluster-robust inference, an
operating-point control and a threshold-free AUC. (iii) Validation that the case-level part
is zero when it should be, covers at the nominal rate under image clustering, and separates
calibration, prior and shortcut effects, with protocols for the confounds it does not.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} Every scene-graph result is PredCls, where object boxes
and classes are given and every model sees the cell; in SGCls and SGDet the cell is itself
predicted, and the split as stated does not apply. The split is conditional on the declared grouping: $\hdR$
measures case-level discrimination \emph{among relations sharing an object pair}, and
credits any signal varying inside a pair, including a shortcut nobody recorded. It is not
invariant to recalibration --- rescaling a model's confidence moves score between the two
parts while changing no ranking --- which is why the comparisons whose models differ visibly in
confidence, TDE's and the CLIP model's, are read confidence-matched.
For mean recall the split is of the micro-averaged version, which pools relations
across images; it tracks the official per-image average but is not it. And a proper-score
case-level part near zero can depend on the calibration protocol and on the label mix;
\cref{app:recall,app:robust} report every configuration. These limits are taken up
in \cref{sec:discussion}.
