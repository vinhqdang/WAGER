\section{The model the aggregates rank last}\label{sec:vgvisual}

The predictors of \cref{sec:data} never see an image: they read the class pair and, at most,
box coordinates from the annotation. On the same reconstructed split we add one that reads pixels --- subject and object
regions cropped from the image and encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021learning},
concatenated with the class embeddings MLP-CLASS uses. CLIP is not fine-tuned, so any
case-level gain reflects what pretraining had already recovered. All three compared models
are retrained on one fixed subsample of $100{,}000$ training relations, so differences isolate
the features rather than the amount of data.

By every aggregate measure the CLIP model is the worst of the three: top-1 accuracy $0.6156$
against $0.6467$ for the geometry model, and a quadratic score $0.04642$ lower. An evaluator
reading either number would discard it. The split disagrees. Matched under the primary
protocol, the CLIP model \emph{gains} $0.00331$ of case-level score against the geometry
model, interval $[+0.00185,+0.00477]$ ($+0.00464$, $[+0.00311,+0.00618]$, by temperature
alone; on raw outputs $0.00640$, $[0.00513,0.00767]$), while losing $0.04705$ of group-level
score under temperature matching. Its whole deficit is group-level (\cref{fig:where}). Against the class-only model, pixels buy $0.01663$
of case-level score on raw outputs where box geometry buys $0.01023$, with well-separated
intervals.

A larger case-level part is not the same as better ranking, and here the two disagree. With
each model's prior matched within cells, the CLIP model still ranks the correct predicate
first less often (top-1 accuracy $2.17$ points lower, MRR $1.67$ lower), and under the primary
weighting the within-cell AUC favours geometry, $-0.0067$, $[-0.0113,-0.0015]$; by comparison
the sign reverses, $+0.0085$, $[-0.0062,+0.0237]$. Over five training seeds
(\cref{app:vgseeds}) the CLIP model's temperature-matched advantage stays between $+0.00413$ and
$+0.00474$, its accuracy below geometry's.

\paragraph{Under a label shift.} A label shift moves group-level parts and only reweights
case-level ones (\cref{sec:method}). Along a path to uniform predicate frequencies within each
pair (\cref{app:labelshift}), seven of the eight group-level parts of \cref{sec:sggaudit} and
this section change sign, and the CLIP model beats geometry by $+0.02981$. Five of the six
case-level parts that excluded zero keep their sign and grow; TDE's rises from $-0.00006$ to
$+0.01430$, $[+0.01116,+0.01744]$, while the AUC separates it from the baseline at neither end:
TDE gains discrimination between some predicate pairs and loses it between others.

\paragraph{Beyond scene graphs.}\label{sec:further} The supplement applies the split to
CIFAR-100-LT, long-tailed text and Waterbirds (cells: background scenes); there the fine class
label cannot serve as a cell, since it forces the case-level part to zero.
