\section{Does the split measure what it claims?}\label{sec:exp}

A released checkpoint cannot validate the split, because what the model can see is unknown.
We validate it where the answer is known, first in simulation and then on Visual Genome
predictors whose information content we control. All intervals are 95\%.

\paragraph{Simulation.}\label{sec:simulation} With 24 cells over five labels, an old model
equal to the cell prior and a new one interpolating towards an oracle, the estimated
case-level gain grows linearly with the injected signal; under a within-cell null the
randomization test rejects in $0.030$ of 200 datasets at level $0.05$. With relations
clustered into images by a shared per-image effect and Zipf-distributed cell sizes, the
image-clustered interval covers in $94.0\%$ of 500 runs, against $88.6\%$ for one that treats
relations as independent; with VG150's own cell sizes it covers $96.3\%$, and so do the
mean-recall and share intervals, and only when every cell holds two cases does it fall
short, to $81.5\%$ (\cref{app:clt}).

Three designs check that the two parts separate what they claim to. A \emph{prior-only}
improvement, where the new model corrects a noisy estimate of the cell prior, gives a
case-level gain centred at zero ($0.0002$, sd $0.0035$). An \emph{unrecorded within-cell
shortcut} --- a binary feature varying inside each cell that the new model uses --- is
credited to the case-level part ($+0.041$, sd $0.004$): the split measures all within-cell
signal relative to the declared grouping, shortcut or not. And a \emph{calibration-only}
change, where the new model is a temperature-softened copy of an overconfident one carrying
identical case-level information, is credited a large spurious case-level \emph{loss} of
$-0.488$ on raw outputs, which the confidence-matching protocol of \cref{sec:matching}
reduces to $-0.0003$ (sd $0.0017$). A \emph{class-wise shift of the operating point}, a
fixed logit offset per label, changes no within-cell ranking yet survives temperature
matching ($+0.0106$ for a logit-adjustment offset) and vanishes under a temperature with a
per-class bias ($0.0000$, sd $0.0003$; \cref{app:rankrob}): the criterion of
\cref{sec:matching} at work.

\paragraph{Controlled predictors on VG150.}\label{sec:data} We train predicate classifiers
whose inputs we control on a VG150-style split reconstructed from the released annotations
(same vocabulary, images divided 70/30 between fitting and audit; \cref{app:vgmain-full}),
not the canonical split of \cref{sec:sggaudit}: a frequency table on the class pair (FREQ),
an MLP on class embeddings (MLP-CLASS), and MLPs adding twelve box-geometry features
(MLP-SPATIAL, MLP-SPATIAL+). The audit covers $227{,}337$ of $229{,}605$ test relations
($99.0\%$) in $6{,}346$ class-pair cells.

MLP-CLASS beats FREQ by $0.03386$ of quadratic score and the split assigns \emph{all} of it
to the group-level part: the case-level gain is $0.00000$ with a zero-width interval, an
algebraic zero (both models are functions of the class pair, so \cref{thm:population} forces
it) and the sharpest check that nothing is credited to case-level evidence when none exists.
Adding box geometry yields a case-level gain of $0.01439$ (CI $[0.01373,0.01504]$, $p=.002$)
that no aggregate isolates; against MLP-CLASS the total gain of $0.00885$ even
\emph{understates} it, because the group-level part falls by $0.00554$.
