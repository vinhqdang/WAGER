\section{Does the split measure what it claims?}\label{sec:exp}

A released checkpoint cannot validate the split, because what the model can see is unknown.
We validate it where the answer is known, first in simulation and then on Visual Genome
predictors whose information content we control. All intervals are 95\%.

\paragraph{Simulation.}\label{sec:simulation} With 24 cells over five labels and a new model
interpolating from the cell prior towards an oracle, the case-level gain grows linearly with
the injected signal, and under a within-cell null the randomization test rejects in $0.030$
of 200 datasets at level $0.05$. With relations clustered into images, the image-clustered
interval covers in $94.0\%$ of 500 runs ($88.6\%$ treating relations as independent); with
VG150's own cell sizes it covers $96.3\%$, as do the mean-recall and share intervals, and only
with two cases in every cell does it fall to $81.5\%$ (\cref{app:clt}).
A \emph{prior-only} improvement gives a case-level gain centred at zero ($0.0002$, sd
$0.0035$). An \emph{unrecorded within-cell shortcut} the new model uses is credited to the
case-level part ($+0.041$, sd $0.004$): the split measures all within-cell signal, shortcut
or not. A \emph{calibration-only} change, a temperature-softened copy of an overconfident
model, is credited a spurious case-level \emph{loss} of $-0.488$ on raw outputs, which
confidence matching reduces to $-0.0003$ (sd $0.0017$). A fixed logit offset per label
changes no within-cell ranking yet survives temperature matching ($+0.0106$) and vanishes
under a temperature with a per-class bias ($0.0000$, sd $0.0003$; \cref{app:rankrob}): the
criterion of \cref{sec:matching} at work.

\paragraph{Controlled predictors on VG150.}\label{sec:data} We train predicate classifiers
whose inputs we control on a VG150-style split reconstructed from the released annotations
(same vocabulary, images divided 70/30 between fitting and audit; \cref{app:vgmain-full}),
not the canonical split of \cref{sec:sggaudit}: a frequency table on the class pair (FREQ),
an MLP on class embeddings (MLP-CLASS), and MLPs adding twelve box-geometry features
(MLP-SPATIAL, MLP-SPATIAL+). The audit covers $227{,}337$ of $229{,}605$ test relations
($99.0\%$) in $6{,}346$ class-pair cells.

MLP-CLASS beats FREQ by $0.03386$ of quadratic score, all group-level: the case-level gain is
$0.00000$ with a zero-width interval, as \cref{thm:population} forces for two functions of the
class pair.
Adding box geometry yields a case-level gain of $0.01439$ (CI $[0.01373,0.01504]$, $p=.002$)
that no aggregate isolates; against MLP-CLASS the total gain of $0.00885$ even
\emph{understates} it, because the group-level part falls by $0.00554$.
