\begin{abstract}
Debiasing methods for scene graph generation are judged by mean recall, and one of the best
known, Total Direct Effect (TDE), raises it on Visual Genome from 14.6 to 24.8. What did
those ten points buy? A model raises mean recall either by predicting rare predicates more
often within each subject--object pair, or by telling which individual relations carry
them, and the metric credits both alike. Scoring each prediction against the label of
another relation from the same pair keeps the pair's predicate frequencies but breaks the
link between each relation and its own label. This splits the difference between two frozen
models, in a proper score or in mean recall itself, exactly into a group-level part and a
case-level covariance, in $O(NK)$ time with image-clustered intervals. On TDE's released
checkpoint, 87\% of the mean-recall gain (interval 83--91\%) is group-level, and a logit
adjustment that changes no ranking within any pair recovers nine of the ten points at under
a tenth of TDE's cost in recall. No measure detects a change in TDE's ranking within pairs,
and its small case-level part changes sign with the calibration protocol and the label mix.
A second released method, IETrans, gains 21 points, 97\% group-level, and loses
within-pair discrimination under every measure.
\end{abstract}
