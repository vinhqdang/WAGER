\begin{abstract}
Debiasing methods for scene graph generation are judged by mean recall, and one of the best
known, Total Direct Effect (TDE), raises it in Visual Genome PredCls from 14.6 to 24.8. What
did those ten points buy? A model raises mean recall either by predicting rare predicates more
often within each subject--object pair, or by telling which individual relations carry them,
and the metric credits both alike. Scoring each prediction against the label of another
relation from the same pair keeps the pair's predicate frequencies but breaks the link between
each relation and its own label. This splits the difference between two frozen models, in a
proper score or in mean recall itself, exactly into a group-level part and a within-pair
covariance, in $O(NK)$ time with image-clustered intervals. We audit three released
Neural-Motifs PredCls checkpoints of prior-correcting methods, one training run each. With
subject--object class pairs as cells, 87\% of TDE's mean-recall@50 gain (graph-constrained,
micro-pooled; interval 83--91\%) is group-level, 84\% of the official per-image statistic; with
subject classes alone as cells it is 18\%. At $K=50$ with the graph constraint, a logit
adjustment that changes no ranking within any pair recovers $+0.094$ of TDE's $+0.097$; at
other cut-offs, or without the constraint, the two differ by $0.02$ to $0.15$. No
weighting of the within-pair AUC shows TDE gaining discrimination: it excludes a change larger
than $0.0038$ with cells weighted as in the split, $0.0077$ with every comparison weighted once
and $0.028$ with every predicate weighted equally, and with every predicate pair weighted
equally it falls by $0.015$. DLFE,
against its own released baseline, gains 11 points, 94\% group-level, its ranking
gaining $0.003$ under one weighting; IETrans, against a separately trained baseline, gains 21 points, 97\%
group-level. SGCls and SGDet replications of TDE agree.
\end{abstract}
