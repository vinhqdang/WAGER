\begin{abstract}
Debiasing methods for scene graph generation are judged by mean recall, and one of the best
known, Total Direct Effect (TDE), raises it on Visual Genome from 14.6 to 24.8. What did
those ten points buy? A model raises mean recall either by predicting rare predicates more
often within each subject--object pair, or by telling which individual relations carry
them, and the metric credits both alike. Scoring each prediction against the label of
another relation from the same pair keeps the pair's predicate frequencies but breaks the
link between each relation and its own label. This splits the difference between two frozen
models, in a proper score or in mean recall itself, exactly into a group-level part and a
case-level covariance, in $O(NK)$ time with image-clustered intervals. We audit two released
Neural-Motifs checkpoints in PredCls. For TDE, 87\% of the mean-recall@50 gain (graph-constrained,
micro-pooled; interval 83--91\%) is group-level when cells are subject--object class pairs, and
a logit adjustment that changes no ranking within any pair recovers $+0.094$ of its $+0.097$ at
under a tenth of TDE's cost in recall. The within-pair AUC excludes a change in TDE's ranking
larger than $0.0034$ with cells weighted as in the split, but only one larger than $0.0077$ when
every comparison counts once, and TDE's case-level part grows when the label mix is made
uniform within pairs. IETrans, against a separately trained baseline, gains 21 points, 97\%
group-level, and its within-pair ranking falls. SGCls and SGDet replications of TDE agree.
\end{abstract}
