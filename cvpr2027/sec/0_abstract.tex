\begin{abstract}
Debiasing methods for scene graph generation are judged by mean recall; Total Direct Effect
(TDE) raises it in Visual Genome PredCls from 14.6 to 24.8. A model raises mean recall either by
predicting rare predicates more often within each subject--object pair, or by telling which
relations carry them, and the metric credits both alike. Scoring each prediction against the
label of another relation from the same pair breaks the link between a relation and its own
label but keeps the pair's label frequencies; this splits the difference between two frozen
models, in a proper score or in mean recall, exactly into a group-level part and a within-pair
covariance, with image-clustered intervals. We audit four released PredCls checkpoints, one run
each. With class pairs as cells, 87\% of TDE's mean-recall@50 gain (graph-constrained,
micro-pooled; interval 83--91\%) is group-level; the share is 84\% for the official statistic and
18\% with subject classes as cells. At $K=50$ with the graph constraint a logit adjustment that
changes no within-pair ranking recovers $+0.094$ of TDE's $+0.097$; at other cut-offs, or
without the constraint, the two differ by $0.02$ to $0.15$. TDE's within-pair AUC shows no gain:
four weightings exclude changes above $0.0038$ to $0.028$, tightly only under the two that favour
head predicates. DLFE (a prior correction) and IETrans (relabelling and reweighting) gain
$+0.111$ and $+0.220$, $94\%$ and $97\%$ group-level. PE-Net, a prototype-based model, gains
$+0.170$, $86\%$ group-level; its case-level part, $+0.024$, exceeds the $+0.001$ between two
separately trained baselines, but its AUC change, $+0.005$, does not exceed their $0.006$ spread,
and without relation-level suppression its mean recall falls from $0.314$ to $0.296$.
\end{abstract}
