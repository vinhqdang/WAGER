\begin{abstract}
Debiasing methods for scene graph generation are judged by mean recall; Total Direct Effect
(TDE) raises it in Visual Genome PredCls from 14.6 to 24.8. A model raises mean recall either by
predicting rare predicates more often within each subject--object pair, or by telling which
relations carry them, and the metric credits both alike. Scoring each prediction against the
label of another relation from the same pair breaks the link between a relation and its own
label but keeps the pair's label frequencies; this splits the difference between two frozen
models, in a proper score or in mean recall, exactly into a group-level part and a within-pair
covariance, with image-clustered intervals. On four released PredCls checkpoints, one run each,
with class pairs as cells, 87\% of TDE's mean-recall@50 gain (interval 83--91\%; 84\% of the
official statistic; 18\% with subject classes as cells) is group-level. At $K=50$ with the graph
constraint a logit adjustment that changes no within-pair ranking recovers $+0.094$ of TDE's
$+0.097$; elsewhere the two differ by $0.02$ to $0.15$. TDE's within-pair AUC shows no gain: it
excludes changes above $0.0038$ to $0.028$ under four weightings, tightly only under the two
that favour head predicates. DLFE and IETrans, also prior corrections, gain $+0.111$ and
$+0.220$, $94\%$ and $97\%$ group-level. PE-Net, a prototype-based method, gains $+0.170$, $86\%$
group-level; its case-level part, $+0.024$, far exceeds the $+0.001$ between two separately
trained baselines, but its AUC change, $+0.005$, does not exceed their $0.006$ spread.
\end{abstract}
