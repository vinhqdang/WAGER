\begin{abstract}
Debiasing methods for scene graph generation are judged by mean recall; Total Direct Effect
(TDE) raises it in Visual Genome PredCls from 14.6 to 24.8. A model raises mean recall either by
predicting rare predicates more often within each subject--object pair, or by telling which
relations carry them, and the metric credits both alike. Scoring each prediction against the
label of another relation from the same pair breaks the link between a relation and its own
label but keeps the pair's label frequencies; this splits the difference between two frozen
models, in a proper score or in mean recall, exactly into a group-level part and a within-pair
covariance, with image-clustered intervals. On three released Neural-Motifs
checkpoints of prior-correcting methods, one run each, with class pairs as cells, 87\% of TDE's
mean-recall@50 gain (interval 83--91\%) is group-level (84\% of the official statistic; 18\%
with subject classes as cells). At $K=50$ with the graph constraint a logit adjustment that
changes no within-pair ranking recovers $+0.094$ of TDE's $+0.097$; elsewhere the two
differ by $0.02$ to $0.15$. No weighting of the
within-pair AUC shows TDE gaining discrimination: it excludes changes above $0.0038$ to $0.028$,
and with every predicate pair weighted equally it falls by $0.015$. DLFE and IETrans gain 11 and
21 points, 94\% and 97\% group-level. PE-Net, a prototype-based method that changes features
rather than the prior, gains 17 points, 86\% group-level, and its within-pair AUC rises (the
last two against a separately trained baseline).
\end{abstract}
