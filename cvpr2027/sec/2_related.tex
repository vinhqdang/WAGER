\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Bias and its evaluation in scene graph generation.}\label{sec:related-applied}
Since MOTIFS showed a frequency predictor of the predicate given the object pair to be
competitive~\cite{xu2017scene,zellers2018neural}, mean recall has become the headline metric,
and debiasing methods span the causal TDE intervention~\cite{tang2020unbiased}, class-balanced sampling
and group-wise learning~\cite{desai2021learning,dong2022stacked}, predicate-rebalancing message
passing~\cite{li2021bgnn}, predicate-correlation and hierarchical
losses~\cite{yan2020pcpl,yu2021cogtree,lyu2022fine}, prototype embeddings~\cite{zheng2023prototype}, edge-selective and relation-aware transformers~\cite{jung2023devil,sudhakaran2023vision}, and
correction or relabelling of the training labels~\cite{li2022devil,zhang2022finegrained}.
Correction by the predicate prior is known to rival them: post hoc, DLFE~\cite{chiou2021recovering}
recovers unbiased predicate probabilities from estimated label frequencies and Structured
Sparse R-CNN~\cite{teng2022structured} applies logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021long}; in
training, RTPB~\cite{chen2022resistance} resists a prior bias. The
metrics themselves have been shown to reward pathologies~\cite{li2022rethinking}, and
open-vocabulary methods inherit them~\cite{li2024pixels}. These works compare methods by their
metrics; we ask which part of a metric's change is group-level. Outside scene graphs, VQA models collapse when answer
priors change~\cite{agrawal2018dont}, models exploit non-visual
shortcuts~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut,zhou2025shortcut}, and gains are credited to mechanisms
that did not produce them~\cite{shrestha2020negative,teney2020value}. Long-tailed recognition faces the
same question for re-weighting and logit adjustment~\cite{cao2019learning,menon2021long}.
All of these characterise a \emph{single} model; we account for the gain \emph{between} two.

\paragraph{Proper-score decompositions.} Conditional on a grouping, a proper score splits into
reliability and resolution~\cite{murphy1973vector,degroot1983comparison,brocker2009reliability}.
Our case-level term is not the resolution term: it descends from the \emph{covariance}
decomposition of the Brier score due to Yates~\cite{yates1982external}, which a monotone
recalibration moves while the resolution is invariant to it; the two coincide only for models
calibrated within every group. Variance estimators exist for single-model
decompositions~\cite{siegert2014variance}, not for a contrast between two models, and the grouping
loss~\cite{perezlebel2023beyond} is the single-model relative of our case-level part. Transporting
labels within a group permutes the input--label pairing, so one model's case-level part is a
conditional permutation importance~\cite{strobl2008conditional,fisher2019all} of the non-grouping
part of its input and a gain's is the difference of two models' (\cref{prop:reliance}). What the
split adds is the exact additive accounting of a paired gain, including thresholded metrics, and
paired inference for it.

\paragraph{Comparing two predictors.} At a single group our total-gain interval is the
clustered Diebold--Mariano test~\cite{diebold1995comparing,giacomini2006tests}
(\cref{cor:dm}); the case-level term is a paired order-two U-statistic, as in DeLong
\etal's comparison of ROC areas~\cite{delong1988comparing,hoeffding1948class}, with a H\'ajek
projection~\cite{serfling1980approximation} as its influence function.
