Tang \etal~\cite{tang2020unbiased} release a causal MOTIFS PredCls checkpoint that yields two
models from one set of weights: the baseline (MOTIFS-SUM), whose predicate logit sums a
union-region visual term, a context term and a frequency prior indexed by the object pair, and
TDE, which subtracts the same sum with an averaged context and so keeps only the context term
minus its average. We ran it once with the authors' code on the canonical VG150 test split,
saving every branch's logits, and replayed the official evaluator offline (\cref{app:recall}).
The replay reproduces the official numbers to the fourth decimal: baseline $R@50=0.6612$ and
$mR@50=0.1459$, TDE $0.4588$ and $0.2476$; without the graph constraint TDE's mean recall
\emph{falls}, from $0.3260$ to $0.2981$. The audit covers $183{,}642$ relations over $26{,}446$
images in $7{,}127$ cells; $98.9\%$ fall in the $5{,}044$ cells holding at least two.

\paragraph{An operating-point control.} We add one model that needs no training: the
baseline's logits minus $\tau\log\pi(y)$, the training predicate prior, with
$\tau=1$~\cite{menon2021long}. Within a cell this adds the same constant to every
log-odds $\log q(y)-\log q(z)$, so it changes no within-cell ranking: whatever case-level
part it shows is existing discrimination spent at a new operating point, not new
discrimination. It reaches $mR@50=0.2343$ at $R@50=0.6437$.

\paragraph{Mean recall, split.} Pooled over identified relations, mean recall@50 rises by
$0.0972$ ($0.1017$ on the official per-image average). Of that, $+0.0841$ is group-level
and $+0.0130$ case-level, interval $[+0.0093,+0.0168]$ (\cref{tab:sggaudit}): $87\%$ of the
gain, interval $[83\%,91\%]$, survives shuffling every label among the relations that share
its object pair.
It lives in the body of the distribution. Splitting predicates by training frequency (9
head above $10{,}000$ instances, 26 body, 15 tail below $500$), the body gains $+0.1224$, of
which $+0.1082$ is group-level; the head loses $0.0255$; the tail gains $0.0003$: of the 15 tail
predicates the baseline recalls none and TDE one, \textsf{to}, at $0.016$. \Cref{fig:predicates} shows the mechanism one
predicate at a time. Recall of \textsf{parked on} rises from $0.000$ to $0.886$, almost all of
it group-level: TDE now says \textsf{parked on} for (\textsf{car}, \textsf{street}). Recall of \textsf{on} falls by $0.558$ in the same way.

\paragraph{The case-level part is matched by the control.} The control reproduces the
whole picture: $+0.0941$ of mean recall, $+0.0802$ group-level and $+0.0139$ case-level.
Against it, TDE's case-level part is $-0.0008$, interval $[-0.0050,+0.0033]$. So a
case-level part of a thresholded metric is not by itself evidence of new discrimination.
When the boundary between \textsf{on} and \textsf{parked on} moves, the cases that cross it
are those the baseline already scored as more \textsf{parked on}-like, and they are more
often labelled so; the covariance is real, but bought with discrimination the baseline had. The direct test is the within-cell AUC, which no prior
shift or recalibration can move: TDE's change is $+0.0026$, interval $[-0.0028,+0.0086]$,
weighted by comparison, and $-0.0015$, $[-0.0036,+0.0018]$, weighted as the split weights
cells. By predicate tier the comparison-weighted change is $+0.0068$, $[-0.0003,+0.0134]$,
on the head--head pairs that carry $65\%$ of its weight and $-0.0051$, $[-0.0130,+0.0026]$, on
the rest (\cref{app:rankrob}): a pattern of redistribution, not a detected one.

\paragraph{The proper score depends on the protocol.} With each model's temperature fitted on
held-out images ($T^*=2.50$ baseline, $0.92$ TDE), TDE's quadratic-score change is
$-0.18264$, of which $-0.18258$ is group-level and $-0.00006$, interval $[-0.00120,+0.00109]$,
case-level (\cref{fig:where}); over 20 calibration halves the case-level part stays within
$[-0.00131,+0.00059]$. But a temperature cannot undo a class-wise shift of the operating
point, and the control shows one: its case-level part is $+0.0039$. Matching a temperature
and a per-class bias for each model, which also preserves every within-cell ranking, makes
the control identical to the baseline and TDE's case-level part $+0.00445$, interval
$[+0.00354,+0.00536]$; one temperature shared by both models makes it $-0.0184$; the log
score gives $+0.04396$ (\cref{app:rankrob,app:splits}). The sign of TDE's case-level part
depends on the protocol. Merging synonymous
predicates (32 classes) or collapsing them into three families changes none of this
(\cref{app:merge}).

\paragraph{What that licenses.} On this checkpoint the ten points of mean recall are a change in
which predicates the model favours within each object pair. A one-line prior shift that ranks
the relations of every cell exactly as the baseline did reproduces the gain, its split and
its tier profile at under a tenth of TDE's cost in $R@50$ ($0.0175$ against $0.2023$), and a
better $F@50$, the harmonic mean of $R@50$ and $mR@50$ ($0.344$ against $0.322$). That a prior
correction rivals debiasing is known~\cite{chiou2021recovering,teng2022structured}; the split
shows which part of TDE's gain it reproduces. No measure we tried detects a change in
within-cell ranking, and the proper-score case-level part, small under every protocol, has no
protocol-free sign.

\paragraph{Why.} Under the released SUM fusion, TDE's change from the baseline is three
subtractions, and splitting along them answers why; the parts add up to TDE's exactly (\cref{app:tdepath}). Removing the
frequency prior, constant within every cell, moves mean recall by $-0.0009$ and the
within-cell AUC not at all. Removing the visual (union-region) term adds $+0.0164$, with a case-level part
of $+0.0016$, interval $[-0.0006,+0.0037]$: within a cell, the context term already knew what
the visual term did. Subtracting the averaged context supplies $+0.0817$ of the $0.0972$.
That term is, to $99.96\%$ of its energy, one vector shared by every relation in every image,
correlated $+0.75$ with the log training prior. In PredCls, TDE's counterfactual is to first
order a learned logit adjustment of the context branch, which is why the explicit one
reproduces it.

\paragraph{A second method.} IETrans~\cite{zhang2022finegrained} relabels training data,
moving general predicates to specific ones within the same object pair, and adds the
training log-prior at test time. Its released Neural-Motifs PredCls checkpoint is the
IETrans+Rwt variant, trained with a reweighted loss as well; we ran it once with the
authors' evaluation command and re-scored it the same way (\cref{app:ietrans}). It reaches
$mR@50=0.3587$ ($0.3576$ on the authors' test set, which drops duplicate relations): $+0.2204$
over the baseline, of which $+0.2143$ is group-level and $+0.0060$ case-level, interval
$[-0.0031,+0.0151]$. Unlike TDE it reaches the tail ($+0.0889$), again group-level. Its
within-cell discrimination falls under every measure: matched case-level parts $-0.0162$
(quadratic) and $-0.0544$ (log), $-0.0168$ with a per-class bias as well, and the AUC
change weighted as the split weights cells is $-0.0235$, interval $[-0.0275,-0.0182]$,
concentrated on head--body and body--body pairs. Both methods' gains are group-level; with TDE no change in within-cell ranking is detected,
while IETrans+Rwt loses some. No IETrans baseline was released, so this compares two
separately trained models and does not isolate the effect of relabelling.

\paragraph{Beyond PredCls.} The same release holds SGCls and SGDet checkpoints, where object
classes (and in SGDet boxes) are predicted. One TDE pass still yields all three models, which
share every object prediction, so the ground-truth pair remains a common grouping; we replay
the evaluator inside the inference loop and reproduce the released recalls (\cref{app:det}). In
SGCls TDE raises $mR@50$ by $+0.0517$, $99\%$ of it group-level; the control gains $+0.0518$ at
$R@50=0.384$ against TDE's $0.263$; and TDE's within-cell ranking gets worse, by $-0.0128$ of
relation-weighted AUC, interval $[-0.0171,-0.0074]$. On the first $4{,}000$ SGDet test images
the control again gains more than TDE ($+0.0510$ against $+0.0375$) at nearly twice its $R@50$.
