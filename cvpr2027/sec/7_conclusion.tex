\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Limitations.} \emph{The split is conditional on the grouping}: two equally fine
partitions can disagree (\cref{app:partition}), so the benchmark should declare $\phi$ before a
leaderboard opens. \emph{It credits any within-cell signal}: an unrecorded covariate
correlated at $0.061$ with both parts would account for the case-level gain of
\cref{sec:data} (\cref{app:sensitivity}). \emph{It moves with recalibration, the operating
point and the label mix}; the AUC is invariant to the first two but gives up exact
additivity. \emph{Its interval needs cells that are not tiny}: with two cases in every cell it
covers $81.5\%$ (\cref{app:clt}); $1.0\%$ of audited relations sit in such cells.
\emph{It needs scores for every label}. And \emph{each model is one released run}:
intervals cover test sampling, not training; the matching uses half the test images.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Mean recall reports the sum of two achievements; scoring each
prediction against another case's label from the same group separates them exactly. On three
released prior-correcting PredCls checkpoints the gains are almost entirely group-level: TDE's
is matched by a ranking-preserving logit adjustment at $K=50$ under the graph constraint, and
no weighting of its within-pair AUC shows a gain. We audit no method that changes features
rather than the prior. We propose reporting, beside mean recall, the declared grouping, both
parts with intervals, the within-group AUC and, for a thresholded metric, the
operating-point control.
