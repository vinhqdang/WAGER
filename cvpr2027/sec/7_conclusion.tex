\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Limitations.} Four bound what the split can say. \emph{It is conditional on the grouping.} The case-level part measures
discrimination among relations sharing an object pair; two equally fine partitions of the
same cases can disagree completely about whether a gain is case-level
(\cref{app:partition}), so declaring $\phi$ is a modelling decision that the benchmark, not
the submitting author, should make before a leaderboard opens. \emph{It credits any
within-cell signal}, including shortcuts nobody recorded, and our own bound on that exposure is
not reassuring: for the controlled-predictor pair of \cref{sec:data}, an unrecorded covariate
correlated at $0.061$ with both parts would account for the entire case-level gain
(\cref{app:sensitivity}). \emph{It is not invariant to recalibration, to the operating point or to the label mix}: we
read it confidence-matched and, for thresholded metrics, against an operating-point control;
the within-cell AUC, invariant to the first two, gives up exact additivity; and a case-level
null can be a cancellation that another label mix undoes (\cref{sec:vgvisual}). And \emph{it needs
scores for every label}: most benchmarks archive only ranked top-$k$ predictions, and
recovering the rest took a rerun of the released checkpoint: that, not compute, blocks routine use.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} A ten-point mean-recall gain is two different achievements summed
together, and the field's metrics report the sum. Scoring each prediction against another
case's label from the same group separates them exactly, with no fitted reference model and with
image-clustered intervals. Applied to two released
debiasing methods, the split finds mean-recall gains that are almost entirely group-level:
TDE's, whose case-level remainder a ranking-preserving logit adjustment matches and behind
which no within-group discrimination was gained, and IETrans's, with a case-level loss;
applied to a model every aggregate metric ranks last, it finds a case-level gain that the
aggregates conceal. We suggest that benchmarks whose labels lean on a
feature every model sees report the split beside the headline metric: the declared grouping
and its coverage, both signed parts with the case-level interval, the confidence-matched
row, and, for a thresholded metric, the operating-point control. Code, results files and the scripts that regenerate every prediction are in the
supplement.
