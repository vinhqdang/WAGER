\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Limitations.} Four bound what the split can say, and we would rather state them
than have them found. \emph{It is conditional on the grouping.} The case-level part measures
discrimination among relations sharing an object pair; two equally fine partitions of the
same cases can disagree completely about whether a gain is case-level
(\cref{app:partition}), so declaring $\phi$ is a modelling decision that the benchmark, not
the submitting author, should make before a leaderboard opens. \emph{It credits any
within-cell signal}, including shortcuts nobody recorded, and our own bound on that exposure is
not reassuring: for the controlled-predictor pair of \cref{sec:data}, an unrecorded covariate
correlated at $0.061$ with both parts would account for the entire case-level gain
(\cref{app:sensitivity}). \emph{It is not invariant to recalibration}, which is why every
comparison here is confidence-matched; a rank-based within-cell contrast would be invariant,
at the cost of the exact additivity that is the point of the construction. And \emph{it needs
probabilities}: most benchmarks archive ranked predictions, and that, not compute, is the
obstacle to routine use.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} A ten-point mean-recall gain is two different achievements summed
together, and the field's metrics report the sum. Scoring each prediction against another
case's label from the same group separates them exactly, with no fitted reference model and
with intervals that respect the clustering of relations within images. Applied to a released
debiasing method, the split finds a large metric gain carrying no detectable case-level
improvement under the quadratic score; applied to a model every aggregate metric ranks last,
it finds the best recogniser of the three. We suggest that benchmarks whose labels lean on a
feature every model sees report the split beside the headline metric: the declared grouping
and its coverage, both signed parts with the case-level interval, and the confidence-matched
row. Code and cached predictions to reproduce every number are in the supplementary material.
