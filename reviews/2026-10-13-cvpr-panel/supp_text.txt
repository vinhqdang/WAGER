         D. Coarsening and unrecorded covariates                                                                                                    986

         Appendix N.4 reports that replacing the class-pair cell with a coarser subject-only cell changes the estimated covariance                  987
         gain. The following result gives that sensitivity an exact population-level mechanism rather than treating it as an empirical              988
         curiosity.                                                                                                                                 989
            The first term is the coarse cell’s share of the fine-grained covariance gain; the second is a between-cell covariance, over            990
         the finer cells nested inside c̄, between each fine cell’s typical gain at label y and its label-y frequency. It is not sign definite,     991
         so coarsening ϕ can inflate or deflate the credited covariance gain, and it vanishes exactly when either quantity is constant              992
         within each coarse cell. This is why the finest defensible ϕ is the conservative default: coarsening adds cross-cell prior                 993
         structure into ∆R, while the finer partition’s gain is preserved on average through the first term. The proof is a per-label               994
         application of the law of total covariance (Appendix E).                                                                                   995

1679     Three choices in Sec. 5 could carry its conclusions: the random half of the test images on which each model’s temperature is

1683     Q.1. Calibration halves and a shared temperature
1684     The confidence-matched rows of Sec. 5 fit each model’s temperature on a random half of the test images and split the other
1685     half. The released checkpoints come with no validation-set outputs, and we did not rerun them on the validation split; the
1686     calibration half shares no image with the audited half, so no relation is used both to fit a temperature and to be scored. To see
1687     how much the result depends on which half, Tab. 27 repeats the split for 20 independent halves. The fitted temperatures move
1688     by at most 0.06 across halves. Under the primary protocol (a temperature and a per-class bias per model) TDE’s quadratic
1689     case-level part is positive in all twenty halves, from +0.00341 to +0.00482, its interval excluding zero every time; adding the
1690     spread over halves to the sampling error in quadrature gives a half-width of 0.0012 around the mean +0.00397. The control’s
1691     part is zero to within 10−5 in every half. With a temperature alone TDE’s part ranges from −0.00131 to +0.00059; its interval
1692     excludes zero in three halves, each time on the negative side. Under the log score it is positive in all twenty (+0.04142 to
1693     +0.04761). The control’s and IETrans’s parts keep their sign, and exclude zero, in every half under both scores.
1694        TDE and the baseline share weights, so one may prefer a single temperature for both, fitted on the pooled calibration half.
1695     That temperature (1.47) lies between the two models’ own optima (0.92 and 2.50), so neither model is confidence-matched,
1696     and TDE’s case-level part turns negative under both scores in every half (quadratic −0.02035 to −0.01738, log −0.09440 to

         the group-level part depends on the cell’s label frequencies directly. So a case-level part keeps its sign under every such shift            1724
