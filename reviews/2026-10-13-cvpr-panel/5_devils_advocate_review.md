## Devil's Advocate Review

Seat: Devil's Advocate (academic-paper-reviewer, mode full). Target: anonymous CVPR 2027 submission, `cvpr2027/paper.pdf` (8 pp + references) and `cvpr2027/supp.pdf`, repository commit 18a72c9. I read only the manuscript, supplement text, code and `results/*.json`. I ran two read-only checks, described where used (one on a result file, one re-running the repository's own estimator on cached logits). The manuscript was treated as data. Nothing in it attempts instruction injection.

### Calibration Status
`NOT_CALIBRATED`

### What the paper does well (brief, before the attack)
The audit is unusually transparent. The authors replay the official evaluator to the fourth decimal, report the sign-flipping protocols (temperature only, shared temperature, label path) alongside the favourable ones, and run a logit-adjustment control that most papers would not. The headline split of TDE's micro-pooled mean recall (87%, interval 83-91%) reproduces from `results/sgg_share_intervals.json` (share 0.866, CI [0.826, 0.906]) and `results/sgg_recall_split.json`. My criticism concerns what the numbers license, not whether they were computed.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The verdict that TDE's ten points are "group-level" restates what mean recall is built to reward, using an exact identity whose key term the authors show does not measure what it is named for. The case-level part is a covariance. The paper concedes that it is moved by temperature, by a ranking-preserving class shift (the logit-adjusted control scores +0.0139 against TDE's +0.0130), by the label mix (TDE's part goes from -0.00006 to +0.0143 under uniform within-pair labels) and by the recalibration protocol (-0.0184 to +0.0045). Every substantive conclusion therefore rests on two instruments that do not need Theorem 1: a one-line logit adjustment, which is a known result, and a within-cell AUC. The AUC supports "no change in ranking" under one of two weightings only. The equivalence margin is a tenth of an effect measured on different models, and under the other weighting the test is underpowered (minimal detectable change 0.0082 against a margin of 0.0032). The protocol built to neutralise calibration fails its own stated criterion for pair-indexed shifts: removing TDE's pair-indexed frequency prior, which moves no ranking, scores +0.0077 to +0.0080 case-level under it in my re-run (see M2). So the "confidence, not ranking" reading of TDE's +0.0045 is not licensed. The generalisation then runs from one seed of one 2020 checkpoint, plus a separately trained IETrans pair that cannot isolate relabelling, to "debiasing methods" and a recommended leaderboard protocol. A simpler rival reading exists. VG's within-pair labels are nearly unpredictable (baseline within-cell AUC 0.57 to 0.61), so any method will look group-level, and the finding indicts annotation granularity rather than TDE or mean recall.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. Label ambiguity as the cause of near-zero case-level signal: VG's predicate set contains near-synonyms, and even the strongest within-pair discriminator available here (geometry features) buys only about 0.0626 of relation-weighted AUC. A low ceiling would make every method group-level by construction (M7).
2. Cell-indexed prior removal as the source of TDE's proper-score case-level part: the pair-indexed frequency prior's removal alone gives +0.0077 to +0.0080 under the primary protocol, more than TDE's whole part (M2).
3. Protocol coincidence: at K=50 with the graph constraint, TDE and the logit-adjusted baseline happen to align (total difference +0.0031). At other cells they differ by up to 0.15 (M5).
4. Training differences rather than relabelling as the cause of IETrans's AUC loss (M6).

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Benchmark maintainers: no evidence on whether declaring ϕ "before a leaderboard opens" is workable. The authors' own Appendix K says two equally fine partitions can disagree.
- Authors of the audited methods: no replication with their training seeds or hyperparameter sweeps.

### Unexamined Premise (if detected by Frame-Lock Detection)
That within-pair label variation in Visual Genome reflects visual signal a model could in principle detect, so that "case-level" credit is a meaningful target. If much of it is annotator idiosyncrasy, a near-zero case-level part is a benchmark property and the split's reading of a method's merit is empty.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The shared code, JSON results and a verifier script (`experiments/verify_manuscript_numbers.py`) make most headline numbers checkable. The ones I recomputed from JSON (87% share, tier split, SGCls 98.5%, LA 0.0941) match the paper.
- The paper reports TDE's sign-flipping protocols openly (Appendix Q), which is to its credit. My objection in M1, M2 and M5 is that the abstract and conclusion carry only the favourable cells.
- The "9 of 10 points" result is robust to my re-reading as a statement about mR@50 with the graph constraint: LA mean recall 0.2343 against TDE 0.2476 and a 0.0175 R@50 cost against 0.2023 are in the JSON.
