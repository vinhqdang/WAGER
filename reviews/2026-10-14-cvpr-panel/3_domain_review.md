## Overall Assessment
Recommendation: Weak Accept (borderline), conditional on the scoping fixes in W1-W4.
Confidence: 4/5 on the SGG protocol points, 3/5 on the statistical theory (outside my specialty).

The evaluator replay is the strongest part of the paper. It reproduces official R/mR to four decimals for all four checkpoints, which is more careful than most SGG evaluation papers. The headline TDE finding (a no-training logit shift with unchanged within-pair ranking recovers +0.094 of +0.097 mR@50) is correct as far as I can verify and useful to the SGG community. My concerns are about scope. The abstract and conclusion generalise to "prior-correcting methods" from comparisons that are, for two of the three methods, confounded by training differences, and the headline control match is specific to one protocol cell (K=50, graph constraint, PredCls).

## Summary
The paper proposes an exact within-group label-transport decomposition of the difference between two frozen models' scores (or of a micro-pooled recall difference) into a group-level part and a within-group covariance (case-level) part. Groups are subject-object class pairs. It validates the decomposition in simulation and on controlled predictors. It then replays the official SGG evaluator on released checkpoints of TDE, DLFE, IETrans+Rwt and PE-Net. It finds that 84-97% of mR@50 gains are group-level. It adds a no-training logit-adjusted control and a within-cell AUC as the ranking test. TDE's AUC shows no gain, while PE-Net's shows a small one.

## Strengths
1. Exact replay of the official evaluator. This includes duplicate-box matching through IoU 0.5 (Appx O) and a unit test with deliberate fault injection. Replayed R@50/mR@50 match official values for all models (Tab. 17, DLFE 0.5165/0.2686, PE-Net 0.6523/0.3142; I confirmed these in results/dlfe_official_recall.json and penet_official_recall.json).
2. The operating-point control (LA, tau=1) is the right control for a thresholded metric. The paper correctly notes that the case-level part of a hit rate contains "discrimination spent at a new operating point", and does not over-read it.
3. The paper reports where its conclusion fails: TDE leads LA by +0.030 at K=100, trails it by 0.0234 at K=20 and by 0.08-0.15 without the graph constraint (Tab. 2). Without the graph constraint TDE's mR falls. It acknowledges that subject-only cells give 18%.
4. It reports the unfavourable fact that IETrans's AUC falls and checks synonymy (Q.8). It also includes the PE-Net positive case, which keeps the paper from being a pure takedown.
5. The explanation of TDE as "to first order a learned logit adjustment" in PredCls is mechanistic: the averaged-context term is 99.96% one shared vector, correlation +0.75 with the log prior. It is consistent with Tang et al.'s own framing.
6. Zero-shot handling (Q.7) is sensible. The zero-shot indicator is a valid label weight. The singleton-cell remainder is separated (7,461 identified vs 1,384 singleton, in results/sgg_zeroshot_split.json).

## Weaknesses

### Major

**W1. Fairness of the IETrans and PE-Net comparisons: the abstract's group-level share is not attributable to the methods.**
Anchor: Abstract ll.023-027; Sec. 5 "Two more methods" (ll.495-525); Tab. 1; Supp Q.6 and Appx O.2.
Problem: IETrans+Rwt and PE-Net are compared against the TDE release's MOTIFS-SUM baseline. That baseline is a different codebase and recipe, a different fusion (SUM) and a different training run. The IETrans checkpoint also carries a reweighted loss and relabelled data, and its test-time log-prior is not part of the network. The paper says as much in the text, but the abstract states "DLFE and IETrans gain 11 and 21 points, 94% and 97% group-level" and "PE-Net ... 86% group-level" with no qualifier. The group-level share between two separately trained networks mixes method effect, architecture, optimiser and seed variation. For IETrans, the difference in head/tail behaviour could come from any of these. The case-level and AUC findings for PE-Net (+0.0050, "a twelfth of box geometry") inherit this: a different backbone training could shift within-pair ranking independently of prototypes. "Each model is one released training run" is acknowledged, but intervals "cover test sampling only", so a reader cannot tell whether a 0.005 AUC difference is above training-seed noise. For TDE, the baseline and TDE come from the same weights, so no such problem exists. DLFE is the only clean comparison besides TDE.
Fix: (a) Put the qualifier into the abstract: only TDE and DLFE are controlled; IETrans and PE-Net are system-level comparisons. (b) Use the IETrans without-prior variant (already in Tab. 25, which isolates the test-time prior) as the controlled IETrans statement, and drop the "97% group-level" figure from the abstract. (c) For PE-Net, retrain a MOTIFS baseline in the PE-Net codebase (the repo ships configs) or at least report training-seed spread for the PE-Net AUC claim from a second seed. If this is not feasible, move the PE-Net AUC rise from "the split shows it" to "consistent with", and drop "gains some within-pair discrimination" from the conclusion. (d) Report a baseline-to-baseline sanity check: MOTIFS-SUM (TDE release) vs DLFE's MOTIFS baseline differ in mR@50 (0.1459 vs 0.1647) from supposedly the same architecture. This gives a rough scale for training-recipe variation (about 0.019 mR) against which the case-level parts of 0.006-0.024 should be read.

**W2. Conclusions about "prior-correcting methods" and debiasing are over-generalised; the evidence is one architecture (MOTIFS), one task (PredCls), one metric configuration, one run.**
Anchor: Abstract l.012-013 ("three released ... checkpoints of prior-correcting methods"); Conclusion ll.600-606; Sec. 1 "What is not claimed".
Problem: The paper does scope itself (Sec. 1, Sec. 7), which I credit. But the title question ("What did ten points of mean recall buy?") and the conclusion ("On three released prior-correcting PredCls checkpoints the gains are almost entirely group-level") invite a reading about SGG debiasing in general. The field's main debiasing families are not tested: resampling/loss re-weighting trained from scratch (CogTree, PCPL, GCL/SHA-GCL, FGPL), feature augmentation (CFA), and transformer-based models (SQUAT, VETO, which the paper cites but does not audit). Most of these change features during training, as PE-Net does. The one such method audited (PE-Net) does show a within-pair AUC gain, so the "debiasing = prior shift" reading has at least one counterexample in the paper's own data. Also, DLFE's and IETrans's group-level shares (94%, 97%) are near-trivially expected: both are explicit per-predicate post-hoc rescalings (DLFE) or prior-adding (IETrans test-time), and the paper's own Theorem 1 states a function of c alone has zero case-level part. Only TDE's was a non-obvious result.
Fix: Retitle or sub-scope (for example "...in MOTIFS PredCls"). State in the abstract that two of the three "prior-correcting" methods apply an explicit per-predicate or per-pair constant at test time, so a large group-level share is expected by construction, and the informative finding is the TDE one. If possible, add one trained-from-scratch rebalancing method with a public baseline (the Scene-Graph-Benchmark MOTIFS with GCL/SHA-GCL or CFA checkpoints) to show the method generalises beyond logit-shift methods.

**W3. The headline "control recovers TDE" claim is specific to one protocol cell, yet is prominent in the abstract.**
Anchor: Abstract ll.016-019; Tab. 2; Sec. 5 ll.447-462.
Problem: The abstract says "At K = 50 with the graph constraint a logit adjustment ... recovers +0.094 of TDE's +0.097; elsewhere the two differ by 0.02 to 0.15." That is honest. But the control uses tau=1, chosen on the basis of the TDE comparison (a post-hoc choice, as the paper says for TDE and IETrans), and TDE vs LA at K=100 is +0.0300 and without the graph constraint TDE-LA is -0.08 to -0.15 while TDE's own mR falls. The surprising agreement at K=50 is then a coincidence of operating point, not a mechanism: TDE and LA1 have R@50 of 0.459 vs 0.644, so they are at very different recall/precision trade-offs even there. The F@50 claim (0.344 vs 0.322, ll.451-452) is a single comparison and should come with the same caveat. The more defensible claim is "TDE's mR gain is of the same kind as LA's (group-level, no AUC gain)", not "the control reproduces the gain".
Fix: Lead with the AUC and the group-level share finding. Present the K=50 numeric match as an illustration and report a tau sweep (tau in {0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5}) for TDE vs LA_tau at all six protocols to show that some tau matches TDE at each protocol. This would back the mechanism claim without a lucky operating point. The LA.5 data already exist (Tab. 18).

**W4. Matched-pair restriction in SGCls and SGDet limits what the audit says about the main SGG settings.**
Anchor: Sec. 5 "Beyond PredCls"; Supp Q.9 (Tab. 37, 38).
Problem: Only 58% (SGCls) and 65% (SGDet) of relations have a matched predicted pair. The mean-recall split treats unmatched relations as misses for all models, which is fine. The AUC and proper-score results, however, are computed on matched relations only. That is a selection on object recognition quality, and the paper concedes it (ll.1918-1919). In SGDet, TDE's AUC is a null on this subset, but TDE's effect on mR is largest on exactly the relations whose objects are well detected. More importantly, in SGDet and SGCls the grouping by ground-truth class pair is no longer a pair "both models observe": the model sees predicted labels, and for the 35-42% of relations unmatched, the group-level/case-level partition is vacuous. The shares quoted in the abstract-adjacent text ("99% group-level" SGCls, "87%" SGDet) therefore describe a different population from the PredCls 87%. Also, the SGDet pair selection (largest summed IoU among matching pairs, "chosen from boxes and labels alone") and the replay of the post-processor's triple score ordering (predicate times subject and object scores) are only validated on test images 0-199 to printed precision for all K, plus whole-set R/mR for TDE; Tab. 37 shows SGDet TDE mR@50 0.0894 released vs 0.0901 replayed (0.8% relative), and SGCls 0.1321 vs 0.1322, which is not "reproduced to the fourth decimal" as claimed for PredCls. The paper says "they reproduce the released numbers"; this should say "to within 0.0007".
Fix: Report the share of group-level gain on the full set where possible (the mR split already does) and state in the main text that the AUC claims for SGCls/SGDet are conditional on the matched subset; give a bound or a sensitivity (for instance, restrict the PredCls AUC to the same relation subset type, or weight the SGDet AUC by detection score). Explain the 0.0007 SGDet discrepancy (the 5-sigma lower precision of the inline evaluator? per-image vs micro?). Note that the SGDet detector is fixed for all three models, so detector variance is not an issue, but object-NMS and the "REQUIRE_BOX_OVERLAP"/triplet-NMS settings of the release should be stated.

### Minor

**W5. rel_nms for PE-Net: the replay is faithful for recall, but the probability-based quantities (AUC, quadratic score) are computed on pre-NMS predicate distributions.**
Anchor: Supp Q.6; experiments/colab_sgg_rank.py ll.261-272, 357-372; experiments/run_sgg_penet_audit.py l.151; penet_rel_nms_chunked.py.
Problem: I checked the code. For PE-Net the graph-constrained ranks use the rel_nms path (predicate assigned per pair by greedy NMS with threshold 0.6 in the replay, and 0.5 default in the chunked function, with threshold passed explicitly so this is consistent). The counterfactual rank under label y is derived from the NMS-assigned predicate and position, and this is label-independent, so the transport argument stays valid. Two things need stating. (i) rel_nms assigns to a pair a predicate that is not always its argmax (suppression), and a pair's NMS predicate depends on other pairs; so the evaluator's "hit" under label y is not a function of that pair's own distribution alone. The decomposition remains exact because hits are evaluated at counterfactual labels, but the interpretation "the model's probability change on the label" is looser for PE-Net than for MOTIFS. (ii) The AUC and proper-score parts use raw probabilities (the code stores `probs[v]` from `p[np.maximum(own,0)]`), not NMS-processed scores, so for PE-Net there is a mismatch between the object the recall split sees and the object the AUC sees. (iii) The sort key in nms_predcls_ranks multiplies subject score twice (`obj_scores[off_pairs[:,0]]` twice), which mirrors the original evaluator but is harmless only because PredCls object scores are 1; say so. (iv) The chunked rel_nms was checked on 300 random synthetic images and gives identical R/mR on the real run; good, but the self-test should also include a test with real dense images, since memory failure occurred only there.
Fix: Add one sentence in Q.6 on points (i)-(ii), report `recalls_without_rel_nms` for PE-Net (the code already records it) to show how much NMS moves mR, and note that MOTIFS/TDE/DLFE use argmax-per-pair graph constraint without rel_nms, so PE-Net's protocol differs from the others'.

**W6. Zero-shot list and zero-shot results are under-reported in the main text.**
Anchor: Supp Q.7 (Tab. 36); main text ll.483-486.
Problem: The zero-shot list comes from the benchmark file zeroshot_triplet.pytorch (a legacy pickle decoded without torch; 8,845 test relations, 4.8%). I could not load it here (no torch), but the zR values reproduced against the official printouts per the paper. The substantive point: Tab. 36 shows IETrans's zR@50 falls by 0.0779 and DLFE's by 0.0279 relative to their baselines, while TDE rises +0.033. This is important evidence for the SGG community (the unbiased-SGG methods trade zero-shot generalisation for mean recall) but it is in the supplement only. The main text mentions only the TDE zero-shot, where TDE beats the control by +0.035 with a falling AUC on unseen triplets. The claim "TDE's zero-shot advantage is an operating point that happens to favour unseen triplets, not a better ranking" rests on an AUC of -0.0149 over 11% of the comparison weight and the second weighting (by comparison) is [-0.0343, +0.0097], covering zero.
Fix: Move the DLFE and IETrans zero-shot drops into the main text. Soften "not a better ranking" to "no evidence of better ranking" for the by-comparison weighting.

**W7. Graph constraint, micro-pooling and per-image mean recall.**
Anchor: Sec. 1 l.109-111; Sec. 5 "Protocol"; Supp O "Micro-averaged mean recall".
Problem: The split is for micro-pooled mean recall (+0.0972), while the official per-image statistic moves +0.1017. The paper reports the per-image split (84% group-level) separately. Because the field reports the per-image number, the headline 87% should be stated next to 84% in the abstract, and a reader should know that the decomposition is not exact for the official statistic. I see the abstract does give 84%. Also the "identified relations only" restriction (183,642 relations, 98.9% in cells with 2 or more) is fine.
Fix: None beyond a one-line main-text remark that the split is exact for the pooled version only (already in l.109-111).

**W8. Grouping choice.**
Anchor: Tab. 1 lower panel; Sec. 3 "Choosing the grouping"; Appx K.
Problem: With subject-only cells the group-level share drops from 87% to 18%. The paper explains why (the object identity is no longer fixed). For SGG the natural cell is the pair, but this result means the headline share is a property of the cell definition as much as of TDE. Spatially refined cells give 90%, which is reassuring. A reader from SGG will ask about predicate-conditional alternatives (cells by union-box geometry bins, since SGG benchmarks provide boxes).
Fix: Add a union-box-geometry-bin refinement of the pair cell (e.g., relative position and size bins). Position and size refinement are already in Tab. 1 ("+position" 90%), so this is mostly presentation.

**W9. Writing and framing.**
Anchor: Abstract; Sec. 1.
Problem: The abstract carries about 20 numbers and several nested parentheticals; the central message is hard to extract. "Group-level" vs "case-level" naming is good, but the abstract also mixes "official statistic", "class pairs as cells", "interval 83-91%", and AUC bounds in one run. The paper claims "no weighting shows TDE gaining discrimination"; under comparison weighting TDE is +0.0025 [-0.0028, +0.0087] and the within-pair AUC bound of 0.0038 to 0.028 excluded changes are post-hoc margins ("set after TDE's results were seen").
Fix: Cut the abstract to the three claims (exact split; TDE 87% group-level and matched by a ranking-preserving shift; PE-Net differs), and move margins and per-weighting bounds to the text.

## Questions for the authors
1. Does the PE-Net release use any frequency bias or prior in its relation head (e.g., a `USE_FREQUENCY_BIAS` flag in its config)? The paper states "no prior"; please state the config value used for the released checkpoint.
2. For a tau sweep of LA at each of the six protocols, is there a tau for which TDE and LA agree in group-level part and in case-level part at K=20 and K=100 and without the graph constraint?
3. How large is the spread of the group-level share across training seeds for MOTIFS-SUM (the CLIP study has five seeds; the SGG audit has one)? If the SGG-benchmark MOTIFS baseline is retrained with a few seeds, how much do mR@50 and the case-level part move?
4. Why is the DLFE baseline (R@50 0.6549, mR@50 0.1647) so different from the TDE release baseline (0.6612, 0.1459) if both are MOTIFS PredCls? Which of the two is closer to the PE-Net-relevant baseline?
5. In SGDet, is the 6,320 pairs per image cap the same as the evaluator's, and does the inline replay use the evaluator's triplet-level NMS setting?
6. TDE's mR falls without the graph constraint. Is the 87% group-level share meaningful as a statement about the ten points when the same method loses mR under the other standard protocol of the benchmark?

## Detailed comments
- Tab. 1: the footnote "no calibration or group-level shift moves [AUC]" is correct for exact logit offsets. State that this relies on float32 storage for LA (change below 1e-6), as you do.
- Sec. 5: "The baseline recalls no tail predicate; TDE recalls the predicate to at 0.016": the tail result (+0.0003) makes the "ten points" a body-predicate story. This is a major SGG-relevant finding that deserves a sentence on why tail gains are absent for TDE, given that tail recall is what mean-recall debiasing is supposed to improve. IETrans and DLFE do gain tail recall (+0.2964, +0.1622 per tail predicate, Tab. 36), so the tier profile differs across methods and the "same pattern" statement (l.081-082) is too strong for tail.
- Sec. 5, "Beyond PredCls": SGCls and SGDet results use the TDE release only, so DLFE, IETrans and PE-Net are PredCls-only. The statement in l.098-101 is accurate; keep it near the abstract.
- Tab. 2: the ng-mR@100 case-level +0.0148 [+0.0064, +0.0232] against the control is positive and excludes zero; the text acknowledges this. Say explicitly that the "no discrimination gain" conclusion is therefore not uniform across protocols (the AUC remains the main evidence).
- Interval coverage: image-clustered intervals cover test-set sampling only, and the baseline AUC left little room ("0.61, 0.57"). The null on TDE's AUC is therefore partly a ceiling-type limitation; a note on the minimal detectable effect is welcome (you give margins; good).
- Reproducibility: the code archive and the scripts (colab_*_stage1/2.py, expand_*_to_standard.py) are listed. The reliance on Colab for all four checkpoints is fine. State the checkpoints' hashes or URLs in the supplement.
- The zero-shot list file is a legacy torch pickle decoded without torch; a short test against the official zR printed values is reported, which is sufficient.
- Significance: for the SGG community the paper's most usable output is a cheap diagnostic (LA control + within-pair AUC) that should accompany any mR claim. I would weight the paper's contribution as primarily evaluation methodology; its claims about TDE are solid, the claims about the other methods are suggestive.
