## Overall Assessment

**Recommendation: Weak Accept (borderline; accept conditional on the claim-scope edits in W1 to W4).**
**Confidence: 4 / 5** (the headline mean-recall splits and the TDE AUC numbers were recomputed from the shipped arrays and match; the proper-score, SGCls/SGDet, CIFAR/text/Waterbirds and simulation results were not re-run).

The decomposition is correct, simple and useful, the paper is unusually candid about where the instrument fails (calibration, operating point, label mix, grouping), and the headline numbers reproduce to the fourth decimal. My reservations are about what the evidence licenses: the "no discrimination" and "PE-Net gains discrimination" conclusions rest on non-detections and on borderline intervals, with equivalence margins fixed after the results were seen and with comparisons between separately trained models whose run-to-run variation is not measured. I found direct evidence in the shipped data that this variation is as large as the AUC effects being interpreted (W2).

## Summary

For two frozen models and a grouping C (the ordered subject-object class pair), the paper scores each relation against the label of a different relation in the same cell. The observed gain splits exactly (Theorem 1, a Yates-type covariance identity extended to hit-rate contrasts) into a group-level part (labels transported within a cell) and a case-level covariance. An O(NK) U-statistic estimator, a Hájek influence function summed within images, simulations, a temperature-plus-class-bias matching protocol for proper scores, an operating-point control (logit adjustment, LA), and a within-cell AUC (four weightings) complete the toolkit. On a re-run of TDE's released MOTIFS checkpoint with an offline replay of the official evaluator, 87% of the +0.0972 mR@50 gain (graph constraint, micro-pooled) is group-level; LA, which changes no within-cell ranking, reproduces +0.0941; the AUC change is not distinguishable from zero under three weightings and negative under the pair weighting. DLFE and IETrans are 94% and 97% group-level, and PE-Net 86% group-level with a small positive AUC change.

## Strengths

- S1. The decomposition is exact, estimator-free in the sense that matters (no nuisance model), and implemented with an asserted identity. I confirmed total = group + case on every split I ran.
- S2. Honest scoping. The paper states that the case-level part credits shortcuts, operating-point movement and label noise (Sec. 1, Sec. 7), that the share depends on the grouping (18% with subject-only cells), on K, on the constraint, and on the label mix, and it reports the Appendix K counter-example (equally fine partitions disagree arbitrarily) and Appendix I (a modest confounder, rho = 0.061, would explain the MLP-SPATIAL case-level part).
- S3. The operating-point observation is a real methodological contribution: a ranking-preserving logit shift produces a significant case-level part of a thresholded metric (+0.0139 for LA1), so a hit-rate case-level part cannot be read as discrimination without a threshold-free companion. Pairing the split with a calibration-invariant AUC is the right design.
- S4. Validation is better than usual: simulations with known truth (null, prior-only, shortcut, calibration-only), coverage at VG150's cell-size distribution, a small-cell stress test that exposes undercoverage (81.5% at two cases per cell), and a disclosed failed bootstrap check (88%).
- S5. Evaluator replay is checked against the official numbers (Table 17), with a unit test against the evaluator's own matching code, including duplicated objects. The control arms (LA, "vis+ctx", "ctx") and the step-wise additive TDE path (Table 22) are well chosen.
- S6. Reporting discipline: primary quantities flagged as post hoc for TDE/IETrans (Sec. 5), a table of every protocol (Table 27), explicit statement that one secondary interval is not a finding, and a number-tracing script.

## Weaknesses

### Major

**W1. "TDE gains no discrimination" is a non-detection supported by post-hoc, externally anchored margins, and the bound varies sevenfold across weightings.**
- Anchor: Abstract ("excludes changes above 0.0038 to 0.028"); Sec. 5 paragraph on 90% bounds and margins; Supp. Q.4, Table 32, `results/sgg_equivalence.json`.
- Problem: the margins are 5/10/25% of the AUC that box geometry buys over the class pair, taken from a different model family on a reconstructed split (Sec. 4/6), and "were set after TDE's results were seen." On the comparison weighting TDE is equivalent only at 25% (margin 0.0081) and not at 10% (0.0032); the per-predicate bound (0.028) is about 40% of the reference gain and the interval is [-0.034, +0.021]. The bound is called "under a tenth" of geometry's gain only for the relation weighting, which is the weighting the authors chose as primary after seeing TDE. The baseline AUC is only 0.57 to 0.61, so the instrument operates close to chance and the paper never reports power: with what probability would this procedure flag a real discrimination change of, say, 0.01 under each weighting? No simulation of the AUC bound under a planted ranking gain on the VG150 cell structure is given (the simulations in Sec. 4 target the case-level proper-score part, not the AUC bound).
- Also: the mean-recall equivalence check against LA uses 25% of TDE's own case-level part over the baseline (+0.0130) as a margin, which is circular (the reference is the quantity being explained), and the paper itself concludes "not equivalent even at 25%" and "too wide to call it zero" for that quantity.
- Fix: (i) anchor the margin to a smallest effect of interest that does not depend on the result, for example the AUC change needed to produce a stated mean-recall case-level change, or the run-to-run AUC variation (see W2); (ii) add a planted-signal power curve for the AUC bound at VG150 cell sizes; (iii) in the abstract state the weighting next to each bound and say "no evidence of a gain, bound X at weighting Y" rather than the 0.0038 to 0.028 range; (iv) drop or reword "equivalent" for any weighting where it holds at only the loosest margin.

**W2. Separately trained comparisons (PE-Net, DLFE, IETrans) are read at a precision the design cannot support; my recomputation shows between-run AUC differences as large as the effects.**
- Anchor: Abstract ("PE-Net ... its within-pair AUC rises (the last two against a separately trained baseline)"); Sec. 5 "Two more methods" and PE-Net paragraphs; Table 1; Supp. Q.5, Q.6; Sec. 7 ("intervals cover test sampling, not training").
- Problem: PE-Net's AUC rise is +0.0050 [+0.0008, +0.0091] under one weighting (relation), +0.0055 [-0.0033, +0.0149] under comparison, and -0.0054 [-0.0435, +0.0256] per predicate; two of four weightings exclude zero, the lower limit is 0.0008, and the comparison is against a MOTIFS baseline from a different release and codebase. DLFE's "gain" (+0.0029, lower limit 0.0001) is, by the authors' own Q.5, entirely due to separate training, because the correction is exactly ranking-preserving ("against the same network before the correction the AUC change is 0").
- Recomputed evidence: two separately trained MOTIFS baselines in the shipped arrays (the TDE release's "none" and DLFE's own baseline "dlfe_base"), which should be exchangeable, differ in relation-weighted within-cell AUC by -0.0060 [-0.0099, -0.0017] (B = 200; comparison weighting +0.0017 [-0.0069, +0.0130]), and in mR@50 by +0.0206 (group +0.0199, case +0.0007). The relation-weighted between-run difference (0.0060 in absolute value) exceeds both PE-Net's +0.0050 and DLFE's +0.0029. A sign-flipping, significant difference between nominally identical architectures means the PE-Net "rises" statement is not distinguishable from training or code-fork variation. The group-level conclusions are safe (effects of 17 to 22 points against a 2-point between-run floor); the AUC conclusions are not.
- Fix: report the between-baseline AUC and mR differences as an empirical noise floor next to every cross-release comparison; state PE-Net's result as "AUC difference within the between-run range" unless it exceeds it; remove "its within-pair AUC rises" from the abstract or qualify it as exploratory; if a PE-Net baseline cannot be released, say that the attribution of the AUC change to the method is not identified.

**W3. The case-level part of thresholded recall is not interpretable on its own, so the "control matches" headline is a coincidence of one protocol, and the proper-score case-level part changes sign with the matching family chosen post hoc.**
- Anchor: Abstract and Sec. 5 ("LA recovers +0.094 of +0.097 ... elsewhere the two differ by 0.02 to 0.15"); Table 2; Table 18; Table 21/27/35; Table 22 and Q.3 (Table 31); Sec. 3 ("The family is fixed before any gain is read") versus Sec. 5 ("declared after the TDE and IETrans results were seen ... a post-hoc choice").
- Problem (a): LA is a global per-predicate shift; TDE removes a cell-varying prior. Removing the frequency branch (cell-varying log-prior shift, no within-cell ranking change, AUC change -0.00001) produces a significant mR case-level part (+0.0053, ng +0.0086) and a matched quadratic part of +0.00915 (Table 22). So LA is not a control for TDE's operating-point movement. TDE-minus-LA is null at K=50 with the graph constraint (-0.0008) and not elsewhere: +0.0148 [+0.0064, +0.0232] at ng-K=100, +0.0072 at ng-K=50. The abstract correctly says the two "differ by 0.02 to 0.15" elsewhere, but the "87%" headline and "matched by the control" in Sec. 5 are K=50 / graph-constraint statements. A cell-wise control (baseline logits minus tau log pi_c(y), with the cell-conditional training prior) would be the matching control.
- Problem (b): the matched quadratic case-level part for TDE is -0.0184 (one shared temperature), -0.00006 (temperature), +0.00445 (temperature plus class bias, p = .001); the log score gives +0.044/-0.093/+0.025. The paper says the sign "is set by the protocol" and selects temperature-plus-bias on the criterion in Sec. 3, but the Sec. 3 text says the family was fixed before any gain was read while Sec. 5 says the protocol was declared after TDE and IETrans were seen. These statements conflict; the criterion is principled, but the claim of prior fixing is not supported.
- Problem (c): under the label-shift path the TDE case-level part goes from -0.00006 to +0.0143 and TDE-minus-LA from -0.0040 to +0.0072 (Table 31): "its null holds at the benchmark's exact mix and nowhere near it." With the proper-score part also protocol-dependent, the only quantity that carries the "no discrimination" claim is the AUC (W1).
- Fix: foreground the AUC and the K/constraint-specific nature of the control match; add the cell-wise-prior control; replace "fixed before any gain was read" in Sec. 3 with the Sec. 5 disclosure; present the proper-score case-level part for TDE as protocol-dependent and uninformative about ranking, not as a result.

**W4. The AUC audits only predicate discrimination given the ground-truth pair, but the mean-recall hit contrast also depends on pair ranking and the foreground/background split.**
- Anchor: Sec. 5 "What that licenses", Supp. O ("Replaying the evaluator": pairs ordered by the top predicate's probability), the AUC definition (log q(y) - log q(z) on renormalised predicate probabilities).
- Problem: under the graph constraint a relation is a hit only if its pair is among the top K pairs of the image, ordered by the stored (unnormalised) top-predicate probability. The case-level part of mean recall therefore also credits (or penalises) changes in which pairs are ranked in the top K, which neither the AUC nor the proper score measures, since both renormalise over the 50 predicates after dropping background. This matters here: in the shipped arrays the background column averages 0.85 of the mass for the MOTIFS baseline and 0.02 for TDE (0.65 for LA1), and TDE moves R@50 from 0.66 to 0.46. The paper's reading "the case-level part is an operating point spent on existing predicate discrimination" is then incomplete: some of it may be pair-ranking change. I did not decompose this; it is untested.
- Fix: either restrict the claim ("no change in predicate discrimination given the labelled pair") or add the missing audit: the mean-recall split with pair ranks frozen at the baseline's (or a within-image AUC of relation-vs-background for annotated pairs).

**W5. Interval validity rests on simulation and on an unresolved bootstrap check; headline intervals use the less conservative Hájek variance.**
- Anchor: Theorem 4 and Supp. F, J (condition (iv); C/sqrt(N*) = 13.3); Table 5; the real-data bootstrap coverage of 88% (`results/sgg_interval_and_diagnostics.json`); main-text intervals "image-clustered" for Tab. 1/2.
- Problem: the theorem does not describe the audited regime (the paper says so). Evidence for nominal coverage comes from simulations with 400 to 500 replicates (Monte Carlo SE about 1 point), and the only test on the real data fails (88%), with the explanation (duplicated images produce zero-kernel within-cell pairs) offered but untested. The jackknife is 9 to 13% wider than Hájek in the paper, and the supplement's own remedy (a "second-order term" or delete-a-group jackknife) is used only in places. The Hájek influence treats the weights w(y) = 1/(K n_y) and the identified set as fixed. Calibration is fitted on half the test images with no validation outputs, so intervals for matched quantities condition on the fitted calibrator (spread over 20 halves is reported but not folded into the intervals).
- What I recomputed (modest; see log): the Hájek SE for TDE's case-level part is 0.00193; a delete-a-group jackknife that refits the full pipeline (eligibility, n_y, w) gives 0.00204 (50 groups, ratio 1.05) and 0.00207 (100 groups, ratio 1.07). A 25-split random half-sample test (z = difference of two disjoint-half estimates over the quadrature of their Hájek SEs) gives sd(z) = 1.07 (mean -0.12). So the Hájek SE looks about 5 to 10% too small on this data, consistent with the paper's jackknife, and the case-level interval [+0.0093, +0.0168] should read about [+0.0088, +0.0173] as the paper's own jackknife says.
- Fix: use the delete-a-group jackknife (refitting weights) as the primary interval for all cell-based quantities and shares; move the 88% result and the half-sample check into the main text as the real-data calibration test; add a validation-based or nested fit of the calibrator, or state that matched-protocol intervals are conditional on the fitted calibrator.

**W6. The conclusion is conditional on the declared grouping, and no AUC or sensitivity analysis is given at a finer grouping for the headline nulls.**
- Anchor: Sec. 5 ("subject classes as cells: 18%"), Table 1 lower panel, Supp. K and I; Prop. 3 and the Appendix K four-point example.
- Problem: the paper reports that coarsening reverses the reading (18% vs 87%), that equally fine partitions can disagree "without limit", and "we do not have a procedure that resolves it." The share under finer cells (position quadrants, size terciles: 90%) is reported for mean recall only. For TDE's ranking claim, the omitted-variable term (Prop. 3, Prop. 4) can hide a non-zero within-pair part at a finer grouping, and the paper concedes this ("a null at the declared phi can hide a non-zero part at a finer grouping"), yet no within-cell AUC is computed at the position or size refinement. The sharp sensitivity bound for MLP-SPATIAL (rho-dagger = 0.061) shows the case-level parts are fragile to unrecorded covariates.
- Fix: report the within-cell AUC change for TDE (and PE-Net) at the class-pair x position and class-pair x size-tercile refinements already built for the share analysis; report the sharp bound for the headline mean-recall case-level part; state in the abstract that "group-level" means "at the class-pair cell".

**W7. Multiplicity and the unplanned-comparison structure are acknowledged but not controlled where positive claims are made.**
- Anchor: Sec. 5 protocol paragraph ("one secondary interval excluding zero is not read as a finding"), Table 27 (about 28 TDE configurations), abstract (pair-weighted AUC "falls by 0.015"; PE-Net "rises"), Sec. 5 zero-shot ("AUC falls ... -0.015, [-0.020, -0.006]").
- Problem: the primary protocol was chosen after TDE and IETrans were seen, which the paper says (the same declaration after DLFE does not rescue PE-Net, which came later and is the paper's only positive discrimination finding). Positive findings that are quoted as results: pair-weighted TDE fall (-0.0153, CI [-0.0249, -0.0004], one of four weightings, upper limit at -0.0004); PE-Net rise (2 of 4 weightings, p about .02 on the primary); DLFE (+0.0029, lower limit +0.0001); the zero-shot AUC fall (relation weighting only; the comparison weighting is [-0.034, +0.0097] in the supplement and covers zero; this weighting carries 1.9% of the weight, the relation weighting 11%). None is corrected for the number of weightings, methods and subsets. The negative findings are not hurt by this; the positive ones are.
- Fix: define a family (methods x weightings x subsets), apply Holm or report adjusted limits, and label as exploratory every positive claim that does not survive; do not use a post-hoc subset (unseen triplets) in the main text with only the weighting that excludes zero.

### Minor

**W8. Zero-shot split and AUC (Sec. 5 last paragraph; Supp. Q.7, Q.11).** In the recomputed JSON (`results/sgg_zeroshot_split.json`) TDE's raw zero-shot recall gain is +0.0339, of which +0.0186 [+0.0142, +0.0230] (50%) is case-level, the largest case-level fraction anywhere in the paper; the main text reports only the control-adjusted +0.0131 and the AUC fall. The AUC subset is defined by whether either label is an unseen triplet in that cell (a label-dependent block selection, legitimate as a stratification but data-dependent in its weight). The main text cites "Appendix Q.11" for this; the analysis is in Q.7 (Q.11 is zero-shot CLIP). Fix: report the raw 50% case share, state the weighting, fix the cross-reference, and restate "not a better ranking" as "no detected improvement in ranking" given the covering interval under comparison weighting.

**W9. Label ambiguity (Q.8, Q.2).** The synonym merge folds "parked on", "walking on", "sitting on" into "on"; that is exactly TDE's largest movement (Fig. 3: parked on 0.000 to 0.886, on -0.558), so the merge removes the boundary under test and cannot show whether TDE's discrimination changed there. Merging also makes the LA control's AUC non-invariant (up to 0.0013), and the three merge schemes are hand-declared. For IETrans the merged loss grows (-0.0238 to -0.0255), which the paper reads as "synonymy does not explain it"; an IETrans model is trained to disagree with the recorded general labels, so an AUC against recorded labels penalises it by construction. Fix: use the multi-label structure already in the data (same image and box pair with several predicates, 0.64% of weighted comparisons) and an annotator-agreement ceiling, or report the "on vs parked on" boundary AUC separately.

**W10. Shares and ratio statistics.** The IETrans share interval upper limit is 1.04 (`results/sgg_share_intervals.json`), and shares are undefined when parts have opposite signs (TDE vs LA: 127%); report differences or the case-level fraction with a bounded interval. The official per-image statistic is not a label-weighted hit rate, so the split is by shuffle (200 permutations) with a jackknife; the paper notes that a plausible shortcut for transport reverses the sign (-0.0216). That sensitivity of the per-image split to a counting convention suggests presenting the micro-pooled split as primary only with this caveat in the main text.

**W11. Randomization p-values.** The cyclic-shift randomization ignores image clusters (a third of relations share an image and cell with another relation and are scored identically), and 499 shifts give a floor of .002 (999 give .001); intervals, not these p-values, should carry the inference (the paper says this, but the main text prints p = .002 and .001 without the floor note).

**W12. CLIP versus geometry (Sec. 6) and seeds.** Main text: the case-level part favours CLIP but top-1, MRR and the primary AUC (-0.0067, [-0.0113, -0.0015]) favour geometry and the comparison weighting reverses the sign. Supp. N.3 (line "its within-cell discrimination is significantly better, not worse") is stronger than the main text; align. The five seeds vary only head initialisation with the training subsample, CLIP features and test set fixed (Supp. P), so "stable across seeds" understates variation.

**W13. Quantity bookkeeping.** Abstract "DLFE and IETrans gain 11 and 21 points": Table 1 gives +0.1112 and +0.2204 (22 points) on the micro-pooled statistic; 21 is the official per-image change (0.3587 - 0.1459 = 0.2128) while DLFE's official change is 0.1039. Use one basis. N is 183,642 in the mean-recall analyses and 183,639 in the proper-score analyses (three unscored pairs); say so in the main text. "Eq. (5)" appears garbled in the PDF text extraction (prefactor).

2. Can you add a cell-wise-prior control (baseline minus tau log pi_c(y)) to the K and constraint grid, so that the case-level part of a thresholded metric has a matched operating-point reference?
3. How much of TDE's mean-recall case-level part survives if pair ranks are frozen at the baseline's? Does TDE change foreground versus background ordering of annotated pairs?
4. What is the power of the within-cell AUC bound to detect a planted ranking gain of 0.01 or 0.02 at VG150 cell sizes, under each weighting?

## Detailed comments

- Theorem 1 and its hit-rate extension are algebra, and the paper says so ("exact, model-free"); the substantive contribution is the estimator's inference and the guardrails. I would not push novelty on the identity and would credit Yates in the abstract; the related-work paragraph already does.
- Prop. 1 shows that the group-level part is "improvement in mean-forecast fit minus change in within-cell dispersion", so "group-level" is not "prior fit". The abstract's wording ("a change in which predicates the model favours within each pair") is the correct reading; the phrase "prior-correcting methods" in the abstract and "a one-line prior shift" in Sec. 5 invite the stronger reading. For hit contrasts under the graph constraint the group-level part is essentially the change in cell-frequency-weighted predicted labels, which is how I read it.
- Estimator: the U-statistic kernel assumes exchangeable cases within a cell; the cross-image restriction check (case-level +0.0130 to +0.0140) is a good robustness test and removes the objection.
- Weights: the Hájek contributions do not include the variability of w(y) = 1/(K n_y) and of the identified set. My refit jackknife suggests the effect on the case-level SE is small (ratio 1.05 to 1.07 relative to Hájek), but it is not zero and should be stated.
- The Sec. 4 simulation coverage figures (94.0%, 96.3%, 97.3%) come from 400 to 500 replicates and are consistent with nominal within Monte Carlo error; they do not exclude 92 to 93%.
- AUC details: ties are handled with exact half-counts and the clipping floor is 1e-300, below every stored value (I verified no zeros or denormals), so the earlier 1e-12 tie problem noted in `wager/rank.py` is not present.
