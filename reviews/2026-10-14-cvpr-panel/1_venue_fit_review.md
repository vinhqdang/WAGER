## Overall Assessment
- Recommendation: Major Revision (CVPR-style: Borderline, leaning Weak Accept on the evaluation-methodology contribution, Weak Reject as currently packaged)
- Confidence: 4/5
- One-paragraph verdict: The central idea (an exact, model-free split of the gain between two frozen models into a within-group-invariant part and a within-group covariance, applied to mean recall) is original, correct on the numbers I checked, and addresses a real blind spot in the SGG debiasing literature. The empirical finding that most of TDE's mR@50 gain survives within-pair label shuffling, and that a one-line logit adjustment matches it at K=50, is useful. Against the CVPR bar, three things hold it back: (1) the headline result is close to forced by the choice of cell for prior-correcting methods; (2) the evidence base is four released single-run PredCls checkpoints from 2020-2023, with no modern method and no seed variance; (3) the exposition is extremely dense (hundreds of numbers, 27+ tables in the supplement, protocol-dependent signs) so that the main claim is hard to separate from caveats. Fit to a vision conference is arguable, since the paper reads as a statistical-methodology paper with an SGG case study.

## Summary of the Paper
The paper proposes Delta-T = Delta-P + Delta-R: the observed score gain between a frozen old and a new model, grouped by a declared discrete cell (here the ordered subject-object class pair), splits exactly into a part that survives scoring each prediction against another case's label from the same cell (group-level) and a within-cell covariance (case-level). It holds for Bregman scores and for label-weighted hit rates such as micro-pooled mean recall. The paper gives a U-statistic estimator in O(NK), image-clustered intervals, a confidence-matching protocol (temperature plus per-class bias), an operating-point control (baseline logits minus the log training prior), and a within-cell AUC. Simulations and controlled VG predictors validate it. Applied to TDE/MOTIFS: 87% of the mR@50 gain is group-level; a logit adjustment recovers +0.0941 of +0.0972; no AUC weighting shows TDE improving discrimination. DLFE and IETrans are 94% and 97% group-level; PE-Net (86%) gains some within-cell AUC. A CLIP-crop predictor is ranked last by aggregates but has the largest case-level gain, and a Waterbirds example is case-level.

## Strengths
- S1. Real, well-posed question: mean recall credits "name rare predicates more often for this pair" and "tell which relation carries which predicate" alike. The decomposition makes this distinction operational.
- S2. Exactness and generality: identity holds for any contrast evaluable at a counterfactual label, so thresholded metrics (mean recall) are covered; the official evaluator is replayed offline and reproduces published numbers (R@50 0.6612, mR@50 0.1459/0.2476).
- S3. Unusual honesty about limits: "What is not claimed" paragraph; small-cell undercoverage (81.5%) reported; protocol-dependent sign of the quadratic case-level part (+0.00445 primary vs -0.00006 temperature-only vs -0.0184 shared temperature) disclosed; post-hoc status of the primary protocol for TDE and IETrans disclosed; multiplicity caveat for Tab. 27.
- S4. Validation where truth is known (simulation, FREQ vs MLP-CLASS case-level exactly 0, geometry vs class-pair), and a convincing "aggregates rank CLIP last, split disagrees" demonstration (Sec. 6).
- S5. Reproducibility: code and cached results per number; I spot-checked sgg_share_intervals.json (TDE share 0.8657, CI [0.826, 0.906]) and sgg_grouping_share.json (class pair 0.0841/0.0130; subject-class 0.0175/0.0794); they match Tab. 1 and the abstract.
- S6. Useful, actionable recommendation for evaluation practice (declare grouping, report both parts, AUC, operating-point control).

## Weaknesses

### Major
- W1. The headline "87% group-level" is largely a consequence of the cell choice for methods whose correction is indexed by the object pair. Anchor: Sec. 5 "Why" and Tab. 1 lower panel. Class pair cells give 87%, subject-class cells give 18% (case-level +0.0794, sgg_grouping_share.json). In MOTIFS-SUM the frequency prior is indexed by the class pair, so TDE's removed term is (nearly) a function of the cell; a large Delta-P is close to mechanical. The paper states the dependence but the abstract and conclusion present 87% as a finding about TDE. Fix: state in the abstract that the share is conditional on a cell the method's own prior is indexed by; add a negative-control model whose correction is not pair-indexed and show the split gives a low share; report the share as a function of grouping granularity in the main text, not only an appendix.
- W2. The strongest substantive claim (TDE gains no discrimination) rests on a pre-chosen, post-hoc margin and on protocol choices with sign flips. Anchor: Sec. 5 AUC paragraph (bounds 0.0038/0.0077/0.028; "margins were set after TDE's results were seen"); Tab. 2 where vs the control the case-level part is +0.0148 [+0.0064,+0.0232] at ng-mR@100 and +0.0072 [-0.0001,+0.0145] at ng-mR@50; mean-recall case-level part vs control "too wide to call it zero" (excludes only >0.0043, a third of TDE's own). The paper's conclusion ("no weighting shows TDE gaining") is an absence-of-evidence statement for K=50 gc only, while Tab. 2 shows the control match is "specific to K=50 under the constraint". Fix: rewrite the abstract and conclusion to scope the claim to gc K=50 and the relation/pair weightings; present the equivalence test with a margin justified independently of the result (e.g., the PE-Net or geometry effect size, declared before the TDE read-out), and report all six protocols in the main text claims.
- W3. Narrow and dated empirical base for a CVPR-level significance claim. Anchor: Intro "What is not claimed"; Sec. 5. Four released checkpoints (TDE 2020, DLFE 2021, IETrans 2022, PE-Net 2023), one training run each, PredCls only for three; DLFE and IETrans/PE-Net are compared against separately trained baselines, so effects are confounded with training-run variance (acknowledged for IETrans). No one-stage / transformer SGG (SGTR, RelTR, SSR-CNN), no VLM-based or open-vocabulary SGG, no recent (2024-26) debiasing methods, although related work cites [25]. Intervals "cover test sampling only". Fix: add at least one modern debiasing method and one end-to-end model if possible, or several seeds of the MOTIFS baseline to put a training-variance band around the "group-level share"; otherwise retitle/scope as a case study of prior-correcting methods.
- W4. The central claim is relative to a diagnostic that itself is protocol-sensitive, which undercuts it as a proposed reporting standard. Anchor: Sec. 4 and 5 (calibration only: -0.488 raw vs -0.0003 matched; temperature vs temperature+bias flips sign; "not invariant to recalibration, operating point or label mix"). The recommendation to report the split "beside mean recall" requires a community to agree on a grouping, calibration family, split fraction for calibration, control model (tau=1 only? la0.5 results exist in results/ but are not discussed in the main text), and AUC weighting. The review burden of 6 protocols, 3 weightings and 2 scores is high; "one secondary interval excluding zero is not read as a finding" shows the authors themselves face a forking-paths problem. Fix: propose one default protocol, justify it before looking at results, and show a sensitivity table for the others in a compact form; discuss tau choice for the control.
- W5. Case-level part is interpreted inconsistently: it is variously "discrimination", "shortcut credit", "operating-point movement", "label noise", "synonymy". For IETrans the AUC fall is attributed possibly to synonymy; for TDE-vs-baseline the covariance +0.0130 is "bought with discrimination the baseline had". The reader cannot tell what a positive or negative case-level part certifies without the control and the AUC, i.e. the decomposition alone does not support the ranking/discrimination claims it motivates. Fix: shorten the interpretive claims attached to Delta-R, and give a decision table (which conclusion each of Delta-R, control, AUC licenses).
- W6. Fit and readability for CVPR. Anchor: whole paper. The main text compresses a theorem, estimator, four proposition references, simulations, six experiments and a Waterbirds/CIFAR aside into 8 pages (very small fonts, Fig. 3 label crowding, Table 1 with six number columns and bracketed intervals). The contribution that matters to the CVPR audience (what did SGG debiasing buy) is buried under statistical machinery; key results sit in appendices (Q.1-Q.11, K, J). A CVPR reader needs a self-contained answer in the first three pages. Fix: move Sec. 6 and the Waterbirds paragraph to supplement or cut; keep one figure (TDE split + control + AUC) as the central result; cut the number density in Sec. 5.

### Minor
- W7. Abstract is overloaded (about 20 numbers, "interval 83-91%", "0.0038 to 0.028", "excludes changes above...") and hard to parse. Fix: three headline numbers and one sentence per claim.
- W8. Micro-pooled vs official per-image mean recall: the theorem does not apply to the official statistic; it is split by a shuffle-based analogue (84% vs 87%). Because the field reports the official statistic, the main table should lead with it or show both.
- W9. Singleton cells excluded (1.1% of relations in TDE audit, 1.0% in controlled study; SGCls only 58% and SGDet 65% matched pairs). SGCls/SGDet conclusions therefore concern a selected subset; coverage bias is not analysed.
- W10. Tail finding is thin: "the baseline recalls no tail predicate; TDE recalls ... at 0.016" and tail change +0.0003, yet the paper's framing is about debiasing; state explicitly that the audited gain is body-predicate gain and that tail effects are unassessed.
- W11. Waterbirds/CIFAR-100-LT/text experiments appear in Sec. 6-7 and supplement without being in scope of the title; the Waterbirds "debiasing gain that is case-level" is a single-run linear-probe result with a favourable cell definition and weakens focus. 
- W12. Relation of case-level part to existing tools: conditional permutation importance, grouping loss and DeLong are mentioned; a quantitative comparison against a simple baseline attribution (e.g., within-pair recall after oracle prior replacement, or mean recall computed after replacing predictions by the pair-conditional mode) is absent, so the added value over simpler diagnostics is argued, not shown.
- W13. Presentation: references [28], [25] are recent arXiv entries; ensure venue versions. Several statements ("the share runs from 77% at K=20 to 86% at K=100") would benefit from a figure.

## Questions for Authors
1. What does the split give for a prior-correcting method whose correction is NOT indexed by the declared cell (e.g., a subject-only or predicate-only reweighting)? Does it reproduce a low share, as a negative control, without relying on subject-class cells?
2. How do conclusions change with tau in {0.5, 1, 2} for the control (results/sgg_recall_split_la0.5_vs_none.json exists)? Why is tau=1 primary?
3. Can you give seed-to-seed variation for the MOTIFS baseline and TDE (even 3 seeds)? How large is the run-to-run variation in the group-level share compared with the 83-91% interval?
4. Would the picture change for one-stage or VLM-based SGG models, where no pair-indexed prior is present? What stops you from at least auditing one released SGTR/RelTR checkpoint?
5. For the AUC equivalence bounds: what margin would you have chosen if declared before seeing TDE? Is the PE-Net AUC gain (+0.0050) a plausible smallest effect of interest?
6. Given the sign dependence on the matching family, what is the principled argument for temperature+per-class-bias over, e.g., full per-cell recalibration or isotonic?
7. How should a benchmark maintainer "declare phi before a leaderboard opens" when subject-class and class-pair give 18% vs 87%?

## Detailed Comments by Section
- Abstract: too dense; lead with the finding (one-line prior shift reproduces TDE's gain under graph constraint at K=50) and the caveat that it is K- and protocol-specific.
- Sec. 1: "Debiasing methods are now judged by how far they raise it" is fair; the claim "none attributes the gain between two models" is credible but should cite conditional-permutation attribution for a model pair if any exists. Contribution (ii) and "map of what it does not separate" is honest; contribution (iii) overstates "audits" given single runs.
- Sec. 2: Good coverage of proper-score decompositions. Relation to Yates and the grouping loss is clear. SGG related work stops at 2023; add 2024-26.
- Sec. 3: Theorem 1 is clean (Eq. 2-3) and the estimator is O(NK); the singleton exclusion and the (n_c-1)/n_c plug-in shrinkage are handled. The "choosing the grouping" paragraph defends the finest grouping as conservative, but Tab. 1 shows the finest-class-pair grouping gives the large share, so "conservative" depends on direction; clarify what is conservative with respect to which claim.
- Sec. 4: Validation is good. The 94.0% coverage with image clustering and 81.5% at two cases per cell is informative; note 96.3% at VG cell sizes shows mild over-coverage. The controlled-predictor study uses a reconstructed split, not canonical; make clear that it is validation, not evidence about published methods.
- Sec. 5: Strongest section but contains the issues in W1, W2, W4. The numbers I checked in results/ match (share 0.8657; grouping-share table). The statement that TDE is "to first order a learned logit adjustment" (99.96% of energy in one vector, +0.75 correlation with log prior) is a clear, valuable observation and arguably more convincing than the AUC argument; promote it. Note that the control matches only at K=50 gc; the abstract's "elsewhere the two differ by 0.02 to 0.15" should be in the same sentence as the match claim.
- Sec. 6: A nice illustration of the split disagreeing with aggregates, but it is a synthetic probe on 100k relations with frozen CLIP crops and is peripheral to the title. The statement "larger case-level part is not the same as better ranking" (top-1 and MRR favour geometry, AUC favours geometry) partly contradicts the framing "the model the aggregates rank last" deserves to be first. Reconcile.
- Sec. 7: Limitations are candid. Add: no training-variance, no modern architectures, and the protocol-sensitivity of the sign of the case-level part as a limitation on the proposed reporting standard.
- Figures/Tables: Fig. 1 is clear. Fig. 3 is cluttered; Tab. 1 mixes primary and secondary rows and lower-panel cells with different statistics; Tab. 2 is useful and should anchor the abstract.
- Anonymity/ethics: no violations noted in the text read; the 40-page supplement is far above typical CVPR supplement norms and should not be required to follow the main argument.
