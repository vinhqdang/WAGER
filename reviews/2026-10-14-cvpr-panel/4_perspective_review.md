# Peer Review 3 (Perspective, R3): CVPR 2027 Submission "What Did Ten Points of Mean Recall Buy?"

## Manuscript Information
- Title: What Did Ten Points of Mean Recall Buy? Separating Group-Level from Case-Level Gains in Scene Graph Generation
- Venue: CVPR 2027 (anonymous submission), review target commit 6b8f1b9

## Reviewer Information
- Role R3, Perspective: robustness / spurious correlation / dataset bias / evaluation (group robustness, Waterbirds, long-tailed recognition, shortcut learning, forecast verification). I read the paper from outside scene graph generation (SGG).

## Overall Assessment
- Recommendation: Weak Accept (borderline-positive). Within SGG the contribution is clear and the TDE finding is interesting. As a general evaluation tool the claims of generality are under-supported.
- Confidence: 3/5. I am confident in the framing and generality critique. I could not re-derive the theorems or check the code.

## Summary
The paper proposes to split the score difference between two frozen models into a group-level part (what survives shuffling labels within a declared cell) and a case-level part (a within-cell covariance between prediction change and own label). The split is exact for Bregman scores and for label-weighted hit rates such as mean recall, and comes with image-clustered intervals. On Visual Genome the authors show that 87% of TDE's +0.097 mR@50 is group-level, that a no-training logit-adjustment control matches it at K=50 with the graph constraint, and that the within-pair AUC shows no gain. DLFE and IETrans are similar; PE-Net gains some within-pair discrimination. A Waterbirds study shows a debiasing method (group reweighting) whose gain is case-level. CIFAR-100-LT and 20 Newsgroups are used to argue breadth.

## Strengths
1. The question is the right one. A mean-recall gain can be a re-weighted prior or better discrimination, and the paper gives a concrete, auditable number for each. The "what is not claimed" paragraph is unusually honest.
2. The negative-control design (a prior shift that changes no within-cell ranking, with the AUC alongside) is the strongest part. It converts a decomposition into a falsifiable test, and the TDE result (the control reproduces the gain at under a tenth of the R@50 cost, 0.0175 vs 0.2023) is compelling.
3. The validation is serious: simulation with a known null, coverage under image clustering, an explicit demonstration that calibration moves the split (-0.488 raw, -0.0003 matched), and the criterion for the recalibration family fixed in advance.
4. The "aggregate ranks CLIP last, split disagrees" example (Sec. 6) shows the tool can say something an accuracy table cannot. The paper then correctly refuses to equate a larger case-level part with better ranking (top-1 and MRR lower).
5. Candour about forking paths: the primary protocol was declared after TDE and IETrans were seen, and secondary intervals are not read as findings.

## Weaknesses

### Major

**W1. The generality claim outruns the evidence; the non-SGG studies do not test the framing on its intended use.**
- Anchor: Sec. 6 last paragraph; Supp. N.5 "Choosing phi" and the 20 Newsgroups section ("Nothing about the estimator... changed"; "a favorable result cannot be attributed to anything specific to vision data").
- Problem: In CIFAR-100-LT and 20 Newsgroups the cell is a superclass or topic, "a function of the label, not of the input", which no model observes. The supplement says itself that these rows "show how the estimator behaves under a label-derived grouping, not an audit of a feature both models condition on". That is the opposite of the construction the paper defines (Sec. 3: "a discrete grouping both models observe"). The headline results there (CB: a +0.00143 total hiding +0.197 and -0.196; DRW case-level 98.6% transported on text) therefore have no stated interpretation as "group-level vs case-level gain". They are a re-weighting-toward-the-balanced-marginal effect, which the supplement itself notes is what long-tailed evaluation rewards. Calling this the same attribution transferring is not supported. The text study is also a small MLP on SVD-TF-IDF with one seed per arm, with the supplement itself calling the fuller protocol "the natural next replication".
- Fix: Either (a) drop the claim of breadth to "the estimator runs" and move these to a clearly labelled sanity section, or (b) provide at least one non-SGG benchmark with an input-observable cell and multiple methods, e.g. CelebA/Waterbirds/MultiNLI/CivilComments with several established debiasing methods (JTT, DFR, GroupDRO, reweighting), multiple seeds, and the declared cell being a recorded attribute. Report which gains are group-level and whether that matches known findings.

**W2. Waterbirds is a single model pair and its case-level result is mostly calibration; the headline contrast with TDE is weaker than Sec. 6 states.**
- Anchor: Sec. 6 "A debiasing gain that is case-level"; Supp. Tab. 39, Q.10.
- Problem: (i) One linear probe pair on frozen CLIP features, one seed. (ii) The within-cell AUC moves from 0.9723 to 0.9754 (+0.0031); the supplement concedes the gain "is mostly better-calibrated confidence ... rather than a different ranking of the birds". By the paper's own rule that ranking claims rest on the AUC, "debiasing buys better discrimination inside each group" (main text) overstates what the supplement shows. (iii) The case-level part also flips sign (-0.0456) under recalibration of ERM alone and changes from +0.0914 to +0.1138 when both are matched, so the sign/size is protocol-dependent here as it was for TDE; the main text gives the unmatched +0.0914 without the matched caveat. (iv) The cell is the background scene, but the community's object of interest is the 4 (label x background) groups and the worst one; the split says nothing about the worst group. GRW's worst-group gain (0.643 to 0.854) is not decomposed by group, so the reader cannot see whether the gain occurs where the spurious correlation fails.
- Fix: State in the main text that the Waterbirds AUC gain is 0.003 and that the case-level part is largely confidence-in-own-label. Report seeds. Add per-(label x background) contributions of Delta-P and Delta-R, or a worst-group split, and show how the split relates to worst-group accuracy, which is the metric the robustness literature uses.

**W3. A reader from the robustness literature needs the group/case split to be related to worst-group accuracy and to the shortcut it is meant to expose; the paper explicitly cannot separate a shortcut from real discrimination.**
- Anchor: Sec. 1 "What is not claimed"; Sec. 4 simulation (+0.041 for an unrecorded within-cell shortcut); Sec. 5 "How to read the split".
- Problem: The motivating story is bias (a prior driving the benchmark). But the case-level part "credits any signal varying inside a pair, a shortcut nobody recorded included", and the group-level part is not "bias" either (it can be a legitimate improvement in where the model puts mass, as the paper says). So neither part maps to "spurious vs genuine", and the title's promise ("what did ten points buy") is answered only as "movement of the within-pair predicate prior". That is useful, but a robustness reader will ask what the split says about subpopulation shift, which is not addressed. In Waterbirds the group-level part is -0.0277 for a method that mostly removes a shortcut. Here a negative group-level part would be read as the bias being unlearned, whereas in SGG a positive group-level part is read as prior fitting. The sign convention is task-dependent and nothing in the paper tells a user how to read it.
- Fix: Add a short section, with Waterbirds as the worked example, on how to read the two parts in a spurious-correlation setting, and on what the split cannot tell (shortcut vs signal). Consider a worst-cell version (restrict the split to a stratum) and report how the split changes with the (label, attribute) cell.

**W4. Relation to existing decompositions is argued but not demonstrated; novelty over Yates/Murphy and permutation importance is a bookkeeping claim.**
- Anchor: Sec. 2 "Proper-score decompositions" and Proposition 2.
- Problem: The paper states that the case-level part of one model is a conditional permutation importance of the non-grouping input, and that the case-level part of a gain is the difference of the two models'. That makes the contribution an exact additive accounting and inference for paired differences, plus the thresholded-metric treatment. That is real but modest, and the paper never shows a case where Murphy resolution, the grouping loss, or conditional permutation importance (per model, then subtracted) would lead to a different conclusion from the split. The paper says Yates' covariance moves under monotone recalibration while Murphy resolution does not; this is presented as a feature, then handled by an elaborate recalibration protocol whose result changes sign (the TDE matched case-level part is +0.00445 under the primary protocol, -0.00006 with temperature alone, -0.0184 with a shared temperature). So the "exact" quantity is protocol-sensitive in exactly the case the paper uses as its headline.
- Fix: Add a small table for TDE, PE-Net and Waterbirds giving the Murphy resolution change (or the per-model conditional permutation importance difference) next to Delta-R. Say plainly which conclusions are shared and which differ. If a calibration-invariant quantity (resolution, within-cell AUC) already carries the TDE verdict, state that the split is a decomposition of a metric rather than a new diagnostic of discrimination.

**W5. The reporting protocol is heavy, underspecified for adoption, and its recommended elements disagree.**
- Anchor: Conclusion ("We propose reporting ... the declared grouping, both parts with intervals, the within-group AUC and the operating-point control"); Sec. 5 "What that licenses"; Table 2.
- Problem: Results depend on the choice of cell (87% with class pairs, 18% with subject classes, 90% with refined cells; "equally fine partitions can disagree"), on K and on the graph constraint (77% to 86%; control matches TDE only at K=50 with the constraint, trails by 0.08-0.15 without), on micro vs per-image pooling, on the recalibration family, and on the AUC weighting (three weightings with different verdicts, margins "set after TDE's results were seen"). The authors themselves say that with Tab. 27's many configurations one significant secondary interval is not a finding. A benchmark user cannot be asked to adopt a protocol whose verdict depends on these choices. The main-text headline (87%) is the most favourable of the protocol grid for the "TDE is just a prior shift" reading, and the "control matches TDE" claim is specific to one K. The main text says so, but the abstract and conclusion state the headline more strongly than Table 2 supports ("three prior-correcting PredCls checkpoints ... almost entirely group-level").
- Fix: Provide a one-page reporting template with fixed defaults (cell, K, constraint, pooling, recalibration, AUC weighting), a ready-to-run package, and a sensitivity table as a required output. Say in the abstract that the control matches TDE at K=50 with the graph constraint only. Consider a pre-registered protocol on a fresh method (DLFE was the one prospectively evaluated; say that this is the only confirmatory case).

### Minor / moderate

**W6. Fundamental assumption worth challenging: the cell is the object pair, but the object pair is not a neutral, model-independent unit.**
- Anchor: Sec. 3 "Choosing the grouping"; Sec. 5 protocol; subject-only cells giving 18%.
- Problem: The decomposition tells you how much of a gain is explained by what is constant within the declared cell, so the answer is "relative to the finest grouping you chose to defend". The paper treats the finest benchmark-defined grouping as conservative, but in SGG the object labels come from the same annotation process, and for SGDet/SGCls the pair is a model prediction (58-65% of relations have a matched pair). In those settings the cell is itself noisy, and the split is on the matched subset, a selection that favours easy relations. Also the claim that TDE is "to first order a learned logit adjustment" rests on a +0.75 correlation of one shared vector with the log prior; the per-pair variation is ignored.
- Fix: Report SGCls/SGDet in the main table with the selection-bias caveat made explicit, and state that the "group" in these settings means the predicted pair.

**W7. Single released run per method, intervals cover test sampling only.** Four methods and one checkpoint each; the PE-Net and IETrans comparisons are against a separately trained baseline (IETrans: no released baseline), so training-run variance and method effect are confounded. Fix: state in the abstract that PE-Net/IETrans contrasts are between separately trained models; add seed variance where any checkpoint can be retrained (the supplement does this for CLIP: seeds 0.00413-0.00474).

**W8. Clarity for a non-SGG reader.** The abstract and Sec. 1 depend on SGG jargon (PredCls, graph constraint, mR@50, MOTIFS-SUM, TDE) and on a dense chain of numbers (14.6 to 24.8; 87%, 84%, 18%; +0.094 of +0.097; 0.0038 to 0.028). The key idea (shuffle labels within a cell) is clear only after Fig. 1 and Eq. (2). The abstract has about 25 numerals. Sec. 4-5 mix several protocols. Fix: lead the abstract with the idea and two numbers; move the AUC bounds to the body; add a two-line toy example (two classes, two cells) with the numbers worked out; define "group-level" once as "survives within-cell label shuffling".

**W9. Terminology risks being misread.** "Group-level" will be read by the robustness community as "per-demographic/subpopulation group", and "case-level" as "per-sample"; neither matches the definition (a cell of objects sharing a feature). Also "Group-level is not a lesser gain" co-exists with the title's framing ("what did ten points buy"). Fix: rename to something like "cell-prior part" and "within-cell part", or add a short terminology note and avoid "group" language in Waterbirds sections where groups have an established meaning.

**W10. Ethical/practical implications are not discussed.** The paper recommends that a benchmark declare the cell before a leaderboard opens, and notes that the case-level part credits shortcuts. The risk that a "case-level" label is read as "better, fairer or more robust" is not addressed, and neither is the consequence of metric gaming (methods tuned to maximize the case-level part, which grows with an unrecorded shortcut, +0.041 in simulation). Fix: add a limitations/impact paragraph: the split is descriptive, a high case-level part is not evidence of robustness, and the split can itself be optimized.

**W11. Statistical details.** Intervals assume non-dominating image clusters, and coverage drops to 81.5% with two cases per cell; VG150 cell sizes cover 96.3%, but the singleton exclusion (1.1-1.2% of relations) and the 5,044 of 7,127 cells carrying 98.9% of relations show a heavy-tailed cell distribution. Hypothesis tests (p = .001, .002) sit beside a many-configuration grid (Tab. 27). Fix: report coverage under the actual cell-size distribution with heavy-tailed cells, and apply a multiplicity statement to the headline items.

## Questions to the authors
1. For Waterbirds, what share of the +0.0914 case-level part remains if the models are matched to the same calibration and AUC is held fixed? Is the result stable over seeds and with DFR/JTT instead of GRW?
2. Is the split by (label x background) group computable, and does the case-level part concentrate in the worst group?
3. Does conditional permutation importance (per model, subtracted) agree with Delta-R on TDE, PE-Net and Waterbirds? If so, what does the split add beyond paired inference?
4. With a label-derived cell (CIFAR, text), what is the interpretation of "group-level"? Is it anything beyond a measure of re-balancing toward the test marginal?
5. Under which cell would the authors expect the TDE conclusion to reverse, other than the subject-only cell (18%)? Is there a principled way to choose?
6. Could a method be tuned directly to the case-level part, and what would the AUC and top-1 then show?

## Detailed comments
- Abstract: give the control result with its scope ("at K=50 with the graph constraint"); the sentence on AUC bounds is hard to parse ("excludes changes above 0.0038 to 0.028, and with every predicate pair weighted equally it falls by 0.015").
- Sec. 3 "Estimator": the (nc-1)/nc shrinkage point (Proposition 5) is a good detail; say how often cells with nc < 5 occur.
- Sec. 5: "the share is undefined where the total is negative" is correct; consider a normalization that is defined for all signs, given that the ng-mR rows are the ones where the control and TDE diverge.
- Sec. 6: the sentence "Its whole deficit is group-level" is a clean example; keep it, and move it earlier as motivation, since it is the most transferable result for a non-SGG reader.
- Sec. 7: add the heavy-tail cell caveat and the potential for gaming the case-level part.
- Fig. 3 and Tab. 1: add one summary line stating, for each method, whether the within-cell AUC changed and in which direction; at present the reader must assemble this from the text (TDE: no, DLFE: +0.0029, IETrans: -0.0238, PE-Net: +0.0050).

## Bottom line
A careful, honest and exact accounting tool whose SGG findings are persuasive, and whose negative-control logic is the real contribution. The case that it is a general-purpose diagnostic is not yet made: the two non-vision-grounded studies use label-derived cells, and the one robustness study is a single model pair whose discrimination gain is 0.003 AUC. Strengthen W1-W3 and W5 and this becomes a clear accept for a venue audience beyond SGG.
