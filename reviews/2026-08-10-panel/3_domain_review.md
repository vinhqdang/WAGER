# Reviewer 2 — Domain Expertise (SGG / Long-tail / Evaluation)

> Simulated review panel, 2026-08-10. Persona: CV researcher in SGG, unbiased SGG evaluation, VQA bias, long-tailed recognition. Focus: literature coverage, novelty calibration, experimental adequacy for a CV audience.

## Summary of contribution as positioned by the authors

The paper proposes WAGER, a pairwise gain-attribution procedure: given two frozen probabilistic predictors and a declared discrete "prior feature" φ (e.g., the subject–object class pair in VG PredCls, the superclass in CIFAR-100-LT), it decomposes the proper-score gain of the new model over the old into a "prior-transported" component (recoverable by within-cell label transport) and an "instance-alignment" residual (an antisymmetric U-statistic with cluster-robust CIs and a stratified randomization test). Positioning: existing audits compare a model to a fitted frequency baseline (FREQ, VQA-CP-style prior probes) or intervene on training (mean recall, TDE, class-balanced losses), whereas WAGER audits the *gain between two submitted systems* directly, with an exact finite-sample identity and no fitted nuisance model. Experiments: simulations; five hand-built predictors on VG150 PredCls; a frozen-CLIP crop model on a 100k-relation subsample; and CE/CB/DRW ResNet-32 runs on CIFAR-100-LT (imbalance ratio 100).

## Literature audit

**What is covered well.** The statistics-side lineage is handled carefully and honestly: Murphy (JAM 1973), DeGroot–Fienberg (The Statistician 1983), Bröcker (QJRMS 2009), Gneiting–Raftery (JASA 2007), Hoeffding (1948), conditional permutation testing (Berrett et al., JRSS-B 2020). Section 2.5 (`2related.tex`, lines 85–109) explicitly concedes that ΔR is "a resolution difference, not a new quantity" — this candor is a strength and correctly identifies the nearest prior art. Core SGG anchors are present: Motifs/FREQ (Zellers et al., CVPR 2018), KERN (Chen et al., CVPR 2019), VCTree (Tang et al., CVPR 2019), TDE (Tang et al., CVPR 2020), Li et al. (BMVC 2022) on metric pathologies; VQA-CP (Agrawal et al., CVPR 2018) and Teney et al. (NeurIPS 2020) on Goodharting OOD splits; Torralba–Efros (CVPR 2011) and Geirhos et al. (Nat. Mach. Intell. 2020) for shortcut learning; V-information (Xu et al., ICLR 2020; Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2022) and conditional probing (Hewitt et al., EMNLP 2021).

**Missing key references (CV side).**

1. **Xu et al., CVPR 2017** ("Scene Graph Generation by Iterative Message Passing") — the paper uses "the standard VG150 PredCls regime" (`4experiments.tex`, line 42) without citing the paper that defined the VG150 split and the PredCls protocol. This is a mandatory citation.
2. **Lu et al., ECCV 2016** ("Visual Relationship Detection with Language Priors") — origin of language-prior exploitation in relationship detection and of *zero-shot recall*, which is directly on-topic (see mischaracterization #2 below).
3. **The SGG debiasing method families beyond TDE** are absent: CogTree (Yu et al., IJCAI 2021), BGNN with bi-level resampling (Li et al., CVPR 2021), DLFE (Chiou et al., ACM MM 2021), IETrans (Zhang et al., ECCV 2022), GCL (Dong et al., CVPR 2022). Instead, §2.1 jumps from TDE (2020) to three 2024 works, one of which (`sun2024unbiased`) is an arXiv preprint. A CVIU audience will read the SGG coverage as two anchors plus recency filler.
4. **VQA-CP debiasing methods**: RUBi (Cadene et al., NeurIPS 2019), Learned-Mixin/LMH (Clark et al., EMNLP 2019), CSS (Chen et al., CVPR 2020), CF-VQA (Niu et al., CVPR 2021) — the paper claims VQA prior-exploitation as a target domain in the abstract/intro but cites only VQA-CP itself, Teney et al., and two recent arXiv preprints (`xu2025qirl`, `kuang2024debiasing`).
5. **GQA-OOD (Kervadec et al., CVPR 2021)** — the closest existing *evaluation-methodology* work in vision-and-language: it stratifies VQA evaluation by answer frequency *within question groups*, i.e., a prior-conditioned sliced evaluation. It must be cited and distinguished; its absence weakens the novelty positioning precisely where it needs support.
6. **Long-tail canon**: §2.2 (lines 30–33) attributes "class-balanced re-weighting, logit adjustment, feature fusion" to `ma2024geometric`, `li2026pih2t`, `luo2025rebalancing` — but the canonical references are Cui et al. (CVPR 2019, class-balanced loss — *which is in the bib and used in the experiments but not cited here*), Cao et al. (NeurIPS 2019, LDAM-DRW — same situation), **Menon et al. (ICLR 2021, logit adjustment)** — naming "logit adjustment" without citing this paper is a clear error — and **Kang et al. (ICLR 2020, decoupled representation/classifier, cRT/τ-norm)**, whose post-hoc classifier rebalancing is the single most relevant baseline for this paper's story (a purely post-hoc prior correction should be nearly all ΔP).

**Mischaracterizations.**

1. `2related.tex` lines 9–12 and `1intro.tex` lines 12–14: recent SGG work "still reports gains as a single aggregate score rather than attributing them to prior fitting versus instance-specific evidence." This is a strawman as written. Since ~2019–2020 the community standard is to report mR@K alongside R@K, per-frequency-group recall (head/body/tail), and often zero-shot recall zR@K — Tang et al. 2020 report zR@K *specifically* to detect frequency-prior exploitation, and HiKER-SGG (cited as an offender) reports mR@K prominently. These are stratified reports, not single aggregates. The correct, defensible claim is narrower: none of these metrics *decomposes the score difference of a fixed model pair with an exact identity and inference*. Say that instead.
2. Zero-shot recall is never discussed anywhere in the manuscript, yet it is the community's existing instrument for the exact question WAGER poses on VG (does the gain survive removal of the training-frequency prior?). A domain reviewer will consider this omission disqualifying for the related-work section as it stands.
3. Bibliography hygiene: six 2025–2026 arXiv preprints (`charpentier2026`, `vieira2026`, `kim2026`, `cai2026`, `sun2024unbiased`, `brown2025benchmark`, `xu2025qirl`, `kuang2024debiasing`) carry substantial positioning weight. Where a peer-reviewed classic exists (Menon et al., Kang et al., Cadene et al.), it should displace the preprint.

## Novelty calibration

Nearest existing work, in order of proximity: (a) Bröcker's conditioning-set-explicit reliability/resolution decomposition — the paper itself concedes ΔR is a resolution *difference*; the claimed delta is the paired-contrast construction (one-pass decomposition of the gain vector, exact finite-sample identity, attenuation correction, cluster-robust inference), which I believe is genuinely not in the meteorological or CV literature as a package. (b) Conditional probing (Hewitt et al. 2021) — family-level, not model-pair, correctly distinguished in §2.3. (c) GQA-OOD and head/body/tail mR@K slicing — prior-stratified *evaluation* without decomposition or inference; not cited (GQA-OOD) or not engaged (mR@K slicing). (d) Ablation-based attribution — the paper's own VG experiment is itself a feature-ablation design, and CV readers will note that controlled ablation already "attributes gains to feature sets" when you can retrain; WAGER's real added value is for *frozen third-party* model pairs, and the paper should foreground that use case. (e) Paired forecast-comparison inference (Diebold–Mariano-style tests) gives significance but no prior/alignment split.

Verdict: the technical delta is real and §2.5 calibrates it well. But §2.1 and the intro inflate the gap by understating what mR@K stratification, zR@K, and GQA-OOD already provide. The novelty claim survives contact with the literature; the *motivation* as written does not.

## Experimental adequacy for a CV audience

- **Simulations (§4.1):** Accepted. Clean power/Type-I design; appropriate.
- **VG150 PredCls with FREQ/MLP variants (§4.2–4.4):** Accepted as *validation of the estimator's behavior* (the algebraic zero for MLP-CLASS is a nice touch, and the MLP-SPATIAL vs MLP-CLASS sign-split is instructive), but **rejected as evidence about SGG**. These are purpose-built probes, not scene graph generators: accuracies of 0.65–0.67 on PredCls with no visual features, no context aggregation, no message passing. The paper's motivating claim is that benchmark *submissions* inflate scores via prior fitting — yet not one published SGG model is audited. The Scene-Graph-Benchmark codebase ships checkpoints (MOTIFS, VCTree, Transformer, each with/without TDE and with reweighting variants) whose softmax outputs are exactly the four arrays WAGER needs. Auditing whether TDE's celebrated mR@K gain shows up as ΔR > 0 (and whether reweighting gains are mostly ΔP) is the paper's obvious killer experiment, and its absence will be the first thing every SGG reviewer writes.
- **Metric bridge:** SGG leaderboards rank by R@K/mR@K under graph constraint, not by quadratic score on probability vectors. No analysis connects ΔR to recall-based rankings. A CV reader cannot currently act on WAGER's output.
- **PredCls only:** defensible (matched relation sets across models), but the restriction and its implications for SGCls/SGDet are never stated.
- **Frozen-CLIP experiment (§4.5):** The matched-100k-subsample design is genuinely careful, and the finding (aggregate down 0.047, alignment up 0.006) is the most interesting result in the paper. However, CLIP crops of subject/object boxes are a weak stand-in for the union-box Faster R-CNN features every real SGG model uses; the experiment demonstrates the estimator, not the state of SGG.
- **CIFAR-100-LT (§4.6):** One imbalance ratio (100; standard is 10/50/100), one seed, three methods, and no post-hoc rebalancing baseline (τ-norm/cRT, logit adjustment) — the latter being the perfect falsification test since a purely post-hoc prior correction should register as almost pure ΔP. More seriously: CB at 0.2627 top-1 is drastically below published CIFAR-100-LT (IR 100) numbers for CB-weighted ResNet-32 (~0.39, Cui et al. 2019, who apply CB with tuning; Cao et al. also report CB-from-init figures near baseline, not 10 points below). CE at 0.3662 is also below the usual ~0.38–0.39. The "more instructive" cancellation case (lines 337–351) may therefore be an artifact of an untuned/unstable CB run rather than a property of class-balanced losses. The internal consistency check against Prop. attenuate (line 373–380) is a nice touch and I accept it.
- **VQA:** promised by the abstract, intro, and §2.1, delivered nowhere; §5.2 (lines 56–59) admits this. Given that VQA-CP is one of the three motivating domains, one VQA-CP v2 experiment (UpDn vs UpDn+RUBi or +LMH, φ = question type) is a reasonable demand, not a stretch goal.

## Strengths

1. §2.5's positioning against the classical decomposition is unusually honest and precise; the paper knows exactly what its delta is (paired-contrast construction, attenuation correction, inference) and does not oversell the mathematics.
2. The MLP-CLASS algebraic-zero result and the sign-split cases (SPATIAL vs CLASS on VG; CB vs CE on CIFAR) are pedagogically excellent demonstrations of why signed two-channel reporting beats a bounded ratio.
3. The frozen-CLIP result — worse aggregate, significantly better instance alignment — is a genuinely novel observation with real implications for how VG150 punishes pixel-driven models, and the matched-subsample control is exactly right.
4. Statistical craftsmanship (image-clustered CIs, randomization diagnostics, sensitivity to score/granularity/cell-size, singleton reporting rather than smoothing) exceeds the CV-venue norm.
5. Cross-domain transfer to CIFAR-100-LT with three φ choices, including the head/medium/tail tier analysis (Table cifar-tier), which lands where the long-tail literature predicts.

## Weaknesses

1. **No audit of real SGG systems** (`4experiments.tex` §4.2–4.5). Fix (priority 1): run WAGER on released Scene-Graph-Benchmark PredCls checkpoints — MOTIFS, VCTree, Transformer, each ± TDE, plus a reweighted variant — and report whether debiasing gains are ΔR or ΔP. This requires no training, only cached softmax outputs; there is no excuse at journal scale.
2. **Strawman about SGG reporting and silence on zR@K / mR@K slicing** (`2related.tex` lines 9–12; `1intro.tex` lines 12–14). Fix: reword the claim to "no exact pairwise decomposition with inference"; add a paragraph distinguishing WAGER from zR@K, head/body/tail mR@K, and GQA-OOD; ideally show empirically what WAGER detects that a zR@K delta on the same model pair does not.
3. **Citation errors in the long-tail passage** (`2related.tex` lines 30–33). Fix: cite Cui et al. CVPR 2019 and Cao et al. NeurIPS 2019 where their methods are named (both already in ref.bib), add Menon et al. ICLR 2021 for "logit adjustment" and Kang et al. ICLR 2020 for decoupling; demote the 2024–2026 works to "recent examples."
4. **Missing mandatory citations**: Xu et al. CVPR 2017 for VG150/PredCls (`4experiments.tex` line 42); Lu et al. ECCV 2016; GQA-OOD (Kervadec et al. CVPR 2021); RUBi/LMH/CSS/CF-VQA if VQA positioning is retained.
5. **VQA promised, never tested** (abstract lines 1–4; `1intro.tex` lines 4–11; `5discussion.tex` lines 56–59). Fix (priority 2): one VQA-CP v2 decomposition (UpDn vs RUBi or LMH, φ = 65 question types) — or remove VQA from the abstract and intro framing.
6. **CIFAR study too thin and the CB run looks broken** (`4experiments.tex` §4.6, lines 331–351). Fix (priority 3): ratios 10/50/100, ≥3 seeds with variance, add logit adjustment and cRT/τ-norm (post-hoc rebalancing as the pure-ΔP falsification case), and either reproduce published CB accuracy or explicitly document why the run diverges from Cui et al. before building the paper's flagship "cancellation" narrative on it.
7. **No bridge from ΔR to recall-based metrics** (§4.3–4.4). Fix: report R@50/mR@50 for all VG model pairs alongside the decomposition and discuss when ΔR and Δ(mR@K) agree/disagree; state the PredCls-only restriction and its SGCls/SGDet implications explicitly in §5.
8. **Preprint-heavy positioning** (`ref.bib`, eight 2024–2026 arXiv entries). Fix: substitute peer-reviewed canonical works where they exist; retain preprints only where no published equivalent exists.

## Scores (0–10)

- Literature coverage: **5** (statistics lineage excellent; CV lineage has real errors and load-bearing omissions)
- Novelty vs field: **7** (the paired-transport decomposition is genuinely new; positioning overstates the gap it fills)
- Experimental adequacy: **4** (statistically rigorous but toy models throughout; the one experiment the community will demand — auditing published SGG checkpoints — is absent, and the CIFAR CB baseline is suspect)
- Domain contribution: **6** (a useful instrument the field lacks, if revised to touch real systems)

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The method is a real contribution and the frozen-CLIP finding alone shows it can surface things aggregate metrics hide. But as submitted, a paper whose thesis is "SGG/VQA/long-tail benchmark gains should be audited" audits no published SGG model, no VQA model, and one possibly misconfigured long-tail baseline at a single imbalance ratio, while mischaracterizing the field's existing stratified metrics (mR@K slicing, zR@K) and omitting the nearest evaluation-methodology predecessor (GQA-OOD) and canonical long-tail citations (Menon et al., Kang et al.). Weaknesses 1–3 are necessary conditions for acceptance; weaknesses 5–6 would move me from acceptance-with-reservations to endorsement. All required experiments are inference-only or small-scale training and are feasible within a normal revision cycle.
