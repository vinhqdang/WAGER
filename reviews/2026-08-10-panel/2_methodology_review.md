# Reviewer 1 — Methodology & Statistical Rigor

> Simulated review panel, 2026-08-10. Persona: mathematical statistician (proper scoring rules, U-statistics, forecast verification, randomization inference). Verified proofs line-by-line and cross-checked code and results files.

## Summary of the method as I understand it

WAGER audits the proper-score gain between two frozen classifiers by matching test examples that share a declared discrete prior feature φ and evaluating the score contrast H_i(y) under transported (crossed) labels within each cell. This yields an exact finite-sample identity ΔT̂_c = ΔP̂_c + ΔR̂_c, where the alignment residual ΔR̂_c is an order-two U-statistic with a symmetric pair kernel that is antisymmetric under label crossing; under the quadratic score ΔR_c equals twice the summed within-cell covariance between the probability *change* and the label indicator, i.e., a model-pair resolution term. Inference is via a Hájek influence function with image-cluster-robust sandwich variance, plus a secondary cell-stratified cyclic-shift randomization test.

## Theory audit

I verified every proof line by line (manuscript/3method.tex, manuscript/7appendix.tex).

- **Theorem 1 (population/covariance identities).** Correct. ΔT_c − ΔP_c = Σ_y Cov(H_X(y), 1{Y=y} | c) follows immediately from the independent-coupling definition of ΔP_c. The quadratic-score reduction is correct: the label-constant term ||q₁||² − ||q₀||² drops because Σ_y 1{Y=y} = 1. The cell-constant ⇒ ΔR_c = 0 corollary is exact and is what makes the MLP-CLASS row a genuine algebraic control.
- **Theorem 2 (finite-sample identity + U-statistic).** The counting argument (each H_i(y_i) appears in n_c−1 pairs; crossed terms enumerate ordered pairs) is correct and the identity is deterministic, as claimed. **One imprecision:** unbiasedness of ΔR̂_c for ΔR_c is asserted "under exchangeable sampling within the cell" (3method.tex, Thm 2; 7appendix.tex §A.2). E[A₁₂] = ΔT_c − E[H₁(Y₂)] equals ΔR_c only if the two draws are *independent* (i.i.d. within cell), because ΔP_c is defined via an independent label Y′. Exchangeability alone (e.g., positively dependent within-cell draws) does not give E[H₁(Y₂)] = the independent-coupled ΔP_c. State i.i.d.-within-cell, or redefine the estimand as the two-distinct-draw expectation (finite-population version).
- **Proposition 1 (attenuation).** Algebra checked; exact. The (n_c−1)/n_c shrinkage and the dataset-level Σ(n_c−1)ΔR̂_c consequence both follow. This is a genuinely useful result — it formally retires the projection/evaluation split of the rejected precursor rather than merely asserting the redesign is simpler.
- **Proposition 2 (coarsening).** The per-label law-of-total-covariance proof is correct. **Two prose problems** in 3method.tex lines 90–96: (i) "vanishes identically once φ is refined to make *it* constant on every non-vanishing fine cell" — the referent is unclear; the between-cell term vanishes when E[H_X(y)|C] (or Pr(Y=y|C)) is constant across fine cells inside c̄, which should be stated as such; (ii) "the finest cell that remains non-singleton for every example" mixes a sample-level notion (singletons) into a population statement. Also, "never remove genuine within-cell alignment" is true only *on average* (the first term is E[ΔR_C | c̄], not a per-cell preservation); add that qualifier.
- **Edge cases.** Handled well: n_c = 1 excluded and reported; label-homogeneous cells give transported = observed, hence ΔR ≡ 0 (correctly flagged as an artifact when φ = fine label, 4experiments.tex §5.7). Ties/degenerate priors pose no problem since the identity is deterministic. One unflagged edge case: when the gain vector is exactly cell-constant, the U-statistic is fully degenerate and the sandwich SE is exactly 0, producing the CI [0.00000, 0.00000] in Table 2 — see inference audit.
- **Influence function (Eq. 15).** The formula ψ̂_i = 2Ā_i − ΔR̂_c − ΔR̂ is *asserted, never derived*. I re-derived it: for the n_c-weighted aggregate over multinomially assigned cells it is correct (per-cell Hájek linearization θ_{C_i} + 2(g₁(Z_i) − θ_{C_i}) − θ, estimated by plug-in), and I confirmed the code's ψ has exactly zero mean. But this is the paper's primary inference tool and it deserves a proof in Appendix A, including a statement of what the dataset-level estimand is once cells with n_c = 1 are excluded (the aggregate is a ratio estimator over a randomly selected subpopulation; the selection is ignored, which is fine at 99% coverage but should be said).
- **O(NK) algorithm and Appendix B pair-mean identity.** I verified Ā_i = ½[H_i(y_i) + (O_c−H_i(y_i))/(n_c−1) − P_i − (G_c(y_i)−H_i(y_i))/(n_c−1)] is exactly the mean of A_ij over j ≠ i, and that wager/antisymmetric.py lines 208–219 implement precisely this. Correct.

## Statistical inference audit

- **Cluster-robust CI.** The one-way CR variance with G/(G−1) correction (7appendix.tex Appendix B; `_cluster_interval`, antisymmetric.py:125–143) is standard and correctly implemented; with G = 27,955 clusters normal criticals are fine. However, **coverage of this interval is never validated**: the controlled validation (§5.1, experiments/antisymmetric_simulation.py) checks only the randomization test's type-I error (0.030 at α=.05 over 200 runs — acceptable) and estimator centering. There is no simulation with image-cluster dependence, no simulation in the VG-like regime (thousands of skewed cells, many n_c = 2, K = 50), and no empirical coverage check of the primary Hájek/cluster interval anywhere. This is the largest inferential gap.
- **Randomization test.** Valid as stated: cyclic shifts form a subgroup, uniform sampling includes the identity, and the plus-one estimator is a valid Monte Carlo p-value (Hemerik–Goeman style); code (antisymmetric.py:280–340) matches, and the one-sided orientation is documented. The paper is appropriately honest that image-level dependence violates the relation-level exchangeability null and demotes the test to a diagnostic.
- **p-value usage.** With B = 499, the minimum attainable p is 1/500 = .002; *every* significant row in Tables 2, 4, 5 reports exactly .002, i.e., the resolution floor. Report "p ≤ .002" or raise B. The CB rows' p = 1.000 (one-sided test, effect at the opposite extreme) is explained in the caption but a sign-appropriate or two-sided report would be cleaner. No multiplicity correction over the ~15 reported comparisons; since CIs are primary and effects are 5–20 SEs from zero, this is immaterial in practice but should be acknowledged.
- **Degenerate row.** MLP-CLASS vs FREQ shows a 95% CI of [0.00000, 0.00000]. The statistic is deterministically zero by construction (fully degenerate kernel), so a normal-theory CI is not the right object here; footnote it as exact-by-construction rather than presenting a zero-width sandwich interval.
- **Effect sizes vs p.** Good practice throughout: signed estimates with CIs are primary; the share ρ̂ is correctly de-emphasized (undefined for ΔT ≤ 0, can exceed 1) and the ρ̂ = 1.63 case is discussed rather than thresholded.
- **Conditional scope.** All CIs quantify test-set sampling uncertainty *conditional on the single trained instance of each model* (one seed per model everywhere; 7appendix.tex §D). That is a legitimate estimand for auditing two frozen submissions, but sentences like "deferring the re-weighting evidently does more than re-rank the classes" (§5.7) generalize to the training *method*, for which one seed per arm is insufficient. Either add 3–5 training seeds for the CIFAR pair (cheap: ResNet-32, 80 epochs) or scope such claims explicitly.

## Experimental design audit

- **Synthetic controls (§5.1).** The monotone-recovery design (oracle mixture, β grid, 40 reps) and the permuted-noise null are adequate for the estimator's centering and the randomization test, but under-ambitious: one alternative family, 24 balanced cells of 50, K = 5. Missing: a null/alternative with (i) cluster-dependent examples to validate the *primary* CI, (ii) VG-like cell-size skew dominated by n_c = 2 cells, (iii) a prior-only-improvement alternative (ΔP > 0, ΔR = 0) with heteroscedastic H to confirm no leakage into ΔR̂ beyond the algebraic cell-constant case already unit-tested.
- **VG study (§5.2–5.4).** The five-model ladder is well designed — MLP-CLASS is a true algebraic negative control, FREQ+OVERLAP a useful sign-caution case. The sensitivity analysis (score, granularity, min cell size) is genuine and pre-linked to Prop. 2. Two design caveats: the min-cell-size rows change the estimand (both ΔT and population), which the text should mark by reporting retained-relation coverage per row, not just cell counts; and MLP-SPATIAL+ differs from MLP-SPATIAL not only in capacity but also in training epochs (20 vs 15 in run_sgg_wager.py:64–71), contradicting "merely increases capacity" (§5.2).
- **CIFAR-100-LT (§5.7).** Sound and genuinely informative; the φ-choice discussion (fine label ⇒ vacuous audit) is exactly right. Internal consistency is excellent — I verified the tier-weighted means reproduce the aggregate ΔR to 1e-5, and I independently recomputed the covariance-identity cross-check on the committed data: plug-in covariance −0.19530, rescaled by 500/499 → −0.195693, matching the estimator's −0.19569 exactly. Note, though, that this "external check" verifies a deterministic algebraic identity (Prop. 1) on real arrays; calling it a confirmation of the *theory* on real models (§5.7) overstates it — it confirms the implementation. Main weakness: single training seed per arm (see above).
- **Matched-subsample CLIP study (§5.6).** The design decision to retrain all three predictors on the same seeded 100k subsample is correct and well argued — it is the right answer to the size/feature confound. Remaining confounds worth one sentence each: (i) input dimensionality (1088 vs 76 vs 64) with a *single shared* hyperparameter setting (lr 1e-3, wd 1e-5, 15 epochs) — no evidence the conclusion is robust to head hyperparameters, and shared settings may favor one feature scale; (ii) potential overlap between VG imagery (Flickr/COCO) and CLIP's pretraining corpus is unaddressed; the "pretraining had already recovered from pixels" interpretation should note it. The chain-consistency of results (ΔR(V vs C) = ΔR(V vs S) + ΔR(S vs C), 0.00641 + 0.01023 = 0.01664 ≈ 0.01665) holds as the linearity of the kernel requires.
- **Claim/evidence match (abstract/intro).** All headline numbers in 0abstract.tex are traceable to tables and to committed results files; contribution claims 2–4 are proved, claim 5–6 supported. No overclaim beyond the two flagged phrasings.

## Reproducibility audit

Strong overall — unusually so.

- Every number I checked in Tables 1–6 matches results/antisymmetric_results.json, cifar_lt_results.json, vg_visual_results.json, antisymmetric_ablations.json, antisymmetric_simulation.json to the printed digit (including accuracies, CIs, cell counts, singleton counts, type-I 0.030, null mean −8.97e−5).
- Code matches the paper's formulas exactly: Eq. (12) transport, Appendix B pair mean, Eq. (15) influence, cluster sandwich, cyclic-shift test with plus-one correction. All 10 tests in tests/test_antisymmetric.py pass (0.17 s), including direct numerical checks of Props. 1–2 (the coarsening test against an exact discrete population is a nice touch).
- Seeds fixed everywhere I looked (sgg_models.py, colab_*.py, drivers; per-pair randomization seeds deterministic).
- Gaps: (1) The main-table VG models' training details (hidden sizes, epochs 12/15/20, embedding dim 32, lr, wd) appear only in code — Appendix D documents only the matched-subsample and CIFAR runs. (2) The §5.7 covariance cross-check (−0.19530) is not produced by any committed script; I had to reproduce it ad hoc (it checks out). (3) FREQ is described as "simply the training-count distribution conditioned on the class pair" (§5.2), but sgg_models.py implements add-α smoothing with cell → subject-marginal → global backoff — a materially different baseline definition on rare/unseen test pairs. (4) The cached probability artifacts (sgg_dists.npz, cifar_lt_results.npz, vg_visual_models.npz) are load-bearing for exact reproduction, and no release/availability statement covers them.

## Strengths

1. The finite-sample identity, attenuation proposition, and coarsening proposition are all correct, tight, and assumption-light; the attenuation result in particular is a clean formal justification for a design decision (no projection fold) rather than decoration.
2. Exceptional paper–code fidelity: formulas, estimators, and every reported number are traceable and internally consistent (additivity chains, tier aggregations, cross-check identities all hold to rounding).
3. The MLP-CLASS row is a genuine algebraic negative control, and the FREQ+OVERLAP and CLIP rows show the method reporting signed, unflattering results — the design does not stack the deck.
4. Honest limitations section: conditional-on-φ interpretation, asymptotic CI, randomization-null caveat, singleton coverage are all stated by the authors themselves.

## Weaknesses (numbered)

1. **Primary CI never coverage-validated.** The cluster-robust Hájek interval is the paper's main inferential statement, yet §5.1 validates only the secondary randomization test; there is no simulation with cluster dependence or VG-like cell-size skew. *Fix:* add a simulation arm with image-clustered relations and skewed cells reporting empirical 95% CI coverage (target file: experiments/antisymmetric_simulation.py; report in §5.1).
2. **Influence function asserted, not derived; aggregate estimand under singleton exclusion undefined.** Eq. (15) in 3method.tex §3.7 has no proof, and the dataset-level target after conditioning on n_c ≥ 2 is never written down. *Fix:* add an appendix derivation of ψ̂_i under multinomial cell membership and define the identified-subpopulation estimand explicitly.
3. **Theorem 2's "exchangeable sampling" is insufficient for the stated unbiasedness** (3method.tex Thm 2, 7appendix.tex §A.2): ΔP_c uses an independent label coupling, so unbiasedness needs i.i.d. within-cell draws. *Fix:* one sentence — assume i.i.d. within cell, or restate the estimand as the two-distinct-draw expectation.
4. **Single training seed per model, everywhere.** CIs are conditional on one trained instance; method-level conclusions (esp. DRW vs CE, §5.7, and the CLIP claim in §5.6) need either multi-seed replication or explicit conditional scoping. *Fix:* 3–5 seeds for the CIFAR arms (cheapest), and a scoping sentence for VG.
5. **Randomization p-values reported at their resolution floor** (.002 with B = 499, uniformly across all significant rows; Tables 2, 4, 5), with no multiplicity acknowledgement. *Fix:* report "p ≤ .002" or B = 9,999, and one sentence on multiplicity.
6. **Degenerate CI [0.00000, 0.00000]** for MLP-CLASS vs FREQ (Table 2) presented as a normal-theory 95% CI when the kernel is fully degenerate. *Fix:* footnote as exact-by-construction.
7. **Paper/code discrepancies:** FREQ smoothing+backoff undocumented (§5.2 vs sgg_models.py); MLP-SPATIAL+ epoch difference contradicts "merely increases capacity" (§5.2 vs run_sgg_wager.py:64–71); main-table training hyperparameters absent from Appendix D; the §5.7 covariance cross-check script is not committed. *Fix:* document all four; commit the cross-check.
8. **Garbled coarsening prose** (3method.tex lines 90–96): unclear referent of "it", sample-level "non-singleton" in a population claim, and "never remove genuine within-cell alignment" missing its on-average qualifier. *Fix:* restate the vanishing condition precisely (between-cell term is zero when E[H_X(y)|C] or Pr(Y=y|C) is constant within each coarse cell).
9. **CLIP study robustness:** single shared head hyperparameter setting across feature sets of very different dimensionality, and no mention of possible VG/CLIP-pretraining image overlap (§5.6). *Fix:* a small hyperparameter sensitivity grid for the three -S heads, plus one caveat sentence.
10. **"Confirmed the theory on real trained models" overclaim** (§5.7): the cross-check verifies a deterministic identity, i.e., the implementation. *Fix:* reword.

## Scores (0–10)

- **Theoretical soundness: 8** — proofs correct and tight; docked for the exchangeability imprecision, the underived influence function, and the undefined aggregate estimand under selection.
- **Statistical validity: 7** — inference machinery is correct and correctly implemented, but the primary interval is never coverage-validated, p-values sit at the Monte Carlo floor, and single-seed conditionality is not fully scoped.
- **Experimental rigor: 7** — well-designed controls and two-domain validation with honest signed reporting; docked for the simulation's narrow regime, single seeds, and the untested head-hyperparameter sensitivity in the CLIP study.
- **Reproducibility: 8** — every reported number traces to committed artifacts and passing tests; docked for undocumented training details of the main VG models, the undocumented FREQ smoothing, and the uncommitted cross-check.

## Recommendation

**Minor revision.** The mathematical core — the exact decomposition, the U-statistic residual, the attenuation and coarsening propositions, and the covariance link to the resolution term — is correct; I verified the proofs, the code, and the numerical record independently and found them consistent to the last printed digit. The required changes are real but bounded and require no new theory: a coverage simulation for the primary interval (W1), an appendix derivation of the influence function and estimand (W2–W3), multi-seed or explicitly scoped training claims (W4), p-value floor and multiplicity language (W5–W6), and documentation fixes (W7–W10). None threatens the paper's conclusions; all are needed before the inference claims can be taken at the level of rigor the theory sections promise.
