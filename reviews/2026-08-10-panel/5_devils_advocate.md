# Devil's Advocate Report

> Simulated review panel, 2026-08-10. Role: construct the strongest case against the paper; detect logical fallacies, cherry-picking, overgeneralization. Cross-checked every manuscript number against `results/*.json` and read the estimator code and git history.

## Strongest Counter-Argument

WAGER's formal machinery is sound, but the paper sells an interpretation the estimator cannot identify. By the paper's own covariance identity (Eq. 10, `3method.tex`), ΔR is the within-cell covariance change between *probability movements* and labels. That quantity is not invariant to recalibration: temperature-sharpening a frozen model — same argmax, same ranking, zero new instance information — mechanically produces positive "instance-alignment gain," and softening produces the exact signature the paper celebrates as its headline CIFAR discovery: prior gain up, alignment down, totals cancelling. The manuscript concedes the ingredients (CE is "badly overconfident," mean max-prob 0.764 at 0.366 accuracy; CB's "softer predictions recover on calibration roughly what they surrender on alignment") yet never runs the one control that would separate confidence scaling from lost discrimination — WAGER on a temperature-scaled CE against CE itself. Until that control exists, the "+0.19713 prior gain cancelling a −0.19569 alignment loss" in the abstract is an uninterpretable mixture, and the controlled validation (Section 5.1) is designed to miss this: it injects only oracle signal and a null, never a calibration-only alternative.

The empirical base compounds the problem. Every audited "model" is a toy the authors trained themselves, one seed each, on PredCls — the easiest SGG regime — while the motivating scenario (auditing benchmark submissions) is never once instantiated on a published system, though VG150 checkpoints are freely available. And the abstract's framing claim — WAGER "separates two re-weighting schedules that aggregate scores cannot" — is contradicted by the paper's own Table 4 and accuracy figures (ΔT 0.00143 vs 0.06606; accuracy 0.2627 vs 0.3874): aggregates separate them trivially; only the *composition* claim is defensible, and that composition is calibration-confounded.

## Issue List

1. **CRITICAL — logic/evidence — `3method.tex` §3.2 (Eq. 10), `4experiments.tex` §5.7, `0abstract.tex`.** ΔR is not invariant to monotone recalibration, so "instance-alignment gain" conflates confidence scaling with genuine re-ranking. Under the quadratic score, ΔR = 2Σ_y Cov(Δq_y(X), 1{Y=y}|c); if q₁ is a temperature-rescaled q₀, Δq_y covaries with labels wherever q₀ is informative, yielding nonzero ΔR from a pair containing *identical* instance information. Softening an overconfident model yields exactly the CB signature (ΔP up, ΔR down, near-zero total). The text supplies the confound itself ("The baseline is badly overconfident… the softer predictions of the re-weighted model recover on calibration roughly what they surrender on alignment") but reports −0.19569 as "per-instance discrimination" paid away, with only the accuracy drop as partial corroboration of direction, not magnitude. The narrative claims in abstract and conclusion ("genuine improvement," "what it paid for that was per-instance discrimination") are unidentified without a recalibration control. Fix is cheap: run WAGER on recalibrated-CE vs CE, or report a within-cell-recalibrated variant of ΔR. (Note: the CLIP result plausibly survives this attack — see Observations.)

2. **MAJOR — framing/evidence — `0abstract.tex` (lines 19–22), `2related.tex` §2.2 ("whose aggregate scores would not distinguish them at all").** Factually false as written. The two schedules are separated by every aggregate the paper reports: ΔT 0.00143 vs 0.06606, accuracy 0.2627 vs 0.3874 (Table 4; `results/cifar_lt_results.json` confirms). The "concealment" holds only for one metric (Brier total) on one comparison (CB vs CE), while the accuracy table printed in the same section exposes CB as a 10-point degradation no evaluator would miss. The defensible claim — aggregates cannot reveal *composition* — is much weaker than the sentence sold in the abstract.

3. **MAJOR — evidence/generalization — `4experiments.tex` §5.2–5.7, `experiments/sgg_models.py`.** Every audited model is self-generated. The paper motivates WAGER as auditing "the gain between two submitted systems" (`1intro.tex`), yet audits zero published systems: five author-built toys (FREQ, one-layer indicators, small MLPs) and three author-trained ResNet-32s. VG150 PredCls has abundant public checkpoints (Motifs, VCTree, TDE-debiased models, etc.) whose audit would test the method where its claims live; PredCls is also the easiest SGG regime, and SGCls/SGGen are untouched. Every empirical "finding" is therefore a property of pairs the authors constructed to exhibit it.

4. **MAJOR — generalization — `4experiments.tex` §5.7, `7appendix.tex` §D.** Single training seed per configuration ("seed is identical across runs"; one run per model in `results/cifar_lt_results.json`), yet conclusions are stated at the *method* level: "deferring the re-weighting evidently does more than re-rank the classes"; DRW's gain "is concentrated on rare classes." The reported CIs quantify only test-resampling noise for the one frozen pair; variability of the decomposition across training seeds — the relevant uncertainty for any method-level sentence — is unmeasured. n=1 per training method.

5. **MAJOR — logic/framing — `1intro.tex` (lines 3–19), `4experiments.tex` §5.7.** The meaning of the "prior channel" silently flips between domains. The intro defines the shortcut as fitting *training* annotation regularities ("category frequency dominates"). But ΔP is generated by transport over the *test* cell's label histogram — which on CIFAR-100-LT is balanced. CB's +0.19713 "prior gain" is therefore movement *toward the balanced test histogram*, i.e., *undoing* the training-frequency prior — the opposite of the shortcut the paper's framing indicts. Calling two-thirds of DRW's gain "recoverable from the class-frequency prior alone" (§5.7) insinuates shortcut exploitation for what long-tail evaluation deliberately rewards.

6. **MAJOR — logic/framing — `0abstract.tex`, `3method.tex` §3.3, Tables 3 and 4.** The headline quantity is φ-relative, and materially so: 0.01439 → 0.02181 (class-pair → subject-only, VG); −0.19569 → −0.25430 and DRW share 0.36 → 0.47 (superclass → global/tier, CIFAR). The abstract states unqualified point estimates ("+0.00641," "one-third instance alignment") as if properties of the model pair. The "finest defensible partition is conservative" argument is rhetoric, not theorem: Prop. 2's between-cell term is sign-indefinite (the paper shows both signs empirically), and no canonical finest φ exists — CIFAR's superclass is one arbitrary point between the degenerate fine label and the global cell. The Discussion concedes conditionality; the abstract does not carry the qualification.

7. **MAJOR — evidence — `4experiments.tex` §5.1.** The controlled validation lacks discriminant-validity alternatives. It tests (a) oracle-mixture signal, guaranteed by construction to raise ΔR, and (b) a shuffled null. It never tests the confusable cases the method must distinguish to support its interpretation: a calibration-only change (Issue 1) and an unrecorded within-cell shortcut (acknowledged as a threat in §3.2 and §6.1, never simulated). The validation suite validates the arithmetic, not the interpretation.

8. **MINOR — framing — `1intro.tex` line 48 vs. lines 51–78.** "WAGER makes five contributions" followed by six enumerated items.

9. **MINOR — evidence — Tables 2, 5, 6.** p = .002 is the floor of a 499-draw randomization (1/500); reporting it row after row presents a resolution limit as a measured quantity. The uniform .002 column adds no discriminating information across rows.

10. **MINOR — evidence — `7appendix.tex` §D ("ResNet-32 pair").** Stale: "Both models…", "the only difference between the two runs is the per-example loss weight," while three models are compared; DRW's defining hyperparameter (re-weighting start epoch 60, per `results/cifar_lt_results.json`) is stated nowhere in the appendix, only "final twenty epochs" in §5.7 prose. Reproducibility spec is incomplete for the paper's most-featured model.

11. **MINOR — framing — `4experiments.tex` §5.7 ("an external check on the theory").** Theorem 1 and Prop. 1 are algebraic identities holding for arbitrary inputs; agreement between two code paths verifies the implementation, not the theory. "Confirmed on real trained models" is puffery.

12. **MINOR — framing — `1intro.tex` line 80, `3method.tex` §3.6, `4experiments.tex` §5.4.** Revision-history leakage: "the rejected precursor of WAGER," "Unlike the rejected projection-based formulation," "The redesign is important in what it removes." A fresh reader is asked to credit the paper for beating an invisible unpublished draft; the acronym itself (WAGER) is a leftover of the abandoned betting method (git: `4fb4ef9`, `4e3b3fb`), with "Reasoning" retained in the name while the text disclaims it ("used operationally").

## Ignored Alternative Explanations or Paths

- **Recalibration as the driver of ΔR** (Issue 1) — the dominant unaddressed alternative for the CB result; testable in minutes with the repo's own cached arrays (`data/cifar_lt/cifar_lt_results.npz` + `decompose_gain`).
- **CB's collapse as an optimization pathology, not a trade.** Effective-number weights at β=0.9999 from random initialization are known to destabilize from-scratch training (why Cui et al. and Cao et al. defer or warm up). "What it paid for prior fit was discrimination" presumes a trade; simple underfitting under extreme weights predicts the same table.
- **CLIP's prior collapse.** Attributed to 1024 CLIP dims "dominating" 64 class-embedding dims (§5.6) — asserted, not tested. Alternatives: 100k-subsample starving frequency estimation for the highest-capacity input; feature scaling/optimization. One ablation (CLIP features + explicit frequency logit) would separate these.
- **Unrecorded within-cell shortcuts in VG.** Acknowledged abstractly (§3.2, §6.1) but never probed: box-derived quantities correlate with predicate priors within class pairs (e.g., relative scale for "wearing" vs "on"), so MLP-SPATIAL's ΔR may partly be a finer annotation prior that φ fails to record — precisely the parasitism-on-φ worry, conceded in principle, unquantified in practice.
- **The stratified-comparison path.** Table 6's tier breakdown is largely stratified ΔT reporting — the repo even contains the old stratified analysis (`results/stratified.json`); the paper never measures what WAGER adds over "stratified score deltas plus a recalibration step," the cheapest competitor.

## Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- **Benchmark maintainers**: WAGER needs full per-instance predictive distributions from both systems ("requires released logits or probabilities," §6.1); leaderboards overwhelmingly collect top-k predictions. Deployment feasibility for the motivating use case is never discussed.
- **SGG practitioners**: no audit of any model a practitioner uses; no SGCls/SGGen; no guidance on what ΔR = +0.006 licenses one to conclude or decide (the decision threshold was deliberately removed; nothing replaces it).
- **Long-tail/calibration community**: no engagement with calibration metrics (ECE) or logit-adjustment theory, which analyze the same CE-vs-reweighting phenomenon and would immediately raise Issue 1.
- **Reviewers of the prior submission**: the paper argues extensively against its own rejected precursor rather than against the strongest published alternative (subtracting two Bröcker-style resolution estimates with a bias correction), which is dispatched in one paragraph (§2.5).

## "So What?" Test verdict

**Partial pass.** The one result a stratified/mean-recall report genuinely cannot produce is the CLIP sign reversal: a model worse on every aggregate showing *positive* within-cell alignment gain (+0.00641) — that is invisible to stratified means and is the paper's real contribution, and the calibration confound cuts *against* it rather than explaining it (a softer model is penalized by the covariance term, yet still wins). The O(NK) exact identity with no fitted nuisance is a clean, reusable tool. But on CIFAR the practically actionable content (CB is broken; DRW helps the tail) is fully recoverable from accuracy plus per-tier score deltas that the paper itself tabulates, and the marginal value of ΔR over "stratify, recalibrate, compare" is asserted, never measured. As a CVIU paper, the theory is elementary (rearranged sums, law of total covariance — the paper admits ΔR is "a resolution difference, not a new quantity") and the vision content is toy-scale, leaving the contribution real but thin.

## Observations (Non-Defects)

- **The cherry-picking attack fails.** I checked every manuscript number against `results/*.json`: all match exactly (VG table, sensitivity table, CIFAR tables, VG-visual table, accuracies, CIs, per-tier values). The extra files in `results/` (`phase1_results.json`, `sgg_results.json`, `stratified.json`, `ablations.json`) are leftovers of the abandoned e-value method, not suppressed runs of the current one. No evidence of selective reporting within the runs that exist.
- **The math holds.** I verified the algebra of Theorem 2, Prop. 1, and Prop. 2 (Appendix A); `wager/antisymmetric.py` implements the paper faithfully, asserts the decomposition identity at runtime, and the leave-one-out transport, Hájek influence, and cluster variance match the stated formulas.
- **The "tautology" attack partially fails.** The MLP-CLASS zero *is* algebraic, but the paper says so itself ("The zero is worth dwelling on: it follows algebraically from the construction"), and the CIFAR fine-label degeneracy is explicitly pre-empted (§5.7 "Choosing φ"). The authors do not pass constructions off as discoveries.
- **The matched-subsample design (§5.6) is genuinely careful** — retraining all three models on the same 100k relations to unconfound features from data size is a control many published papers skip, and the appendix documents failure modes (QuickGELU, crop dedup, 3 missing images) with unusual candor.
- **The formal statements are honest even where the narrative overreaches**: §2.5 openly derives WAGER from the classical Murphy/Bröcker decomposition; §6.1 states the φ-conditionality and the shortcut caveat plainly. The paper's defects live in the abstract, the interpretive prose, and the missing controls — not in hidden claims or wrong proofs.
