### Strengths (brief)
The Appendix O replay reproduces the official evaluator (0.6612/0.1459 baseline, 0.4588/0.2476 TDE). The simulations check the split in both directions: null recovery, signal recovery, clustered coverage. The paper reports its own failures candidly: the −0.488 temperature artefact, the 81.5% coverage on two-case cells, and the 0.061 robustness value. I recomputed Table 1 (TDE total 0.0972, group 0.0841, case 0.0130; share 0.866, CI [0.826, 0.906]) from the JSON and it matches.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
Suppose a scholar who thinks TDE did something real. They would say the paper builds a decomposition whose main quantity, the case-level part ΔR, is not allowed to mean what it says, and then draws a conclusion that depends on that quantity.

Look at how ΔR is treated. When it is small (TDE vs the logit-adjusted control, −0.0008), it is cited as evidence of no new discrimination. When it is large and positive for a pure operating-point shift (logit adjustment itself, +0.0139), it is "existing discrimination spent at a new operating point". When it is large for CLIP (+0.00464), it is "real covariance, not better ranking". When TDE's quadratic ΔR flips between −0.0184 and +0.0440 across calibration protocols, it is "protocol-dependent". An estimand that must be overruled in each of these cases has no stable interpretation. The load-bearing evidence is then the within-pair AUC, and that is a null. The AUC change is +0.0026 [−0.0028, +0.0086] when weighted by comparison and −0.0015 when weighted by relation. There is no equivalence margin. The same weak AUC separates CLIP from geometry by +0.0085 [−0.0062, +0.0214], which the paper itself calls not a detected difference.

The "ten points" also exist only under the graph constraint, the rule that allows one predicate per pair. Under that rule a per-pair prior shift is nearly the only lever, so a group-level share near 90% is close to guaranteed. Without the constraint, TDE's mean recall falls (0.3260 to 0.2981), and its case-level part against the control is positive and excludes zero at k=100 (+0.0148 [+0.0064, +0.0232]) and nearly so at k=50. Within-pair AUC is about 0.57 for the baseline and TDE alike, so the case-level share is small for any method on VG150 and the audit may be unable to tell methods apart. Only two checkpoints are audited, one run each. The paper never shows a scene-graph method for which the split gives a large case-level share of mean recall. A fair conclusion is that TDE mostly re-weights predicates within pairs under one protocol; it does not show that debiasing "buys" nothing.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. The 87% may be a property of mR@50 under the graph constraint on VG150, not of TDE: any method that moves mass towards rare predicates within pairs would show it, and no method with case-level discrimination is included to show the split can return a different answer.
2. TDE's change may redistribute discrimination (gain between some predicate pairs, loss between others); the paper concedes this under label shift but treats it as a null at the benchmark mix.
3. A finer or different ϕ (geometry bins, image-level context) could move part of the group-level gain into the case-level part.
4. IETrans's lower within-pair AUC could come from training differences (relabelling, reweighted loss, separately trained baseline) rather than from the method.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Method authors of TDE and IETrans, who would argue the gains were meant to be prior shifts. Not elaborated here (Perspective Reviewer's scope).

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes that case-level covariance or discrimination among relations sharing an object pair is the quantity a good debiasing method should improve, and that its absence is a negative. VG150 within-pair labels are largely a matter of annotator predicate choice, so the benchmark may not reward case-level gain at all.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The 81.5% interval coverage on two-case cells affects only 1.0% of audited relations in VG150; the candour is welcome.
- The Table 1 numbers match the JSON; I found no numerical errors in the headline.
