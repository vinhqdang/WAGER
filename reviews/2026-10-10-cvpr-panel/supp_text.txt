         D. Coarsening and unrecorded covariates                                                                                                   988

         Appendix N.4 reports that replacing the class-pair cell with a coarser subject-only cell changes the estimated covariance                 989
         gain. The following result gives that sensitivity an exact population-level mechanism rather than treating it as an empirical             990
         curiosity.                                                                                                                                991
            The first term is the coarse cell’s share of the fine-grained covariance gain; the second is a between-cell covariance, over           992
         the finer cells nested inside c̄, between each fine cell’s typical gain at label y and its label-y frequency. It is not sign definite,    993
         so coarsening ϕ can inflate or deflate the credited covariance gain, and it vanishes exactly when either quantity is constant             994
         within each coarse cell. This is why the finest defensible ϕ is the conservative default: coarsening adds cross-cell prior                995
         structure into ∆R, while the finer partition’s gain is preserved on average through the first term. The proof is a per-label              996
         application of the law of total covariance (Appendix E).                                                                                  997

1304     L. Terminology
1305     M. Why the transported gain is not prior fit
1306     Proposition 1 splits ∆Pc into two brackets, and only the first is prior fit: it is positive exactly when the new model’s average
1307     within-cell forecast sits closer to the cell’s label frequencies. The second has nothing to do with the prior. It rewards the
1308     new model for making less dispersed predictions inside the cell, at a fixed mean forecast, so ∆P can be large and positive
1309     when neither model’s prior fit has changed at all. One cell suffices: with two labels and frequencies ( 12 , 12 ), let q0 alternate
1310     between (1, 0) and (0, 1) in a pattern uncorrelated with the label and let q1 predict ( 12 , 12 ) throughout. Both mean forecasts
1311     equal the cell frequencies exactly, so the first bracket is zero, and the whole observed gain of 12 is transported, produced
1312     entirely by the collapse in dispersion. The channel is therefore the reliability-and-sharpness channel of the classical partition,
1313     not a prior-fitting channel, which is why we name it for the operation. Two consequences: a claim that some gain is “mostly
1314     prior-fitting” does not follow from a large ∆Pd alone, and Eq. (8) explains why confidence matching moves ∆P       d so sharply,
1315     since a temperature change is almost purely a change in dispersion.

         N.5. Long-tailed image classification                                                                                                  1424

         Sections 4–6 are all instances of one task (predicate classification on an ordered object pair). Section 2 argued that long-tailed     1425
         image classification poses a structurally identical question: a class-frequency prior can be fit better without any per-instance       1426
         improvement. We test this directly on CIFAR-100-LT [8], an exponential-imbalance (ratio 100) subsample of CIFAR-100’s                  1427
         training split with the original, balanced test split retained for evaluation. We train ResNet-32 [17] classifiers that differ only    1428
         in their per-class loss weighting: a vanilla cross-entropy baseline (CE); class-balanced effective-number re-weighting applied         1429
         from initialization (CB) [8]; and the same re-weighting deferred to the final twenty epochs (DRW) [3]. Optimizer, schedule,            1430
         augmentation, epoch budget, and seed are identical across runs (Section H).                                                            1431


         Choosing ϕ. A declared grouping variable has to predict the label without determining it, and this second benchmark makes              1432
         the constraint unusually easy to violate. Were ϕ taken to be the fine class label itself, every cell would be label-homogeneous,       1433
         within-cell transport would have nothing to cross, and ∆R d would come out identically zero – an algebraic artifact wearing the        1434
         appearance of a finding. CIFAR-100’s own 20 coarse superclasses avoid this, each gathering five fine labels into a cell of 500         1435
         balanced test images, which is structurally the situation of a VG class-pair cell holding several predicates (Figure 5). Unlike a      1436
         class pair, though, a superclass is a function of the label, not of the input, and no model observes it at test time: the superclass   1437
         rows show how the estimator behaves under a label-derived grouping, not an audit of a feature both models condition on.                1438
         The input-grounded row is the global cell, which every model trivially observes. Alongside the superclass audit we report              1439
         two further declared priors: the trivial coarsening to a single global cell, which audits against the global class marginal and so     1440
         instantiates Proposition 3 in a second domain, and a partition into head, medium and tail training-frequency tiers. CIFAR-100          1441
         test images are independent, unlike the several relations VG draws from one photograph, so no cluster grouping is required             1442
         and the plain Hájek interval of Section 3 applies directly. One semantic point matters in this domain: transport uses each            1443
         cell’s test label histogram, which on CIFAR-100-LT is balanced. A positive ∆P here therefore records movement toward                   1444
         the balanced test marginal – undoing the training-frequency skew – which is precisely the adjustment long-tailed evaluation            1445
         is designed to reward; a transported gain is a description of where score was earned, not an accusation (Section 7).                   1446

1457     Under the quadratic score the covariance channel is a covariance between probability movements and labels (Eq. (3)), and
1458     a monotone recalibration moves it: softening an overconfident model transfers score mass from the covariance channel to
1459     the transported channel while adding no information about any individual image. The baseline invites exactly this concern,
1460     assigning a mean maximum probability of 0.764 while being correct on only 0.3662 of the test set. We therefore repeat the
1461     audit in a calibration-matched regime: each model’s temperature is chosen by held-out likelihood on a calibration half of the
1462     test split (T ∗ = 2.85 for CE, 3.51 for CB, 2.61 for DRW), the decomposition is computed on the disjoint audit half, and the
1463     whole procedure is repeated over twenty random splits (Table 10). The regime’s necessity is quantified by its control row:
1464     recalibrating the baseline alone – a transformation carrying no instance information – moves the channels by +0.380 (prior)

1467     ance deficit does not shrink but widens, to −0.23991 (0.00491): the model re-weighted from initialization has genuinely

1678     Q.2. Merging near-synonymous predicates

1679     A case-level part near zero could reflect predicates that no model can tell apart from the image: on, sitting on and standing
1680     on; wearing and wears; has, with and of. We apply two merges declared before looking at their results. Synonym merges
1681     eleven placement verbs into on, three in forms, the two wear forms, and six possessive forms into has, leaving every other
1682     predicate itself (32 classes). Coarse collapses the 50 predicates into three families: geometric, possessive and semantic. A
1683     model’s probability for a merged class is the sum over its members; for recall, a relation counts as a hit for a merged class
1684     when any member would (the smallest of the members’ first-hit ranks). Temperatures are fitted on the unmerged distributions
1685     on the audit’s calibration half. We also restrict to the 166,940 of 183,639 relations whose subject–object box pair carries a
1686     single annotated predicate, so that no second annotated predicate competes with the label.
1687        Table 27 reports the result. No conclusion of Sec. 5 depends on the predicate vocabulary. TDE’s matched case-level part
1688     stays null under both merges (+0.00059, interval [−0.00050, +0.00168]; −0.00015, [−0.00095, +0.00065]) and on single-
1689     annotation pairs, and the interval of its AUC change covers zero under both. IETrans’s stays negative and excludes zero
1690     under every merge (−0.01540 synonym, −0.00761 coarse); under the coarse merge its mean-recall case-level part also turns
1691     significantly negative (−0.0063). The logit-adjusted control keeps a positive matched case-level part throughout. Summing
1692     probabilities over a merged class can reorder the merged classes within a cell, so under a merge the control’s AUC is no
1693     longer fixed by construction; it moves by at most 0.0012.
1694        Could the benchmark leave little room for any case-level gain? One kind of ceiling is measurable: two relations with the
1695     same image and the same subject and object boxes but different predicates receive the same prediction from every model
1696     that sees the image and the boxes, so every comparison between them is a tie. They carry 0.07% of the AUC’s comparison
1697     weight (0.64% weighted as the split weights cells), which caps the within-cell AUC at 0.9996 (sgg auc ceiling.py).
1698     MOTIFS’s 0.5705 is far below that; how much of the gap is annotator style rather than missing recognition, the data cannot
1699     say.

         Q.3. A label-shifted test                                                                                                                   1700
                                                                        P
         Section 3 writes a cell’s case-level part as ∆Rc =           y<z p(y)p(z) D(y, z), where D(y, z) depends only on the class-                 1701
         conditional behaviour of the gain. A label shift that leaves P (X | Y, C) fixed changes the weights p(y)p(z) and no D, while                1702
         the group-level part depends on the cell’s label frequencies directly. So a case-level part keeps its sign under every such shift           1703
         if its D(y, z) share one sign, and can change sign otherwise; that much follows from the identity, and the reweighted estimator             1704
         below inherits it. What the data decide is which comparisons have D(y, z) of mixed sign, and how far the group-level parts                  1705
         move. This is a sensitivity analysis, not an independent test.                                                                              1706
             Relation i in cell c gets weight wi = 1/(Lc p̂c (yi )), with Lc the number of predicates present in the cell and p̂c their              1707
         frequencies, so that every predicate present in a cell carries equal total weight; the path wiα , α ∈ {0, 41 , 12 , 34 , 1}, moves from     1708
         the benchmark’s mix to that uniform one. The split is recomputed with pairs (i, j) of a cell weighted by wi wj , so a gain                  1709
         constant within a cell still has a case-level part of exactly zero and unit weights give the estimator of Sec. 3; the within-               1710
         cell AUC is recomputed with the same comparison weights. Temperatures are those fitted at the benchmark’s mix, on the                       1711
         calibration halves of Secs. 5 and 6, each study on its own temperature grid; they are not refitted under the shift. Intervals are a         1712
         delete-a-group jackknife over images (100 groups). An image bootstrap biases the case-level part towards zero here, because                 1713
         a resampled image’s same-label pairs have kernel zero and receive weight E[w2 ] = 2. At the uniform end the weights are                     1714
         very unequal: the Kish effective sample size of the audit half falls from 92,027 to 7,296.                                                  1715
             Table 28 gives the result for the eight comparisons of the two audits. The group-level part changes sign in seven of them; in           1716
         the eighth, the class-pair model against FREQ, it shrinks from −0.01432 to −0.00241. With the group-level parts, the totals                 1717
         reverse too: the CLIP model, last on the benchmark by every aggregate, beats geometry by +0.02981 under uniform labels.                     1718
         Of the six case-level parts whose interval excludes zero on the benchmark, five keep their sign and grow in size: geometry                  1719
         over the class-pair model (+0.00891 → +0.01157), CLIP over geometry (+0.00464 → +0.00615), IETrans+Rwt over the                             1720
         baseline (−0.01623 → −0.01830). The exception is TDE against the logit-adjusted control, whose case-level part turns from                   1721
         −0.00398 to +0.00723; and TDE’s null against the baseline becomes +0.01430, interval [+0.01116, +0.01744]. Along the                        1722
         path (Tab. 29) TDE’s part rises monotonically and already excludes zero at α = 41 (+0.00238, [+0.00092, +0.00385]): its                     1723
         null holds at the benchmark’s exact mix and nowhere near it. Both exceptions mean that TDE’s D(y, z) have mixed signs:                      1724

1775     Q.6. An input-grounded audit outside scene graphs: Waterbirds
1776     Waterbirds [34] pastes landbirds and waterbirds on land and water backgrounds so that in training the background agrees
1777     with the label 95% of the time. The cell here is the background’s scene category (bamboo forest, forest, lake, ocean), a
1778     property of the input, and the test split holds 5,794 images. Models are logistic regressions on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 features
1779     (colab waterbirds features.py, run waterbirds audit.py): ERM, and GRW, which reweights training im-
1780     ages so that each (label, background) group counts equally, the standard debiasing intervention and the analogue of TDE here.
1781     Temperature and a per-class bias are fitted on the group-balanced validation split and both models are evaluated on the test
1782     split.
1783         GRW raises accuracy from 0.8952 to 0.9391 and worst-group accuracy from 0.6433 to 0.8536 (Tab. 35). Its Brier gain
1784     is +0.06373, and unlike TDE in PredCls it is not a change of per-background priors: the group-level part is −0.02766
1785     and the case-level part +0.09139, interval [+0.08494, +0.09785]; with both models recalibrated the same way the parts
1786     are −0.05563 and +0.11375, [+0.10726, +0.12025]. The within-cell AUC rises only from 0.9723 to 0.9754 (+0.0031,
1787     [+0.0009, +0.0057]), so what GRW gains over ERM is mostly better-calibrated confidence in a bird’s own label within a
1788     background, rather than a different ranking of the birds. Recalibration alone moves both parts (−0.04560 case-level), which
1789     is why the matched comparison recalibrates both models. Four cells are coarse, and the result holds with the two backgrounds
1790     (land, water) as cells. It is one model pair on one dataset: it shows that the split can return a case-level answer where TDE’s
1791     is group-level, not that group reweighting is generally case-level.

         Q.7. Zero-shot CLIP on the canonical split                                                                                              1792

         The CLIP model of Sec. 6 trains a head on Visual Genome labels. As a first look at a vision–language model used with                    1793
         no training at all, we score the canonical test relations of Sec. 5 zero-shot: frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 [31] encodes the union              1794
         box of subject and object and is compared with one prompt per predicate, “a photo of a {subject} {predicate} a {object}”                1795
         (colab clip zeroshot.py). The map from dataset index to image file follows the codebase’s loader, and every image’s                     1796
         size matches the one stored with the audit. Nothing is trained, so the model has no predicate prior of its own. FREQ is the             1797
         MOTIFS frequency branch, constant within a cell up to 31 relations whose scored pair is a duplicate box with another label              1798
         (hence its AUC of 0.5002). Against FREQ a model’s case-level part is its own within-cell information. “Zero-shot CLIP                   1799
         + FREQ” multiplies the two distributions and renormalises, which leaves the within-cell AUC unchanged. Protocol as in                   1800
         Sec. 5: per-model temperature on the calibration half, split on the other.                                                              1801
             Zero-shot CLIP ranks the correct predicate first for 2.5% of relations, and its group-level loss against FREQ is −0.51900.          1802
         It does carry within-cell information: its AUC is 0.5164, above the 0.5 of any model constant within cells, and its case-level          1803
         part over FREQ is +0.00046, interval [+0.00041, +0.00051]. But this is a small fraction of what MOTIFS learns from                      1804
         Visual Genome: MOTIFS’s AUC is 0.5705, the difference −0.0542, interval [−0.0655, −0.0423], and its case-level part                     1805
         over FREQ is +0.02943. Given the training prior, zero-shot CLIP reaches 0.6289 top-1, but its case-level part over FREQ                 1806
         (+0.00989) is a third of MOTIFS’s. Frozen ViT-B/32 with one prompt per predicate is a weak baseline, at 2.5% top-1 close                1807
         to chance. A larger model with prompt ensembles (below) changes the picture little, but region-level features or fine-tuning            1808
         may carry more, and we draw no general conclusion about vision–language models from it. What it shows is that the split                 1809
         separates a model with no predicate prior and little within-pair information from one with both, in line with evidence that             1810
         such models often treat captions as bags of words [48]. TDE’s case-level part over FREQ, +0.02937, equals MOTIFS’s to                   1811
         the third decimal, as the null of Sec. 5 implies.                                                                                       1812
