### Genuine strengths (brief)
The finite-sample identity (Theorem 1 / Theorem 3) is exact and cheap to compute. The MLP-CLASS vs FREQ check (∆R̂ exactly 0) and the calibration-only simulation (−0.488 raw → −0.0003 matched) are well-chosen tests with known answers. Reporting all five TDE configurations, including two that contradict the authors' preferred reading (§5 l.382-386), is unusually candid. Every printed number traces to a committed JSON file.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The headline says TDE's ten-point mean-recall gain carries no case-level improvement. A sceptic would reply: the audit targets the one method whose answer is fixed in advance, measures a different quantity from the title's, and finds its zero in one configuration of five.

**Fixed in advance.** The authors themselves call TDE "a group-level intervention by design". By their own Theorem 2, two models differing only by a cell-dependent logit offset have a log-score case-level part of exactly zero. The audit therefore largely re-derives TDE's definition. That is a known-answer test, not a discovery, on one checkpoint, one backbone, one task mode.

**A different quantity.** Mean recall averages top-1 recall over predicates. The audited ∆R is a proper-score covariance weighted by relation, so head predicates such as *on*, *has* and *wearing* dominate it. Suppose TDE gains case-level discrimination on tail predicates and loses it on head predicates, which is the pattern the paper's own CIFAR and text tier tables show. The pooled result would then be zero while the ten points were real tail-predicate gains. The paper never stratifies TDE's ∆R by predicate, even though Eq. (3) is literally a sum over predicates.

**One configuration of five.** Across the five rows, the case-level part ranges from −0.217 to +0.044 and changes sign. Under the log score with calibration matched, TDE has a significant case-level gain. Matching applies T=2.50 to one model and T=0.92 to the other, and by Theorem 2 that injects a term proportional to the baseline's own case-informative logits. Either the +0.044 is real, contradicting the abstract, or it is a temperature artefact and the quadratic zero is equally suspect.

**The converse fails too.** The CLIP "reversal" disappears on every prior-matched rank metric the authors themselves computed.

### Resolution Paths (six-week horizon)
For each CRITICAL or MAJOR issue: what would defeat the attack, and whether it fits before 16 November 2026. Everything below except M1(b) runs on cached predictions (hours of compute). M1(b) needs some GPU training.

- **C1 (defeat condition: show the tail is not hiding a case-level gain).** (i) Report the per-predicate terms of Eq. (3), Σ_c w_c·2Cov(Δq_y, 1{Y=y} | c), for all 50 predicates. Give their macro-average (the mean-recall analogue) and a head/body/tail breakdown with image-clustered CIs, as in supp Tab. 12 and Tab. 14. (ii) Use Theorem 1's any-H identity to split the mean-recall gain itself: take H_x(y) = [1{argmax q1(x)=y} − 1{argmax q0(x)=y}]/n_y and report ∆T̂, ∆P̂ and ∆R̂ of *mean recall*. Check that per-relation top-1 mR tracks the reported mR@50. If the tail ∆R̂ is ≈ 0 and the mR split is almost entirely ∆P̂, the title is answered directly, C1 is withdrawn, and this becomes the paper's strongest figure. If the tail ∆R̂ is significantly positive, the headline has to change.
- **M1 (defeat condition: n > 1 with a pattern).** (a) Audit at least 3-4 more debiasing approaches on the same canonical PredCls split. Options: further released causal checkpoints from the same codebase if available (other backbones or fusion types); training-time re-weighting or re-sampling of MOTIFS (trainable in about a day on one GPU); and one or two later methods with public weights. (b) If compute allows, train a second seed of the TDE model. Either a uniform "all ∆R̂ ≈ 0" or a split that separates methods would strengthen the paper. Restrict the claims to PredCls throughout.
- **M2 (defeat condition: show the audit is not a tautology).** (a) State TDE's logit-level form under sum fusion, derive what the split *must* report, and quantify how much of z_TDE − z_SUM is constant within a cell (a within-cell variance share of the logit difference). (b) Add a post-hoc frequency-subtraction baseline, z_SUM − λ·log p̂(pred | pair), swept over λ, and plot mR gain against ∆P̂ and ∆R̂. If TDE sits on that curve, report it as "TDE ≈ prior subtraction", a sharper and citable finding. If it sits off the curve, the audit says something TDE's definition does not. (c) Show at least one real SGG method with significant positive ∆R̂, so the tool is seen to discriminate.
- **M3 (defeat condition: show the null does not depend on the scale).** Compute the within-cell rank contrast the paper itself recommends (supp App. K; a DeLong-style within-cell AUC difference, per predicate and pooled). Show ∆R̂ over a grid of (T0, T1), and with a common temperature applied to both models, which is natural because they share logits. Repeat the matched audit over ≥20 random image splits, as for CIFAR, so the CI includes temperature and split variability. Add a vector-scaling (per-class bias) recalibration. If the rank contrast is ≈ 0 and the null holds across the grid, M3 is withdrawn. If the rank contrast is negative, the honest headline becomes "TDE loses case-level discrimination", which is a stronger and more surprising result.
- **M4 (defeat condition: apply one protocol to all comparisons).** Make matched results primary for every comparison: CLIP (+0.00467), and newly computed matched values for the geometry pairs. Correct "every comparison here is confidence-matched", or make it true.
- **M5 (defeat condition: one standard, stated conditionally).** Drop the "twelfth" ratio or report it for every row, including raw-log 3.85. Make the abstract's conclusion explicitly conditional on the quadratic score. Explain the matched-log +0.044 using the temperature term above (M3), rather than presenting it as small.
- **M6 (defeat condition: the reversal survives a scale-free metric).** Report the within-cell AUC difference for CLIP vs geometry. Add a CLIP+geometry model, so that "pixels add to the proxy" is tested directly. Use 3-5 seeds for each head. Replace "better recogniser" with "larger within-pair covariance" unless the rank contrast agrees.
- **M7 (defeat condition: predictive validity on Visual Genome).** Build a label-shifted VG test, for example by within-cell importance reweighting or resampling to uniform per-cell predicate frequencies, or to the frequencies in the zero-shot triplet split. Show that the split predicted in advance which gains survive: TDE and MLP-CLASS over FREQ should fall, geometry and CLIP should hold. This takes a few days and turns the §3 toy argument into evidence.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **Head/tail cancellation (C1).** The pooled zero averages case-level gains on tail predicates with case-level losses on head predicates. This explanation fits all reported data equally well and contradicts the headline. The paper's own CIFAR and 20NG tier tables show the same mechanism.
2. **Mechanical equivalence to prior subtraction (M2).** TDE under sum fusion may be close to a logit offset that depends on the cell. If so, the split's verdict is a theorem about the architecture, not a measurement of what TDE "bought".
3. **The temperatures cause the null (M3).** Separate temperatures for two models that share weights add a term proportional to the baseline's evidence. A matched zero can be a raw case-level loss plus a gain induced by matching.
4. **A ceiling set by label ambiguity.** Visual Genome predicates are heavily synonymous (on / sitting on / standing on), and each relation carries one annotation. A case-level part near zero may reflect how far the labels can be predicted per case at all, rather than whether TDE recognises. TDE's tail predictions may often be acceptable to a human but are scored as wrong. Evaluating on a re-annotated subset or with multiple labels would separate the two.
5. **A zero-shot stratum the paper does not exploit.** TDE raised zR@50 from 0.110 to 0.143 (§5 l.338). Auditing only the zero-shot triplets is a natural case-level test, and it is unexplored.
6. **Capacity and dispersion behind the CLIP result.** The CLIP head receives 1024 appearance dimensions against 64 class dimensions, and no geometry. Its larger within-cell dispersion inflates a covariance that depends on scale. The authors' own hypothesis (supp l.1133-1135) concerns how well it fits the prior, not how well it recognises.
7. **A scale-invariant primary statistic.** Within-cell paired AUC (DeLong, already cited as [7]) would remove the calibration confound that the paper spends a protocol on. The exact additivity could be kept for the proper-score row as a companion.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Benchmark maintainers and codebase owners, who would need to archive full probability vectors and adjudicate the declared φ (§7 l.447-448 assigns this to them without consulting their constraints).
- Authors of debiasing methods who release rankings or checkpoints without probability dumps. The method cannot audit them, which biases any future audit toward methods with available dumps.
- Downstream users of scene graphs (VQA, captioning, embodied agents), for whom a group-level shift toward informative tail predicates may be exactly the desired product rather than a lesser kind of gain.
- Dataset annotators and curators, whose labelling conventions (synonymy, single label per relation) set the ceiling on achievable case-level gain.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes the community reads a mean-recall gain as evidence of better recognition of individual relations ("it is not what a ten-point gain is usually read as meaning", l.085-086). It cites no work that makes that reading. The SGG debiasing literature, including TDE itself, largely presents mean-recall gains as redistribution toward informative tail predicates. If the premise is weak, the "surprise" of the TDE audit disappears and the paper's value rests entirely on the tool and on the CLIP result. That makes M1, M2 and M6 decisive.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- Theorem 1 holds for any integrable H, not only proper scores. This gives the authors a direct, exact route to answering their own title (C1 remedy (ii)), and it is currently unused.
- The ng-mR result in the committed JSON (m2) actually *supports* the group-level reading. Putting it in the paper costs one sentence and removes an obvious reviewer objection.
- The candour of §5 l.382-393, which reports the configurations where "overwhelmingly group-level" is false, is a real strength, but it also gives critics their ammunition. Pairing it with the scale-invariant check (M3) would turn a disclosure into a robustness result.
- The calibration-only simulation (§4 l.298-304) is the paper's most persuasive validation. Extending it to two models that share weights and differ by a logit offset (the TDE case) would test M3's mechanism directly.
- If the per-predicate and rank-based analyses come out favourably, the paper's empirical claims become considerably harder to attack than they are now. Most of the attack surface above comes from analyses not yet run rather than from analyses that came out badly.
