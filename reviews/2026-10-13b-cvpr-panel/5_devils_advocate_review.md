### Strengths (brief, genuine only)
Numbers I tested reproduce from the committed result files. The authors disclose most of their own weak points (protocol-dependent signs, K-specificity, grouping dependence, shortcut credit, single-run intervals). They include an oracle and null/shortcut simulations that show the split behaves as designed. The attacks below are therefore about what can be inferred, not about concealment.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The paper sells an exact decomposition of a metric gain into "group-level" and "case-level" parts, then reads the second part as "telling which individual relations carry a predicate" and the first as a gain that "buys" nothing about recognition. The authors' own evidence shows the second part is not that. In the CLIP example the model has the larger case-level covariance yet ranks worse within pairs (primary within-cell AUC -0.0067) and has lower accuracy. For TDE the proper-score case-level part changes sign with the matching protocol (+0.0045, -0.00006, -0.0184) and is reproduced by removing a frequency prior that changes no ranking (+0.0082). For thresholded mean recall the case-level part is contaminated by operating-point movement, so a ranking-free logit shift scores +0.0139. In every real application, then, the exact half of the split cannot be interpreted alone; the work that carries the conclusion is done by two add-ons outside the identity: a logit-adjustment control and a within-cell AUC.

Those add-ons are weak where it matters. The control matches TDE only at K = 50 under the graph constraint; at K = 100 TDE leads by +0.0300 and without the constraint it trails by 0.08 to 0.15. The AUC null is an interval bound (0.0034 or 0.0077 depending on weighting) benchmarked against a geometry gain from a different, reconstructed system, and the baseline's within-cell AUC is itself only 0.57 to 0.61, so there was little discrimination to lose. The three audited methods are all prior shifts in one backbone, which makes "mostly group-level" the expected outcome rather than a test of the tool. The one condition under which the instrument could show it distinguishes the two routes (a method that really improves ranking) is shown only in controlled MLPs, not in SGG. And even granting the finding, the authors concede "Group-level is not a lesser gain", and that prior correction rivals debiasing is "known". What remains is a diagnostic tool whose positive result in the target domain is missing, wrapped in a title that implies a deflationary finding the paper does not establish.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. A coarser reading of the same data: TDE's gain is "logit adjustment", fully accounted for by well-known prior correction; then the split adds no information beyond the control run alone, and the exact decomposition is not what supports the conclusion.
2. The low case-level share reflects the benchmark's annotation (about one third of relations sit on a box pair with more than one relation, per results/sgg_auc_ceiling.json), so any method, including a truly discriminative one, would show a small case-level share; the paper checks only the tie-based ceiling (0.9996), not annotator-choice noise among near-synonymous predicates.
3. Within-cell ranking could be measured at instance level (box pair) instead of class pair; the grouping conditions on a coarser object than the one the model sees.
4. TDE's tail failure (tail gain +0.0003) may reflect the evaluation's graph constraint rather than group-level mechanics.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Authors of the audited methods (TDE, DLFE, IETrans): their stated goals are rebalanced predicates, so a "group-level" finding aligns with the stated aim. The paper does not separate "method fails its goal" from "metric credits the goal".

### Unexamined Premise
That a within-class-pair covariance with the annotated label is the right operational definition of "which individual relations carry a predicate". The annotated label for a box pair is itself one choice among several valid predicates; if the annotation is arbitrary among near-synonyms, then case-level covariance measures agreement with annotator idiosyncrasy, and a null is expected for any model.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The oracle and prior-only simulations do separate the two parts as designed; the tool is credible in controlled settings.
- Disclosure is generous; most of my attacks are against the headline framing relative to the paper's own tables.
- Rerunning the released TDE checkpoint to recover full logits is a real reproducibility contribution.
