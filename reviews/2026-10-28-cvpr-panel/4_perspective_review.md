# Peer Reviewer 3 (Perspective, R3) - Round 4

Submission: "What Did Ten Points of Mean Recall Buy? Separating Group-Level from Case-Level Gains in Scene Graph Generation" (CVPR 2027, anonymous). Target commit ea0efe7.
Status flags: NOT_CALIBRATED. criteria_binding_unavailable (no venue rubric bound; scores below are advisory only).
Scope of reading: paper_text.txt in full; supp_text.txt selectively (N.5, N.6, Q.9-Q.10, Tab. 11, 38, 39). Reviewer comes from robustness / spurious-correlation / forecast-verification, outside SGG. I did not read the proofs and did not rerun code.

## 1. Summary of the paper (as understood)
Two frozen models are scored under observed labels and under labels permuted within a declared cell (here the subject-object class pair). The score gain splits exactly into a group-level part (survives the permutation) and a case-level covariance part (Yates-style). The split is extended to mean recall, with image-clustered inference, a within-cell AUC and an operating-point control (logit adjustment). Main claims: 87% of TDE's +10 mR@50 is group-level, a no-ranking-change logit adjustment recovers +0.094 of +0.097, and DLFE, IETrans and PE-Net are likewise mostly group-level. Waterbirds, CIFAR-100-LT and 20 Newsgroups are offered as evidence of generality.

## 2. Strengths
- S1. The question is well posed for a metric-driven subfield: "which route did the number go up by?" The exactness of the identity, the controlled predictors in Sec. 4 (the class-pair-only models give exactly zero) and the simulations with known ground truth are the right way to validate a diagnostic.
- S2. Unusual honesty on limits: "What is not claimed", the post-hoc declaration dates, 81.5% coverage at two cases per cell, and a Waterbirds sentence that says "one model pair on one dataset".
- S3. The logit-adjustment control is a clean, cheap null-model idea that transfers: shift the old model by something that provably changes no within-group ranking, and see how much of the gain it reproduces.
- S4. The CLIP-vs-geometry example (Sec. 6), where aggregate ranking and case-level ranking disagree, is the most convincing demonstration of added value over accuracy.

## 3. Findings
Severity: M = major (affects a central claim), m = minor, c = clarity. Each has anchor / problem / fix.

### F1 (M) The "new" decomposition is close to a family of known ones; positioning is too thin for the novelty implied
- Anchor: Contributions (i); Sec. 2 "Proper-score decompositions" (l.137-157).
- Problem: Theorem 1 is the paired-difference form of Yates's covariance split, applied per cell, with transport equal to a within-cell permutation. The related-work text itself concedes that a single model's case-level part is conditional permutation importance / model reliance and that a gain's part is the difference of two such quantities. What remains new is (a) paired inference, (b) the thresholded-metric extension and (c) the control. These are reasonable contributions but are narrower than the title and abstract ("exact, model-free decomposition") suggest. Missing neighbours: Kitagawa/Oaxaca-Blinder decompositions of a between-group gap into "composition" and "within-group structure" terms, which is structurally the same accounting identity (group-level = composition/prior, case-level = within-group association) and which has a large literature on path dependence and the choice of reference (analogous to the paper's grouping dependence); Shapley-style decompositions of metric gains; and bias-corrected Brier decompositions (finite-sample bias of REL/RES is exactly the small-cell issue behind the 81.5% coverage result).
- Fix: Add a short paragraph and one table row comparing Murphy 1973, Yates 1982, grouping loss, permutation importance and KOB on three axes: paired (two-model) vs single, handles thresholded metrics, has finite-sample paired inference. State plainly that the identity is classical and the contribution is its use for paired gain and thresholded metrics. Candidate references: Blinder, "Wage discrimination: reduced form and structural estimates", J. Human Resources 8(4):436-455, 1973 (verified via web search); Ferro and Fricker, "A bias-corrected decomposition of the Brier score", QJRMS 138(668):1954-1960, 2012 (verified via web search; abstract only, full text not accessed). Both are optional; the point is the positioning.

### F2 (M) The generality claim rests on studies where the "cell" is not an input feature, or where the split contradicts the robustness reading
- Anchor: Sec. 6 last paragraph; Supp. N.5, N.6 (l.1474-1480, 1563-1575), Q.10 (Tab. 39).
- Problem (a), CIFAR-100-LT and 20 Newsgroups: the cell is a coarse superclass, a function of the label that no model observes. The supplement says so ("not an audit of a feature both models condition on"). The identity still holds, but the interpretive core of the framing (what the model could know from the cell) is lost: group-level here means "movement toward the balanced test marginal inside the superclass", which for a long-tail method is the intended effect, not a finding. Moreover, the labels carry no ground truth for "real" case-level gain on these tasks, so the N.5/N.6 results (CB and DRW swap channels between image and text) show the tool returns different numbers on different tasks, not that those numbers are right. The Sec. 7 claim of breadth ("any benchmark supplying full probability vectors, labels and a declared grouping") is supported by an algebraic fact, not by validation. Also, Sec. 6 says in one sentence that the supplement "splits" these; the main paper offers a non-SGG reader no way to see the caveat.
- Problem (b), Waterbirds: the cell is the background, and the label is bird type. The standard robustness quantity (worst group = label x background) is not decomposed; instead the Brier split is reported. GRW shows group-level -0.028, case-level +0.091, and within-cell AUC +0.003. The supplement itself concludes this is "better-calibrated confidence", and that recalibration alone moves the case-level part by -0.046 (Tab. 39). So the headline "a debiasing gain that is case-level" (Sec. 6) is, by the authors' own AUC, mostly a calibration/confidence effect of reweighting, i.e. precisely the confound the paper warns about for TDE. Using an AUC-first reading, Waterbirds would be read as "almost no ranking gain", the same verdict as for TDE, which weakens the contrast the paragraph is built on. In addition, GRW removes the background-label prior by design, so a negative group-level part is the intended mechanism, not evidence of loss; and recalibration is fitted on the group-balanced validation split, which hands ERM a group-balanced prior correction that is itself a debiasing step (the matched comparison is therefore not neutral about what is being credited).
- Fix: (1) Retitle the sub-claims: the non-SGG studies show the estimator runs and behaves coherently, not that conclusions transfer. (2) For Waterbirds, report the split of worst-group accuracy / mean group recall (it is a label-weighted hit rate, which Theorem 1 covers) with cells = background and with cells = (background) and a threshold-free within-cell AUC, and lead with the AUC. (3) Add a Waterbirds sanity control analogous to LA: shift ERM's logits by a per-background prior and show it recovers the group-level part but not the worst-group gain. (4) Move one non-SGG result with an input-grounded cell into the main text; relegate label-derived-cell studies to a clearly labelled "behaviour of the estimator" paragraph.

### F3 (M) Does the group/case dichotomy have a stable meaning outside a benchmark with a natural, near-complete cell? (fundamental assumption)
- Anchor: Sec. 3 "Choosing the grouping"; Sec. 5 Tab. 1 lower panel (87% vs 18% vs 90% vs 84%); Sec. 7 Limitations.
- Problem: The headline share is a function of phi: the same checkpoint is 87% group-level with class pairs and 18% with subject class alone. The paper treats the class pair as "the finest grouping the benchmark's construction defends", but in SGG the pair is a human-chosen proxy for the context the prior is estimated on; adding any context variable (scene, image-level tags) would push mass from group to case. At the limit of a fine enough phi (each cell a single case) the case-level part vanishes and everything is group-level, and at the coarsest everything is case-level; the dichotomy is a dial, not a property of the method. For a general reader the practical question "which phi" is the whole problem, and Proposition 4's sensitivity bound needs an elicited correlation that practitioners will not have. Relatedly, the decision-relevant question for TDE users is whether the gain is available from a trivially cheaper correction, which the LA control answers without any cell declaration. That suggests the control, not the split, carries the main practical value.
- Fix: Present the share as a profile over a declared hierarchy of groupings (as in Tab. 1 lower panel) as the default output, not a single number; state in the abstract that the 87% is conditional on class-pair cells; and in the recommendation say which of the two (split vs control) a user should prefer when no defensible phi exists.

### F4 (M) Practical reporting recommendation is under-specified and heavy
- Anchor: Conclusion (l.605-608); Sec. 7.
- Problem: "Report the declared grouping, both parts with intervals, the within-group AUC and, for a thresholded metric, the operating-point control" is four artefacts beyond the headline metric. The paper itself shows each is fragile: the split moves with recalibration (so a calibration protocol must be chosen and dated), the AUC is "not additive" and its verdict depends on the weighting (primary AUC bound 0.0038 vs per-predicate 0.028; none of the bounds is tight for the weighting closest to mean recall), the control matches only at K=50 with the graph constraint (TDE leads at K=100, trails at K=20), and per-model cross-fitting halves the audit sample. No guidance says who sets phi, how a leaderboard would implement a post-hoc control for a method with no logits, or what a reader should conclude when the AUC bound is wide (as with IETrans and PE-Net, where the authors "claim no AUC gain" either way). The recommendation also needs full probability vectors for all labels, which many methods do not release.
- Fix: Give a minimal protocol (a numbered 3-step checklist, one default cell, one default weighting) and a short decision table ("AUC bound narrow and control matches -> prior correction; AUC gain beyond bound -> discrimination; otherwise undetermined"). Say explicitly that "undetermined" is a legitimate outcome and report how often it occurs among the audited methods (by my read, TDE per-predicate, IETrans and PE-Net AUC are all effectively undetermined).

### F5 (M) Reading "group-level" as "cheap prior fix" or "not real" risks a normative overreach; the metric-goodness argument is only half made
- Anchor: Sec. 5 "What that licenses"; "How to read the split" (l.465-468); Intro (l.42-52).
- Problem: The paper says group-level is "not a lesser gain" but the title, the abstract's prominence of the control and the repeated "under a tenth of TDE's cost" framing invite precisely that reading. The control reproduces the mR@50 total, yet it costs 0.2023 vs 0.0175 in R@50 (l.450), i.e. the control is not a free lunch on the other headline metric; TDE and LA are therefore not interchangeable users' choices. The audit is also silent on whether a user who wanted a tail-aware output actually cares about within-pair ranking at all (retrieval and captioning examples are mentioned in one sentence). In long-tailed recognition the accepted view since Menon et al. is that post-hoc logit adjustment is a principled solution to the balanced-error objective; showing that a debiasing method equals logit adjustment is a finding about the method's novelty, not about its worth.
- Fix: Reframe the verdict as "what the gain is" rather than "what it bought"; add one sentence in the abstract that group-level gains can be the intended target of the metric; and report R@50 beside the control in Tab. 1.

### F6 (m) Relation to worst-group / group-robustness evaluation is asserted but not drawn
- Anchor: Sec. 2 (l.130-136), Sec. 6 Waterbirds.
- Problem: In group robustness the reported quantity is worst-group (or group-balanced) accuracy, and known results (Idrissi et al., simple group/class balancing is competitive; Kirichenko et al., DFR, last-layer retraining suffices) say that the gain comes mostly from reweighting the classifier head, i.e. a change of prior and decision boundary rather than of features. That literature predicts exactly the paper's SGG finding, and a reader from that area will ask whether the split reproduces it (e.g. ERM vs group-balanced reweighting vs DFR on Waterbirds, where the AUC change is tiny). Not doing so leaves the group-robustness audience with no reason to adopt the tool. Verified references: Idrissi, Arjovsky, Pezeshki, Lopez-Paz, "Simple data balancing achieves competitive worst-group-accuracy", CLeaR 2022 (PMLR 177:336-351); Kirichenko, Izmailov, Wilson, "Last Layer Re-Training is Sufficient for Robustness to Spurious Correlations", ICLR 2023 (web search verified).
- Fix: Add to Waterbirds a three-way comparison ERM / GRW / DFR-style last-layer retraining, and state whether the split agrees with the literature that these gains are head-level.

### F7 (m) Clarity for a non-SGG reader (abstract density and jargon)
- Anchor: Abstract (l.001-027); Intro; Fig. 1 caption.
- Problem: The abstract holds about 20 numerical results and undefined terms in 27 lines (PredCls, TDE, DLFE, IETrans, PE-Net, "Bregman score", "image-clustered intervals", "cell", "four weightings", "0.0038 to 0.028"). A reader outside SGG cannot recover the claim without the body. "Group-level" and "case-level" are used for the same objects as "transport" and "covariance" (and "prior" vs "ranking"); "cell" is defined only in Sec. 3 yet used in the abstract. The central one-line message, that a metric credits shifting probability within a pair and distinguishing relations alike, is good but is buried. The paper is also very dense for ten pages, with several protocol-dependent numbers per sentence in Sec. 5; Tab. 27 and "every other protocol" are referred to but not summarised.
- Fix: Rewrite the abstract to four or five sentences: the problem, the idea (permute labels within a group), the key result (87%, control), the limit (conditional on the grouping, four checkpoints). Move the AUC bounds and PE-Net numbers to the body. Define cell, group-level and case-level in the abstract in plain words; add a one-column glossary in the supplement; and give Fig. 1 a 2x2 toy table with numbers.

### F8 (m) Evidence base for the headline is four released checkpoints, one run each
- Anchor: Abstract, "four released PredCls checkpoints, one run each"; Sec. 7.
- Problem: Intervals cover test sampling only; training variance is represented by one separately trained baseline pair (+0.0206 mR, +0.001 case-level) and one AUC spread (0.006), and the AUC spread between the two baselines (-0.0060) has opposite sign to the DLFE baseline comparison. PE-Net's case-level claim ("far exceeds" a +0.001 spread) rests on a single pair. A reader from the dataset-bias side would want seed variance on at least TDE vs LA, which are cheap to retrain, so that "TDE minus LA = -0.0008" is not simply one draw.
- Fix: Retrain MOTIFS baseline and TDE for 3-5 seeds (the authors already do five seeds for the CLIP model in Appendix P) and report the spread of group share and of the TDE-LA difference.

### F9 (m) Ethical and practical implications are unaddressed
- Anchor: LLM Usage Statement; Sec. 7.
- Problem: (a) There is no discussion of how the tool could be misused: a leaderboard that rewards case-level gain would reward exploitation of within-pair shortcuts ("credits any signal varying inside a pair, shortcuts included"), which for scene graphs includes social and gender-linked predicates that the benchmark can encode; the paper tells readers shortcuts are credited but not what to do about it. (b) The comparison against released checkpoints of named methods (TDE, IETrans, DLFE, PE-Net) with verdicts such as "a learned logit adjustment" should be framed as a property of the declared protocol, not of the authors' methods; the PE-Net and IETrans sections already hedge. (c) The LLM statement (writing, code, "simulated reviews used as feedback") is detailed and appropriate, but the claim that a script checks every manuscript number against stored results should be backed by the released artefact.
- Fix: Add a short "broader implications" sentence on shortcut crediting and on the reading of a negative AUC (synonymy, label noise); ensure the number-checking script is in the release.

### F10 (m) Interpretation of a negative AUC change and of synonymy
- Anchor: Sec. 5 IETrans paragraph (l.504-512); SGCls paragraph (l.529-534).
- Problem: AUC against recorded labels is treated as evidence about discrimination yet the paper concedes it measures "agreement with the recorded labels" and the labels are noisy and synonymous in VG. For TDE and IETrans the AUC falls significantly in SGCls and for IETrans, and these falls are not decisive either way. This is the standard "evaluation against noisy shortcut labels" problem in robustness work (same as accuracy on spuriously labelled data), and a reviewer in that area would ask for the standard remedy: a small, cleaned or multi-label test subset.
- Fix: Evaluate the AUC on a re-annotated or merged-synonym subset (e.g. predicate merging as in the IETrans taxonomy) and show the sign of the AUC change is stable.

## 4. Questions for the authors
1. With phi = class pair, how many of the 5,044 cells with at least two cases are effectively one-label cells (zero covariance by construction)? What fraction of mean-recall mass sits there?
2. If one adds scene-level or image-level tags to phi, how does the TDE share move? Is there a hierarchy at which it crosses 50%?
3. Does the split reproduce the known "reweighting changes the head" result for DFR on Waterbirds?
4. Why is the primary AUC weighting the one that favours head predicates when the headline metric is mean (per-predicate) recall?
5. For which methods (if any) among those audited would you say the verdict is "undetermined"?

## 5. Assessment (NOT_CALIBRATED; criteria_binding_unavailable)
- Significance: moderate; the diagnostic idea (null control plus paired split) is useful, and the finding about TDE's mechanism is informative for SGG.
- Originality: moderate; the identity is classical (Yates, KOB-style accounting), contribution lies in paired inference and the thresholded-metric extension (F1).
- Soundness: mostly careful, but the generality claims are overextended (F2, F3) and the AUC verdicts are frequently inconclusive (F4, F10).
- Clarity: the abstract and Sec. 5 are too dense for non-specialists (F7).
- Suggested recommendation (advisory only): borderline / weak accept conditional on tempering generality claims, reframing the headline as conditional on phi, and adding the Waterbirds control and seed-variance checks. Confidence in this call: moderate (outside subfield; proofs not read).
