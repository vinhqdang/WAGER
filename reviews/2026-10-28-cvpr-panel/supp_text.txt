       D. Coarsening and unrecorded covariates                                                                                                   1002

       Appendix N.4 reports that replacing the class-pair cell with a coarser subject-only cell changes the estimated covariance                 1003
       gain. The following result gives that sensitivity an exact population-level mechanism rather than treating it as an empirical             1004
       curiosity.                                                                                                                                1005
          The first term is the coarse cell’s share of the fine-grained covariance gain; the second is a between-cell covariance, over           1006
       the finer cells nested inside c̄, between each fine cell’s typical gain at label y and its label-y frequency. It is not sign definite,    1007
       so coarsening ϕ can inflate or deflate the credited covariance gain, and it vanishes exactly when either quantity is constant             1008
       within each coarse cell. This is why the finest defensible ϕ is the conservative default: coarsening adds cross-cell prior                1009
       structure into ∆R, while the finer partition’s gain is preserved on average through the first term. The proof is a per-label              1010
       application of the law of total covariance (Appendix E).                                                                                  1011

1318   A recalibration-invariant alternative, and what it would cost. Because ∆R is a covariance between forecast values, its
1319   sensitivity to recalibration is structural rather than incidental, and a reader may reasonably ask why we do not use a rank-
1320   based within-cell contrast instead — a within-cell paired AUC difference, say, for which DeLong et al. [10] already supply
1321   the correlated-U-statistic variance. Such a quantity would be invariant to any monotone rescaling of either model and would
1322   dissolve the confound this paper spends a protocol on. We think the trade is worth stating and is not obviously in our favour
1323   on every axis. What a rank-based contrast gives up is the exact identity: it is not a component of the score difference, so
1324   it cannot be added to anything to recover ∆T , and a benchmark reporting it learns how the models rank cases but not how
1325   the reported score gain decomposes. The construction here buys exact additivity with respect to a declared proper score, and
1326   pays for it with scale dependence. A rank-based diagnostic is the natural companion rather than a competitor, and reporting
1327   both — one exact and scale-dependent, one invariant and non-additive — is what we would now recommend to a benchmark
1328   that could choose.

1346   L. Terminology
1347   M. Why the transported gain is not prior fit
1348   Proposition 1 splits ∆Pc into two brackets, and only the first is prior fit: it is positive exactly when the new model’s average
1349   within-cell forecast sits closer to the cell’s label frequencies. The second has nothing to do with the prior. It rewards the
1350   new model for making less dispersed predictions inside the cell, at a fixed mean forecast, so ∆P can be large and positive
1351   when neither model’s prior fit has changed at all. One cell suffices: with two labels and frequencies ( 21 , 12 ), let q0 alternate
1352   between (1, 0) and (0, 1) in a pattern uncorrelated with the label and let q1 predict ( 12 , 12 ) throughout. Both mean forecasts
1353   equal the cell frequencies exactly, so the first bracket is zero, and the whole observed gain of 12 is transported, produced
1354   entirely by the collapse in dispersion. The channel is therefore the reliability-and-sharpness channel of the classical partition,
1355   not a prior-fitting channel, which is why we name it for the operation. Two consequences: a claim that some gain is “mostly
1356   prior-fitting” does not follow from a large ∆Pd alone, and Eq. (8) explains why confidence matching moves ∆P       d so sharply,
1357   since a temperature change is almost purely a change in dispersion.

1416   N.3. Real pixels against a geometric proxy, in full
1417   None of the predictors above ever sees an image: they are functions of the class pair and, for MLP-SPATIAL(+), of box
1418   coordinates recorded in the annotation. Since box geometry is only a proxy for image content, a sceptical reader may ask
1419   whether the covariance channel responds to pixel evidence or merely to that proxy. Figure 5 sharpens the question — the cell
1420   ϕ = (man, surfboard) holds both “riding” and “carrying”, and what distinguishes them is what the photograph shows.
1421       MLP-VISUAL answers it. Subject and object regions are cropped from the original image and each encoded by a frozen
1422   CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder [34]; the two 512-dimensional embeddings are concatenated with the class embeddings MLP-CLASS
1423   already uses and passed to a small trained head. Because CLIP is never fine-tuned, whatever covariance the model gains
1424   reflects information its pretraining already recovered from pixels rather than representation learning on Visual Genome.
1425       Encoding every training relation with CLIP is expensive, so MLP-VISUAL must be trained on a subsample; setting a
1426   subsampled model against the full-data MLP-SPATIAL would confound feature content with training-set size. All three
1427   compared predictors are therefore retrained on one fixed, seeded subsample of 100,000 of the 532,987 training relations,
1428   written MLP-CLASS-S, MLP-SPATIAL-S and MLP-VISUAL-S, so any gap between them isolates the features. The audit
1429   still uses the full 229,605-relation test split with the same class-pair ϕ and image-clustered intervals.

1430   Result. By any aggregate measure the CLIP model is the worst of the three: top-1 accuracy 0.6156 against 0.6467 for
1431   MLP-SPATIAL-S and 0.6416 for MLP-CLASS-S, and a quadratic score 0.04642 below MLP-SPATIAL-S. An evaluator
1432   reading either number would discard it.
1433      The decomposition contradicts that reading on the axis that matters. Against MLP-SPATIAL-S the CLIP model loses
1434   0.05282 of transported gain while gaining 0.00640 of covariance (95% CI [0.00513, 0.00767], randomization p = .002): its
1435   entire deficit sits in the transported channel and its within-cell discrimination is significantly better, not worse. Against the
1436   class-only baseline the point is plainer still — pixels buy 0.01663 of covariance where box geometry buys 0.01023, with
1437   well-separated intervals. Real image content carries more case-specific signal than the geometric proxy standing in for it.
1438      A plausible mechanism, stated as hypothesis rather than finding: the 1024 CLIP dimensions dominate the 64 dimensions
1439   of class embedding the head also receives, so the model fits VG150’s class-pair frequency structure markedly less well than
1440   a predictor built around it. A benchmark whose headline metric rewards transported gain will punish this model, and the
1441   aggregate numbers duly do. What the decomposition adds is that the punishment is not for failing to use the image. Two
1442   falsification checks, reported in Section H, support that reading: correcting a model’s within-cell prior towards the training
1443   histogram registers as almost pure transported movement (+0.02390 against +0.00114 of covariance), as a correction carry-
1444   ing no case-level information must, and applied to the CLIP model it halves the aggregate deficit (−0.04642 to −0.02138)
1445   while the covariance advantage persists (+0.00754, and +0.00664 when both models are corrected) and survives confidence
1446   matching (+0.00464, CI [+0.00311, +0.00618]).

1466   N.5. Long-tailed image classification
1467   Sections 4–6 are all instances of one task (predicate classification on an ordered object pair). Section 2 argued that long-tailed
1468   image classification poses a structurally identical question: a class-frequency prior can be fit better without any per-instance
1469   improvement. We test this directly on CIFAR-100-LT [8], an exponential-imbalance (ratio 100) subsample of CIFAR-100’s
1470   training split with the original, balanced test split retained for evaluation. We train ResNet-32 [17] classifiers that differ only
1471   in their per-class loss weighting: a vanilla cross-entropy baseline (CE); class-balanced effective-number re-weighting applied
1472   from initialization (CB) [8]; and the same re-weighting deferred to the final twenty epochs (DRW) [3]. Optimizer, schedule,
1473   augmentation, epoch budget, and seed are identical across runs (Section H).

1474   Choosing ϕ. A declared grouping variable has to predict the label without determining it, and this second benchmark makes
1475   the constraint unusually easy to violate. Were ϕ taken to be the fine class label itself, every cell would be label-homogeneous,
1476   within-cell transport would have nothing to cross, and ∆R d would come out identically zero – an algebraic artifact wearing the
1477   appearance of a finding. CIFAR-100’s own 20 coarse superclasses avoid this, each gathering five fine labels into a cell of 500
1478   balanced test images, which is structurally the situation of a VG class-pair cell holding several predicates (Figure 5). Unlike a
1479   class pair, though, a superclass is a function of the label, not of the input, and no model observes it at test time: the superclass
1480   rows show how the estimator behaves under a label-derived grouping, not an audit of a feature both models condition on.
1481   The input-grounded row is the global cell, which every model trivially observes. Alongside the superclass audit we report
1482   two further declared priors: the trivial coarsening to a single global cell, which audits against the global class marginal and so
1483   instantiates Proposition 3 in a second domain, and a partition into head, medium and tail training-frequency tiers. CIFAR-100
1484   test images are independent, unlike the several relations VG draws from one photograph, so no cluster grouping is required
1485   and the plain Hájek interval of Section 3 applies directly. One semantic point matters in this domain: transport uses each
1486   cell’s test label histogram, which on CIFAR-100-LT is balanced. A positive ∆P here therefore records movement toward
1487   the balanced test marginal – undoing the training-frequency skew – which is precisely the adjustment long-tailed evaluation
1488   is designed to reward; a transported gain is a description of where score was earned, not an accusation (Section 7).

1489   Result. The three classifiers reach top-1 accuracies of 0.3662 (CE), 0.2627 (CB) and 0.3874 (DRW), so re-weighting from
1490   initialization degrades the classifier substantially while the deferred schedule delivers the improvement it was designed to
1491   produce. Table 13 decomposes both gains against the superclass audit.
1492       Of the two, the class-balanced run is the more instructive, precisely because its aggregate score conceals almost everything
1493   of interest. On the quadratic score it gains +0.00143 over the baseline, a difference small enough that a reader working from
1494   the headline number alone would reasonably conclude that re-weighting had changed very little. The decomposition tells
1495   another story: within superclasses the transported channel improves by +0.19713 while within-group covariance deteriorates
1496   by −0.19569 (95% CI [−0.20728, −0.18411]), so the near-zero total is not a small effect but two large ones that happen to
1497   cancel.

1498   A calibration-matched regime. A channel split of that shape could, however, be produced by confidence scaling alone.
1499   Under the quadratic score the covariance channel is a covariance between probability movements and labels (Eq. (3)), and
1500   a monotone recalibration moves it: softening an overconfident model transfers score mass from the covariance channel to
1501   the transported channel while adding no information about any individual image. The baseline invites exactly this concern,
1502   assigning a mean maximum probability of 0.764 while being correct on only 0.3662 of the test set. We therefore repeat the
1503   audit in a calibration-matched regime: each model’s temperature is chosen by held-out likelihood on a calibration half of the
1504   test split (T ∗ = 2.85 for CE, 3.51 for CB, 2.61 for DRW), the decomposition is computed on the disjoint audit half, and the
1505   whole procedure is repeated over twenty random splits (Table 11). The regime’s necessity is quantified by its control row:
1506   recalibrating the baseline alone – a transformation carrying no instance information – moves the channels by +0.380 (prior)
1507   and −0.175 (covariance), so the raw split does mix calibration with discrimination.

       O.1. TDE along its branches                                                                                                         1654

       In PredCls the baseline’s logits are vis + ctx + frq and TDE’s are ctx − ctxavg , so the change runs through two intermediate       1655
       models built from the same branches: the baseline without the frequency prior (vis + ctx), and the context term alone. The          1656
       split is linear in the gain vector, the AUC is a difference of per-model values, and in the matched proper-score split each         1657
       model keeps one held-out temperature throughout; so along this path every part adds up to TDE’s exactly, which the script           1658
       asserts (Tab. 22). The order of the three steps is a choice, and the steps are attributions along it, not order-free effects.       1659

           Two observations make the mechanism plain. Dropping the frequency prior, which is constant within every cell, cannot            1660
       move the within-cell AUC and does not; it moves mean recall by −0.0009, but shifts the matched quadratic case-level part            1661
       by +0.00915, another instance of the operating-point effect. And the averaged-context term that TDE subtracts is almost a           1662
       constant: splitting each logit term’s energy, after removing each row’s mean, into the vector shared by every relation, what        1663
       the subject–object cell adds, and what varies within cells (Tab. 23), 99.96% of ctxavg is the shared vector, with a non-shared      1664
       remainder of 0.021 logits against a size of 1.10. The shared vector correlates +0.75 with the log training prior. Subtracting it    1665
       is therefore, to first order, a logit adjustment of the context branch by a learned prior; its small remainder is what moves the    1666
       within-cell AUC by +0.0016.                                                                                                         1667

       Q.3. A label-shifted test                                                                                                                   1774
                                                                      P
       Section 3 writes a cell’s case-level part as ∆Rc =           y<z p(y)p(z) D(y, z), where D(y, z) depends only on the class-                 1775
       conditional behaviour of the gain. A label shift that leaves P (X | Y, C) fixed changes the weights p(y)p(z) and no D, while                1776
       the group-level part depends on the cell’s label frequencies directly. So a case-level part keeps its sign under every such shift           1777
       if its D(y, z) share one sign, and can change sign otherwise; that much follows from the identity, and the reweighted estimator             1778
       below inherits it. What the data decide is which comparisons have D(y, z) of mixed sign, and how far the group-level parts                  1779
       move. This is a sensitivity analysis, not an independent test.                                                                              1780

1927   Q.9. Beyond PredCls: SGCls and SGDet
1928   The causal MOTIFS-SUM release includes SGCls and SGDet checkpoints alongside the PredCls one. Under SUM fusion
1929   TDE’s logit is ctx(post) − ctx(avg) in every protocol, so one TDE pass again yields the baseline (vis + ctx(post) + frq of
1930   the predicted labels) and the logit-adjusted control, and all three share every object prediction: the boxes, labels and scores
1931   come from the factual object branch. A pass stores logits for up to 6,320 pairs per image in SGDet, too many to keep,
1932   so the evaluator is replayed inside the inference loop (colab sgg inline.py): for each ground-truth relation and each
1933   predicate it keeps the first-hit rank with and without the graph constraint, ordering pairs by the post-processor’s triple score
1934   (predicate times subject and object scores) and matching a pair when both its labels equal the ground truth and both boxes
1935   overlap it at IoU ≥ 0.5, and it keeps the predicate distribution of the matched pair with the largest summed IoU, chosen
1936   from boxes and labels alone and so the same for all three models. On test images 0–199 the replayed TDE recalls equal the
1937   codebase’s evaluator in both protocols to its printed precision, for R, mR, ng-R and ng-mR at K = 20, 50, 100; on the full
1938   test set they reproduce the released numbers (Tab. 37).
1939       The grouping is the ground-truth subject–object class pair, as in PredCls. Grouping by the predicted pair instead changes
1940   nothing that can be computed: a ground-truth relation is matched only by a predicted pair whose two labels equal its own,
1941   so for every matched relation the predicted pair is the ground-truth pair, and an unmatched relation has no predicted pair to
1942   group by. A relation whose objects the model fails to recognise is a miss for all three models under every predicate label, so
1943   it adds to neither part; the AUC and the proper score use the relations with a matched pair (58% in SGCls, 65% in SGDet).
1944   That selection favours relations whose objects are easy to recognise, so the AUC and proper-score results describe TDE on
1945   that subset; the mean-recall split, which counts an unmatched relation as a miss for all three models, does not depend on it.
1946       Table 38 gives the result. In SGCls TDE raises mR@50 by +0.0517, of which +0.0510 is group-level (99%) and
1947   +0.0008, interval [−0.0024, +0.0040], case-level. The logit-adjusted control gains as much, +0.0518, at R@50 = 0.3840
1948   against TDE’s 0.2631, and TDE’s case-level part against it is −0.0027, [−0.0063, +0.0009]. On relations with a matched
1949   pair TDE’s within-cell discrimination falls: the AUC change weighted as the split weights cells is −0.0128, interval
1950   [−0.0171, −0.0074] (−0.0047, [−0.0122, +0.0031], by comparison), and the matched quadratic case-level part is −0.00540,
1951   [−0.00655, −0.00425]. In SGDet, on all 26,446 test images, TDE raises mR@50 by +0.0310, 87% group-level, with a
1952   case-level part of +0.0041, [+0.0013, +0.0070], which the control matches exactly (TDE against the control: −0.0000,
1953   [−0.0030, +0.0030]); the control gains more, +0.0418, at R@50 = 0.3186 against TDE’s 0.1657; neither AUC weighting
1954   detects a change in TDE’s within-cell ranking (−0.0012, [−0.0051, +0.0030], relation-weighted), and the matched quadratic
1955   case-level part is negative, −0.00446, [−0.00560, −0.00332].
