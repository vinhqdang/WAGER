## Journal-Fit Review Report

### Reviewer Identity
Associate Editor, JSPI — background in nonparametric/semiparametric inference, U-statistic theory, and model-comparison methodology; Journal-Fit seat only (bird's-eye scope/originality/significance, not proof-level or citation-completeness audit).

### Overall Recommendation
Major Revision

### Confidence (1-5)
4

### Summary Assessment
WAGER decomposes the score gain between two frozen probabilistic classifiers into an exact prior-transported component and an antisymmetric U-statistic instance-alignment residual, with a full statistical apparatus: finite-sample identity, Hájek influence-function inference, a CLT (`Theorem thm:clt`), a Bregman-family generalization (`Theorem thm:bregman`), an exact attenuation result for the naive in-sample plug-in (`Proposition prop:attenuate`), a coarsening/law-of-total-covariance result (`Proposition prop:coarsen`), a Cauchy–Schwarz sensitivity bound for omitted confounders (`Proposition prop:sensitivity`), and an exact reduction to a clustered Diebold–Mariano/Giacomini–White test at the trivial partition (`Corollary cor:dm`). This is a coherent, genuinely statistical contribution to the comparative-forecast-evaluation and proper-score-decomposition literatures, and the empirical audit of a widely cited debiasing method (`sec:sggaudit`, Table `tab:sggaudit`) is a substantively important finding regardless of discipline. My core concern at the fit/strategy level is not the science but the packaging and proportion: the submission's own front matter (journal macro, cover letter, title page) still addressed a different journal, and roughly two-thirds of the paper's real estate is CV/ML benchmarking detail (VG150 reconstruction, CLIP crop preprocessing, ResNet-32 schedules) that is likely to read as foreign to JSPI's readership relative to the statistical machinery that is this paper's actual selling point to that audience. Both problems are fixable without new experiments.

### Strengths

1. **Exact finite-sample identity with no fitted nuisance model** (`sec:finite`, `Theorem thm:sample`, Eq. `eq:kernel`) — a clean, assumption-light result of exactly the kind JSPI readers value; the U-statistic framing and unbiasedness argument (requiring independence, not mere exchangeability) is stated with appropriate care.
2. **Bregman-score generalization** (`sec:bregman`, `Theorem thm:bregman`, `Corollary cor:bregman`) turns what could have been a "quadratic score, plus a log-score afterthought" paper into one genuine covariance theorem with the quadratic and log identities as named corollaries — this is real added statistical generality, not padding.
3. **Explicit situating relative to Diebold–Mariano/Giacomini–White** (`sec:asymptotic`, `Corollary cor:dm`; also `sec:related-decomposition`) gives JSPI readers an immediate, familiar anchor and states precisely what WAGER adds beyond the classical test (the further $\Delta P/\Delta R$ split) rather than only claiming an analogy.
4. **Sensitivity analysis for an unrecorded confounder** (`Proposition prop:sensitivity`, `Corollary cor:sensitivity-crude`, Table `tab:sensitivity-numeric`) borrows the Rosenbaum/Cinelli-Hazlett/Freidling–Zhao omitted-variable idiom and adapts it cleanly to this estimand — a bridge to a literature JSPI's causal-inference-adjacent readers will recognize.
5. **Attenuation proposition** (`Proposition prop:attenuate`, Eq. `eq:attenuation`) is a small, sharp, non-obvious exact-bias result (the $(n_c-1)/n_c$ shrinkage of the in-sample plug-in) — the kind of self-contained lemma that reads well in a methodology journal independent of the CV application.
6. **Reproducibility and internal verification are unusually strong**: the data/code-availability statement in `main.tex` ("Data and code availability"), the `verify_manuscript_numbers.py` cross-check against quoted values (per `README.md`), and the independent-code-path covariance cross-check in `sec:cifarlt` ("two independent code paths agree to the fifth decimal") are the sort of evidentiary rigor that should reassure any editor about the paper's factual reliability.
7. **The MOTIFS-TDE audit** (`sec:sggaudit`, Table `tab:sggaudit`) is a genuinely significant empirical result — a widely cited debiasing method's 10.2-point mean-recall improvement collapses to a statistically indistinguishable-from-zero instance-alignment change once calibration is matched — and it is reached by applying the estimator to someone else's released checkpoints rather than only to purpose-built toy predictors, which strengthens the paper's claim to real-world relevance.

### Weaknesses

1. **Severity: Critical.** The manuscript's own front matter has not been retargeted to the journal it is now under review at. `manuscript\main.tex` line 32 still reads `\journal{Computational Statistics and Data Analysis}`, and its header comment (lines 1-5) explicitly frames the file as a CSDA submission. `manuscript\cover_letter.tex` opens "To the Editors, *Computational Statistics and Data Analysis*" and argues fit against *CSDA's* own section taxonomy ("Section II, Statistical Methodology for Data Analysis... Section I"). `manuscript\title_page.tex`'s header comment says "CSDA runs single anonymized review, so these details also appear in main.tex." Yet `README.md` and `report/REPORT.md` both state the paper is now under major revision at *JSPI* (Ms. Ref. No. JSPI-D-26-00452) after a CSDA desk rejection. Why it matters: an AE opening this package sees a cover letter and journal macro addressed to a different journal, which is either a serious oversight in resubmission hygiene or evidence the "JSPI revision" (new theory, third domain) was bolted onto old CSDA files without a full pass. Fix: update the `\journal{}` macro, rewrite the cover letter for JSPI's own scope statement and review-type policy, and audit `title_page.tex`/`main.tex` for every remaining CSDA-specific claim before resubmission.

2. **Severity: Major.** The paper's proportion of content skews heavily toward CV/ML implementation detail relative to its statistical core. Sections `sec:data` through `sec:vgvisual` (roughly half of `4experiments.tex`) and most of `app:implementation` in `7appendix.tex` are dense with domain-specific mechanics that read like an ML conference's experiments/supplementary section: VG150 vocabulary reconstruction (`sec:data`), the QuickGELU-vs-plain-GELU CLIP loading pitfall, crop deduplication counts (422,143 unique crops from 659,210), and GPU wall-clock timings ("17 minutes to fetch... about 35 minutes to encode... at ~207/s") in `app:compute`. Why it matters: JSPI's readership is statisticians working on design and inference methodology, not CV/ML practitioners; this level of benchmark-engineering detail is unlikely to be what draws or rewards that reader, and risks the paper being perceived as an ML paper wearing statistical notation rather than a statistics paper that happens to use ML benchmarks as illustration. Fix: move implementation-level detail (CLIP encoding minutiae, GPU timings, crop-deduplication mechanics) to a supplement; lead the exposition with the statistical apparatus (Section 3's theorems and the DM/GW/sensitivity corollaries) and treat the CV material as a single, tighter illustrative case study.

3. **Severity: Major.** The paper has grown very long through four successive rejection-driven revisions rather than a single coherent design pass — per the project's own `report/REPORT.md`, from 17 pages (original) to 25, then to 46 pages, each round adding material keyed to a specific referee/desk-reject complaint (stale references, "insufficient novelty," a vague CSDA rejection, now "add theory depth and a third domain" for JSPI). The result includes a nine-item enumerated contribution list (`1intro.tex`, lines 66-115) and an experiments section organized around five separate research questions across three domains with multiple sensitivity/robustness tables (`tab:sensitivity`, `tab:sensitivity-numeric`, `tab:cifar-robust`, `tab:textlt-tier`, etc.). Why it matters: this length and itemization is unusual for JSPI, where papers are typically 20-30 pages built around 2-4 headline results stated discursively rather than as a numbered list; the sprawl dilutes the core theoretical message and creates avoidable editorial friction ("why does this need 46 pages"). Fix: consolidate the introduction's nine contributions into 3-4 headline results; consider whether the third domain (20-Newsgroups-LT) — which the paper itself flags as lacking the calibration-matched, multi-seed protocol given to CIFAR-100-LT — belongs in this submission at full weight or should be trimmed to a shorter confirmatory note, or held for a follow-up.

4. **Severity: Minor.** The acronym-forcing (WAGER = *Within-cell Antisymmetric Gain Evaluation of Resolution*) and the ML-conference-style inline bolded run-in headers throughout `4experiments.tex` (`\paragraph{Result.}`, `\paragraph{Reading the magnitudes.}`, `\paragraph{Choosing $\phi$.}`) read as conventions imported from a CV/ML venue rather than JSPI's usual register. Not substantive, but worth conforming to house style during the venue-retargeting pass.

5. **Severity: Minor.** The abstract (`0abstract.tex`) is a single dense paragraph of roughly 330 words that names essentially every result in the paper. Why it matters: it reads as a compressed table of contents rather than a scientific summary, and most Elsevier stats-journal abstract limits are well under this length. Fix: cut to the central claim (exact decomposition + inference for a model-pair gain) and one headline empirical finding, letting secondary theoretical results be discovered in the body rather than pre-announced by name.

6. **Severity: Minor.** The keyword list (`main.tex`, lines 52-55) includes "Pattern recognition," a CV/ML-flavored term, while omitting more JSPI-native descriptors such as "sensitivity analysis," "comparative forecast evaluation," or "confounding" that would more accurately route the paper to interested JSPI readers and indexers.

### Journal Fit
Mixed but resolvable. The paper's statistical core — a U-statistic-based exact decomposition, Hájek-projection inference with cluster robustness, an asymptotic normality theorem, a Cauchy–Schwarz sensitivity bound in the Rosenbaum/Cinelli-Hazlett idiom, and an exact reduction to Diebold–Mariano/Giacomini–White — is squarely within JSPI's scope. The difficulty is that this content currently sits underneath, rather than in front of, three full CV/ML case studies narrated with benchmark-specific vocabulary that JSPI's typical reader will not have working familiarity with. A meaningfully rewritten version — leading with the statistical framing, keeping one CV case study as the running illustration, and treating the other two domains as brief confirmatory replications — would be a good and citable fit. The paper as currently proportioned, with its still-CSDA-addressed cover letter and journal macro on top of that CV-heavy structure, reads like a manuscript that has not yet been written *for* JSPI, only resubmitted *to* it.

### Originality
The core mechanism — crossing labels within a declared discrete cell to construct an exact, model-free counterfactual for a model-pair score contrast, rather than fitting and subtracting two single-model resolution decompositions — is a genuine and non-trivial idea, and the paper is honest about what distinguishes it from applying the classical Murphy/DeGroot-Fienberg/Brocker decomposition to each model separately. The building blocks are all classical; the originality is in the synthesis and in the specific new estimand (a model-*pair* gain conditional on a declared nuisance cell) rather than in any single technical device. That is a legitimate, if incremental relative to any one ingredient, contribution — solid rather than groundbreaking.

### Significance
For the narrower community of comparative-forecast-evaluation and proper-score-decomposition methodologists, this is a significant, usable addition. The MOTIFS-TDE audit demonstrates real stakes: a headline result the ML community has cited for years turns out, once calibration is matched, to be almost entirely explained by prior-fitting rather than instance-level improvement — a finding with implications for how benchmark leaderboards should be read, beyond this one paper's authorship. Whether this significance carries fully into JSPI's core readership, versus a comparative-forecast-evaluation or ML-methodology audience specifically, is the open question this review's Journal Fit section addresses.

### Questions for Authors
- Has the manuscript actually been reformatted for JSPI's own submission requirements, given that `cover_letter.tex`, `title_page.tex`, and the `\journal{}` macro in `main.tex` still address *Computational Statistics and Data Analysis*?
- Given four rounds of revision across CVIU, CSDA, and now JSPI, each adding material tailored to that round's specific rejection reason, do the authors have a settled view of which single readership this paper is ultimately written for?
- The paper itself notes that 20-Newsgroups-LT does not yet carry the calibration-matched, multi-seed protocol given to CIFAR-100-LT. Would the authors consider holding this third domain for a follow-up paper, or is a full replication of that protocol planned for this revision?
- Would the authors consider restructuring so that one CV domain serves as the primary illustrative case study while the other two domains are compressed into shorter confirmatory subsections, to better match JSPI's typical length and readership expectations?
